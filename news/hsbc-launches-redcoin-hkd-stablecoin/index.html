HSBC Launches RedCoin, Its First Hong Kong Dollar Stablecoin, for Retail Users

HSBC's RedCoin goes live on its PayMe app, letting users send Hong Kong dollar stablecoins to friends and pay more than 100,000 merchants.

HSBC has brought a bank-issued stablecoin to everyday users in Hong Kong.

What launched

The bank's first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), became available on its PayMe platform on Friday, The Block reported. In the coming days, users will be able to turn on a stablecoin wallet inside the app and use it for person-to-person transfers and for payments at more than 100,000 PayMe merchants.

Maggie Ng, HSBC's chief executive for Hong Kong, said money, assets and information are moving onto digital networks that run around the clock and are becoming more programmable. John O'Neill, the bank's managing director for digital assets and currencies, said more products are planned, including uses for corporate and institutional clients.

How it fits in

HSBC is one of three commercial banks authorized to issue Hong Kong dollar banknotes. Hong Kong's Stablecoins Ordinance took effect in August 2025, creating a licensing system for issuers, and in April 2026 the Hong Kong Monetary Authority granted its first stablecoin licenses to HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial, a venture of Standard Chartered, HKT and Animoca Brands. HSBC also runs a gold token for retail clients and a tokenized deposit service for wholesale payments.

Why it matters for crypto

A major global bank putting a regulated stablecoin in front of retail customers is a meaningful adoption step. It also shows non-dollar stablecoins growing under clear local rules, which could broaden how digital money is used for daily payments in Asia.
