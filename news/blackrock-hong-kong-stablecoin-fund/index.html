BlackRock Opens Hong Kong Money Fund to Stablecoin Subscriptions

BlackRock's first tokenized fund in Asia-Pacific accepts the HKDAP stablecoin from professional investors. Retail buyers pay cash, from HK$10.

BlackRock has launched a tokenized Hong Kong dollar money market fund that professional investors can buy and redeem with a local stablecoin, the South China Morning Post reported Tuesday, citing a company statement. Units start at HK$10, about US$1.28.

The BlackRock HKD Digital Liquidity Fund is the firm's first tokenized fund in Hong Kong and the Asia-Pacific region, according to BlackRock's September announcement of its approval. The fund's product page lists a Nov. 16 inception date and a management fee of 0.31%.

Stablecoin in, fund units out

Professional investors can pay with digital cash, including HKDAP, one of Hong Kong's two licensed stablecoins, the SCMP said. Retail investors can also buy, but only with ordinary Hong Kong dollars, the paper and The Defiant reported.

HKDAP is issued by Anchorpoint Financial, a venture led by Standard Chartered. Standard Chartered is also the fund's custodian, administrator and trustee and handles the tokenization, BlackRock said.

A money market fund parks cash in short-term government bills and bank deposits. This one is a "constant net asset value" fund, which aims to keep each unit at a steady price while paying out the interest. "Tokenized" means ownership records sit on a blockchain, so a buyer holding a stablecoin can settle on-chain without first moving money through a bank.

BlackRock's own fund page warns that the fund "is not a guaranteed investment" and can impose redemption fees or gates under stress. It also flags added technology risks from tokenized units.

BlackRock already runs tokenized money funds in the U.S. and Europe, its September release said. In Hong Kong, HSBC launched a similar service using a stablecoin called RedCoin, the SCMP reported.

In our opinion: The detail worth noting is the split: stablecoin settlement for professionals, plain cash for everyone else. That puts Standard Chartered at the center, on both the stablecoin side and the fund side. The fund's net assets aren't posted yet, so the first published asset figure will show how much stablecoin money actually came in.
