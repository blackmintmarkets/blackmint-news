UK Inflation Rises to 3.6% as Diesel Hits a Record 199.5p a Litre

UK consumer prices rose 3.6% in the year to October, up from 3.5%, as motor fuel costs climbed 33.2%, the ONS said, after this month's Bank of England hike.

UK consumer prices rose 3.6% in the 12 months to October, up from 3.5% in September, the Office for National Statistics said Wednesday. That matches the rate a year earlier, and the ONS said CPI was last higher in September 2025.

Fuel Did Most of the Lifting

Motor fuel prices were 33.2% higher than a year earlier, the biggest annual jump since July 2022, the ONS said. Diesel averaged 199.5 pence a litre in October, the highest since the agency's records began in January 1996. Petrol averaged 174.7 pence, its highest since August 2022.

Restaurants and hotels pulled the other way. Prices there rose 3.4% over the year, down from 3.8% in September, as hotel rates fell.

Core inflation, which strips out energy, food, alcohol and tobacco, was 2.8% and little changed from September. Services inflation held at 3.7%, while goods inflation rose to 3.5% from 3.2%.

How the Number Is Built

The Consumer Prices Index (CPI) tracks the cost of a fixed basket of goods and services that households buy, from bread to bus fares. The headline rate compares October's basket with the same month a year earlier. Core inflation removes the most volatile items to show the slower trend underneath.

What the Bank of England Faces

The reading matched the forecast of economists polled by Reuters, CNBC reported. The Bank of England raised its rate earlier this month for the first time in three years and warned inflation could reach 5% by mid-2027, CNBC said. Traders were pricing about an 80% chance of another hike at the bank's December meeting, according to the same report.

Britain imports more energy than it produces, which leaves it exposed to oil shocks such as the disruption of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, CNBC noted. Oil has eased from wartime highs above $100 a barrel, CNBC said, though it remains elevated.

The ONS publishes November figures on Dec. 16.

In our opinion: The number that matters is the gap between 3.6% headline inflation and 2.8% core: fuel is doing most of the damage while the slower trend holds steady. Drivers and diesel-heavy businesses carry the cost at nearly 2 pounds a litre. The Bank of England's December decision and the Dec. 16 data will show whether the rate hike becomes a pair.
