Coinbase Wins Final Dismissal of TerraUSD Buyer's Lawsuit

A federal judge found Coinbase's own UST page said the token wasn't backed by dollars in a bank account, and barred a fifth try at the proposed class action.

A federal judge in San Francisco has dismissed a proposed class action against Coinbase over sales of TerraUSD, the stablecoin that collapsed in May 2022, and ruled that the plaintiff can't amend it again, according to a Nov. 20 order. Judgment was entered Monday, closing the case, The Block reported.

The suit, led by buyer Larry Pearl, argued that Coinbase failed to make clear that UST relied on an algorithm tied to a second token, LUNA, rather than on dollar reserves. It was his fourth amended complaint.

How UST was supposed to hold $1

Reserve-backed stablecoins hold cash or similar assets behind each token. UST didn't. It kept its price through a swap system: users could burn $1 worth of LUNA to create one UST, or burn UST to get LUNA back, with traders' arbitrage meant to keep the price near $1. When demand dried up in May 2022, that loop broke. Pearl's complaint says UST had dropped to 35 cents by May 9, 2022.

What the judge found

U.S. District Judge Maxine M. Chesney wrote that the same Coinbase web page that called UST a stablecoin also said "UST isn't backed by US dollars in a bank account," and linked to a whitepaper describing its tie to LUNA. She also dismissed claims that UST was an unregistered security, finding the complaint didn't plead facts showing a "common enterprise," one part of the Supreme Court's Howey test for investment contracts.

Several claims were thrown out because they went beyond what an earlier order allowed Pearl to amend, the order says. Bloomberg Law also reported the ruling. Black Mint found no public comment from Pearl's lawyers or Coinbase on the decision.

In our opinion: The lesson in this order is about the fine print. The court treated what Coinbase disclosed on its own listing page as enough, which puts more of the burden on buyers to read how a token works before buying it. Whether Pearl appeals to the Ninth Circuit is the next thing to watch.
