Stablecoin Supply Drops $17.2 Billion in a Month, Most Since 2022

Tether's USDT shrank about $8 billion as holders redeemed tokens for dollars, while bitcoin fell about 26% over the same 30 days, data show.

Stablecoin supply fell $17.2 billion in the 30 days to Nov. 24, a 5.7% drop to $283.2 billion, according to DefiLlama data.

That is the biggest 30-day decline in DefiLlama's records outside May 2022, when TerraUSD lost its peg and about $28.6 billion left in a month. Unchained made the same comparison on Tuesday.

Many traders treat stablecoin supply as "dry powder," or cash waiting to buy crypto, Unchained wrote. A smaller pile means fewer dollars sitting one step from a purchase.

How supply shrinks

A stablecoin is a token designed to stay worth $1. When a holder returns tokens to the issuer for ordinary dollars, called a redemption, the issuer burns them, taking them out of circulation, and supply falls. When someone deposits dollars, new tokens are minted. So a falling total means more money left through the issuer's door than came in.

Tether's USDT led the decline

Tether's USDT, the largest stablecoin, shrank about $8.0 billion over the month to $174.2 billion, DefiLlama's dashboard showed late Tuesday. Circle's USDC fell $2.7 billion to $69.0 billion. Sky's USDS dropped about $3.5 billion to $3.2 billion, more than half its supply.

Not every token shrank. Ethena's USDe added about $310 million and Ripple's RLUSD about $340 million.

By blockchain, Ethereum carried most of the drop: stablecoin supply there fell $13.9 billion, to $129.5 billion, DefiLlama's chain data show.

Bitcoin moved the same way over the period. It traded near $80,000 on Oct. 26 and at $58,933 at 11:53 p.m. ET Tuesday, a drop of about 26%, according to CoinGecko. The data show both fell together. They don't show which moved first.

In our opinion: The number that matters is $174.2 billion, the USDT pool that has carried nearly half of this month's exits. Supply ticked up on Nov. 23 and Nov. 24 before slipping again, so the next weekly reading on DefiLlama will show whether redemptions are still running. Stablecoins are built to sit still at $1; this month, a good share of them sat the market out.
