Australia's Inflation Rises to 4.9% in October as Fuel Jumps 6.3%

Core inflation rose to 3.8%, above the RBA's 2% to 3% target, as electricity costs climbed 25.2% after government rebates ended, the ABS said.

Australia's consumer prices rose 4.9% in the 12 months to October, up from 4.3% in September, the Australian Bureau of Statistics said at 7:30 p.m. ET Tuesday.

Prices rose 0.5% in October alone, above the 0.4% forecast, Reuters reported. The trimmed mean, a core gauge, rose to 3.8% from 3.6%, its fastest pace since 2024.

Why core and headline differ

The Consumer Price Index tracks what households pay for a fixed basket of goods and services. The trimmed mean drops the items with the biggest price moves each month, up or down, so one spike can't swing it. In October the ABS said automotive fuel was left out of the trimmed mean, a main reason it sits more than a point below the headline rate.

Power bills and petrol drove the jump

Housing costs rose 6.4% over the year, led by a 25.2% rise in electricity. "The rise in electricity reflects Commonwealth government rebates, which reduced household electricity costs last year but are no longer in place," ABS head of price statistics Rachael McCririck said in a release.

Transport rose 10.6%. Fuel was up 32.5% over the year and 6.3% in October, on higher world oil prices, the ABS said.

Economists split on the next step

The Reserve Bank of Australia targets inflation of 2% to 3% and has raised rates by 125 basis points this year, Reuters reported. Westpac chief economist Luci Ellis said she expects October to be the peak and sees "nothing to change our view that the RBA is likely finished hiking rates." Krishna Bhimavarapu of State Street Investment Management said persistent inflation "may ultimately require policy to become more restrictive."

After the release, the Australian dollar rose 0.1% to $0.6650, and traders cut the odds of a June rate cut to about 75% from nearly fully priced, Reuters reported. Bitcoin was at $58,933 at 11:53 p.m. ET, down 2.3% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The figure the RBA board will study is 3.8%, not 4.9%. Fuel sat outside the trimmed mean this month, so the core rise came without help from petrol, and that is the reading the two camps are arguing over. The board announces its next decision on Dec. 8, and November's CPI follows on Jan. 6.
