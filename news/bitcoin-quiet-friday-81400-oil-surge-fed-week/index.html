Bitcoin Ends a Quiet Friday Near $81,400 as Oil Surge Keeps Traders Cautious

Bitcoin spent Friday in a tight range around $81,000 to $82,000, still below the average cost basis of U.S. spot ETF buyers, as oil climbed and the Fed meeting neared.

Bitcoin closed out the U.S. trading week with one of its calmest sessions in days, holding in a narrow band while oil prices and next week's Federal Reserve meeting dominated the macro picture.

Where prices stand

The CME CF Bitcoin Real-Time Index stood near $81,330 late Friday afternoon, roughly flat on the day, according to Investing.com. Its range for the session ran from about $80,990 to $81,950. Hourly data from StatMuse showed a brief push above $82,000 in the morning that faded before midday, with prices near $81,400 by late afternoon. Ether traded around $2,400 late in the day, according to The Block's price ticker.

The quiet followed a week of swings of about $3,000, CoinDesk reported. Bitcoin is still trading below about $82,300, which Glassnode data cited by CoinDesk puts as the average cost basis of U.S. spot bitcoin ETF holders. That level can act as a hurdle when many fund investors are near break-even.

What is weighing on the market

Brent crude settled at $116.72 a barrel, its highest close since April, CNBC reported, as fears of renewed conflict involving Iran grew. Higher energy costs feed inflation concerns at a time when the Fed has already raised rates once this year. Policymakers meet Oct. 27-28, and a hawkish message could pressure risk assets, including crypto.

Why it matters

A steady price after a volatile week suggests traders are waiting for clearer signals. ETF flows, oil and the Fed's decision are likely to set the tone for bitcoin's next move.
