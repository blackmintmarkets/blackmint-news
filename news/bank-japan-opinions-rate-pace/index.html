Bank of Japan Members Split on How Fast to Lift Rates From 1.25%

One opinion puts Japan's neutral rate considerably above 1%. Another warns the next hike could weigh on the economy. The next meeting is Dec. 17-18.

Bank of Japan policymakers disagreed over how quickly to raise the 1.25% policy rate, a summary of opinions from the Oct. 29-30 meeting shows. It came out at 8:50 a.m. Tokyo time Tuesday, or 6:50 p.m. ET Monday.

The board held the rate at that October meeting in an 8-1 vote. Member Hajime Takata dissented and proposed 1.5%, the policy statement shows.

Some want to reach "neutral" soon

The neutral rate is the level that neither speeds up nor slows down an economy. One opinion said the bank needs "to step up the pace" of getting there. Another said it must reach neutral "soon," and a third called for doing so "at an early stage." One opinion put Japan's neutral rate "considerably above 1 percent."

Others want to wait

Two opinions backed holding in October to study risks and "the cumulative effects of past rate hikes." Another warned that a future hike could "weigh on the economy and push down underlying CPI inflation."

How the document works: each board member and government representative writes up their own remarks under a word limit, and Governor Kazuo Ueda edits them. No names are attached, so the summary doesn't show how many members hold each view, as EconoTimes noted.

On prices, one opinion projected that consumer prices excluding fresh food will rise more than 3% from a year earlier toward the end of the fiscal year. Four opinions touched on wages, and one said the outcome of next spring's wage negotiations "warrants attention."

A higher BOJ rate also makes it more expensive to borrow in yen, including for investors outside Japan. Bitcoin was near $74,750 at midnight ET Tuesday, up 0.2% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

The next policy meeting is Dec. 17-18, on the BOJ's meeting calendar.

In our opinion: the board's argument has moved from whether to raise rates to how fast, and October already had one vote for 1.5%. Full minutes of the October meeting arrive Dec. 23, after the December decision. Until then, the wage data is what one member said "warrants attention."
