BOJ's Tamura Says Half-Point Rate Hike May Become Necessary

The Bank of Japan board member put the neutral rate near 2%, against today's 1.25%, ahead of a Dec. 18 decision markets expect to bring a hike.

Bank of Japan board member Naoki Tamura said Thursday the central bank should be ready to speed up rate increases, and that a half-point hike "may become necessary" if inflation risks build. The policy rate now stands at 1.25%.

In a speech in Kofu, Tamura said that because "financial conditions remain accommodative," the bank "needs to bring the policy interest rate to the neutral level soon." He put that neutral level at most likely around 2%, a view he first gave in June.

What "neutral" means here

A neutral rate is the level that neither speeds up nor slows the economy. Tamura's estimate leaves about 0.75 percentage point between today's rate and that level. He said Japan's underlying inflation is less firmly anchored at the bank's 2% target, so the bank must respond quickly to signs prices are running above it.

At a news conference after the speech, Tamura said his base case was a quarter-point hike every few months, Reuters reported. A 50-basis-point move could follow if upside price risks persist, he said, according to Reuters and Kyodo. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point.

He cited oil costs tied to the Middle East conflict, a weaker yen and strong AI-related demand as sources of price pressure.

December meeting in view

The BOJ raised its rate to 1.25% from 1% in September, after a hike in June, and held in October. Its next meeting ends Dec. 18, and markets widely expect a quarter-point increase to 1.5%, Reuters and Kyodo reported. Tamura is one of nine board members, and Reuters said markets count him among the board's hawks, meaning members who favor tighter policy. Asked about recent market swings, he said there were about three weeks of data to review before the meeting.

Higher Japanese rates raise the cost of borrowing in yen, which matters to any investor who funds positions abroad with yen loans. Bitcoin traded near $60,460 at 5:53 a.m. ET, up 2.7% in 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The detail worth chasing is the half-point option. Tamura's own base case is quarter-point moves every few months, so a 50-basis-point step would double that pace and land hardest on anyone funding trades with yen loans. He is one vote of nine, and Dec. 18 is when the full board shows its hand.
