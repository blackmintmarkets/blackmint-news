Ukraine Passes Crypto Law Setting 23% Tax on Investor Gains

Lawmakers expect 15 billion hryvnias, about $331 million, in new revenue. Rules start July 1, 2027, and the central bank will oversee stablecoins.

Ukraine's parliament passed a law on Wednesday that legalizes the country's crypto market and taxes individuals' gains at 23%, with 271 lawmakers voting in favor, Ukrinform reported.

Yaroslav Zhelezniak, first deputy chair of the Verkhovna Rada's finance, tax and customs committee, said the law should add 15 billion hryvnias to budget revenue. That is about $331 million at the National Bank of Ukraine's official rate. The rules take effect July 1, 2027.

How the tax works

Individuals will pay 18% personal income tax plus a 5% military levy on crypto profits, Ukrinform reported. Swapping one crypto asset for another is not taxed, and losses can be carried forward to later years.

Coins bought before the law takes effect get a transition rate when holders cash out to hryvnias or other traditional money in 2027 and 2028: 5% plus the 5% levy. Zhelezniak wrote on Telegram that this lower rate applies to the full amount cashed out, not only the gain. After 2028, the standard 18% plus 5% applies.

In plain terms, a holder who cashes out 100,000 hryvnias during the transition owes 10,000 hryvnias, whatever the original cost. Under the standard rate, the same holder owes 23% of the profit only.

Central bank gets stablecoins

Oversight will be split between the National Securities and Stock Market Commission and the National Bank of Ukraine, according to Ukrinform and Decrypt. The central bank will handle stablecoins, tokens pegged to a currency such as the dollar, and can allow or ban their circulation.

The bill, No. 10225-d, was recommended for final passage by the finance committee on Nov. 4, Ukrinform reported. Zhelezniak said it took three years of working groups. The law still needs the president's signature, Decrypt reported.

In our opinion: The question worth chasing is who pays more under the transition rate. Ten percent of the full amount costs less than 23% of the profit only when the gain is about 43% or more of the cash-out, so the lower rate is lower only for holders with large gains. The next step to watch is the president's signature, then July 1, 2027.
