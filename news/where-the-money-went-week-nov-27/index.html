S&P 500 Gains 5.6% in Holiday Week as Bitcoin ETF Outflows Continue

Stocks had their best week since April 2025, yet investors pulled $22.7 billion from global stock funds and $1.25 billion from bitcoin ETFs.

Stocks had their best week in more than a year, but the measured money mostly moved the other way. The S&P 500 closed Friday's shortened session at 7,297.49, up 5.6% from the Nov. 20 close and its biggest weekly gain since April 2025, Reuters and the AP reported. In the week to Nov. 25, investors pulled a net $22.73 billion from global equity funds, the most in 14 months, LSEG Lipper data show.

This is the weekly follow-up to our November "Where the Money Went" deep dive and the midweek note on Monday's Nvidia rally. U.S. markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and stocks closed at 1 p.m. ET Friday. Several flow reports for the final days of the week were not out by publication time, and we say so below where it matters.

All 11 Select Sector SPDR funds rose, led by materials, technology and communication services, Yahoo Finance data show. In Morningstar's sector indexes, basic materials and communication services led, up 7.74% and 7.43%.

How it works: prices and flows are different things

A price move does not, by itself, send dollars in or out. Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so the cash only changes hands. When prices fall, holders lose value on paper and that value is simply gone; when prices rise, it comes back the same way. Flows are different. They count dollars that actually moved into or out of a fund, a stablecoin or a share sale. This week shows why both matter: prices rose almost everywhere, while several of the flow reports showed money leaving.

Stocks: the bounce and the exits

The week opened with Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive, which lifted the stock 31% on Monday and gave the S&P 500 and Nasdaq their best day since April 2025, CNBC reported. Tech gave back part of that on Tuesday and Wednesday before rising again Friday. The Nasdaq gained 6.68% for the week and the Dow 5.46%, Reuters reported, matching Yahoo Finance data.

The fund flow reports out so far show investors selling. Global equity funds lost a net $22.73 billion in the week to Nov. 25, the most since September 2025, and U.S. equity funds lost $30.57 billion, the most since Sept. 9, LSEG Lipper data show, per Reuters and PiQ Markets. Technology funds lost $7.57 billion. European equity funds took in $7.29 billion. That Lipper week runs Thursday to Wednesday, so it includes the Nov. 19-20 selloff and not Friday's gains.

No U.S. IPOs priced during the holiday week, Renaissance Capital reported. Anthropic, which raised $93.5 billion in its IPO the week before, closed below its $83 offer price on Tuesday and Wednesday, then finished Friday at $91.88, Yahoo Finance data show.

Bonds and the dollar

Treasuries ended the week slightly lower in price. The 10-year yield rose to 4.94% from 4.92%, and the 2-year yield rose to 4.69% from 4.63%, Treasury Department data show. TreasuryBonds.com put the 10-year at 4.93% and said the 2-year rose the most. When yields rise, bond prices fall.

Bond fund buyers still showed up. Government bond funds took in a net $7.14 billion in the week to Nov. 25, while global bond funds as a group lost $3.51 billion, LSEG Lipper data show, per Reuters.

The dollar index rose 0.4% Friday to 104.05, up 0.2% for the week, TreasuryBonds.com reported, in line with Yahoo Finance's 104.01 late Friday. It touched a 22-month high of 104.53 earlier in the week, Reuters reported. Fed funds futures priced about a 99% chance of a December rate hike on Friday, Reuters reported.

Gold and oil

Comex December gold settled at $3,639.60, up 3.53% for the week, its biggest weekly gain since the week ended Nov. 6, Dow Jones Market Data show. The December contract had settled at its 2026 low of $3,512.30 on Nov. 20, the Dow Jones data show. Investors still pulled $2.14 billion from gold and precious metals funds in the week to Nov. 25, the most since March, LSEG Lipper data show.

U.S. crude settled at $83.08 a barrel on Friday, down about 1% from $83.98 on Nov. 20, per CNBC and Yahoo Finance data. Brent settled at $95.44 and gained 3% for the week, CNBC reported, after attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy sites.

Cash: flat at ICI, outflows at Lipper

U.S. money market fund assets rose $2.49 billion to $7.979 trillion in the six days to Tuesday, Nov. 24, the Investment Company Institute reported, and Crane Data described the total as flat. Government funds added $5.01 billion and prime funds lost $2.25 billion. That is still just below the record $8.016 trillion set in early November.

LSEG Lipper's broader fund count showed money market funds losing a net $49.42 billion in the week to Nov. 25, their third straight weekly outflow, Reuters reported. The two reports cover different sets of funds and different days, so they do not line up one to one.

What left crypto

  • Spot bitcoin ETFs: $1.25 billion out from Monday to Wednesday, Farside Investors data show, matching SoSoValue's count as compiled by Pre-Tick. That made seven straight days of outflows since Nov. 17, totaling $3.05 billion. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for about $1.01 billion of this week's total.
  • Spot ether ETFs: $217.5 million out over the same three days, per Farside, an eighth straight day of outflows on Wednesday, Bloomingbit reported.
  • Friday's ETF flows had not been posted by publication time.
  • Stablecoins: USD-pegged supply, measured at 7 p.m. ET on DefiLlama, slipped from $282.50 billion on Nov. 20 to $281.93 billion on Thursday, down $0.58 billion. Tether's USDT shrank about $1.3 billion over seven days, while Circle's USDC grew about $0.4 billion. Stablecoin Beat put the total at $282.8 billion on Friday.

The stablecoin drop this week was small next to the $17.2 billion decline in the month to Nov. 24.

What to watch

The November jobs report comes Friday, Dec. 4, ahead of the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting, TreasuryBonds.com noted. Friday's ETF flows are still to come from Farside, and ICI normally publishes its weekly money fund report on Wednesdays.

In our opinion: prices and flows told different stories this week. Stocks and crypto rose, but the flow reports that are complete so far show stock funds, bitcoin ETFs and stablecoins losing money, with government bond funds one of the few groups taking money in. Friday's flow data will show whether any of the rally's buyers came in through these funds, and next week's jobs report tests the December hike bets that have kept the dollar firm.
