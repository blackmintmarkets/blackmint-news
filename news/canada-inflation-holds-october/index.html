Canada's Inflation Holds at 3.1% as Gasoline Prices Run 30.9% Higher

Economists polled by Reuters expected 3.2%. The Bank of Canada's preferred core gauges stayed near 2% ahead of its Dec. 9 rate decision.

Canada's annual inflation rate held at 3.1% in October, the same as September, Statistics Canada said Monday. Economists polled by Reuters had expected 3.2%. Prices rose 0.2% from September, against a forecast of 0.3%.

The reading lands three weeks before the Bank of Canada's Dec. 9 decision. The bank raised its overnight rate a quarter point to 2.50% on Oct. 28. After the release, traders priced roughly even odds of another hike in December, the Globe and Mail reported, citing swaps data from Bloomberg.

Gasoline did most of the lifting

Gasoline cost 30.9% more than a year earlier, up from 27.8% in September. StatCan said part of that is a base-year effect: gasoline fell 4.8% in October 2025, and that drop no longer counts in the 12-month figure. From September to October, gasoline fell 2.5%. Excluding energy, prices rose 2.0%, down from 2.1%.

The Bank of Canada aims for 2% inflation inside a 1% to 3% band, and it leans on "core" measures that strip out the most volatile prices. Its preferred gauges, CPI-trim and CPI-median, came in at 1.8% and 2.0%. So headline inflation sits above the band while the underlying measures sit near the target.

Banks split on December

CIBC senior economist Andrew Grantham said the report backs CIBC's call for a hold, Canadian Mortgage Professional reported. RBC's Abbey Xu kept a base case of a quarter-point hike to 2.75%.

Rate moves reach crypto through yields and the dollar. Higher rates raise the return on cash and bonds, which compete with assets like bitcoin that pay no interest. Bitcoin was near $64,550 at 11:45 a.m. ET, down 0.2% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

The Bank of Canada announces its next decision on Dec. 9.
