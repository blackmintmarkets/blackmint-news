Bitcoin Climbs Back to $60,000 After Dropping Near $55,700 Overnight

Bitcoin rose about 8.6% from its 1 a.m. ET low, while U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $124.5 million on Friday and about $1.25 billion for the week.

Bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 on Saturday morning, about 8.6% above an overnight low near $55,711, CoinGecko hourly data show. It traded at $60,032 at 11:49 a.m. ET, up 1% from the same time Friday.

The low came around 1 a.m. ET. By 9 a.m. the price had reached $60,512, the top of the rebound on CoinGecko's hourly series. Kraken's own BTC/USD market traced a similar path: a low of $55,100 in the 1 a.m. hour and a high of $60,883 in the 9 a.m. hour, according to the exchange's public price data.

ETF investors pulled $1.25 billion this week

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had net outflows of $124.5 million on Friday, Farside Investors data show. That number is still missing BlackRock's IBIT, the biggest fund, which Farside hadn't filled in by midday Saturday. With Friday's partial figure, the funds lost about $1.25 billion over the week. Monday was the only day with a net inflow, at $7.9 million.

How the flow numbers work: when investors cash out of an ETF, the fund redeems shares and its bitcoin holdings shrink. A net outflow means more money left the funds that day than came in. Source: Farside Investors.

Sentiment is still near the bottom of the scale

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index read 12 on Saturday, inside its "Extreme Fear" band. It was 10 on Thursday. Alternative.me builds the 0-to-100 score from inputs that include price volatility, trading volume and social media activity.

In our opinion: An 8.6% bounce on a Saturday morning looks good on a chart. The number worth chasing is Farside's next update, which fills in IBIT's Friday figure and shows whether any of the $1.25 billion that left the funds this week is walking back in. Weekend rallies don't need ETF buyers. Monday's do.
