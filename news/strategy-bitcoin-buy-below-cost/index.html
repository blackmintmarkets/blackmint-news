Strategy Buys 485 Bitcoin as Its Stack Sits $9 Billion Below Cost

The 485 coins cost $33.4 million. Strategy also sold $244.7 million of stock, and most of that cash went to buying back its STRC preferred shares.

Strategy bought 485 bitcoin for $33.4 million last week, its 8-K filing shows, while its full 850,849-coin holding sat about $9.3 billion below what the company paid for it.

The company's average cost is $75,452 per coin, or $64.20 billion in total, as of Nov. 15. With bitcoin near $64,550 at 11:45 a.m. ET Monday, per CoinGecko, the stack was worth about $54.9 billion. That gap is on paper. It becomes a realized loss only if Strategy sells.

Most of the new cash went to preferred stock

Strategy sold 2,087,040 shares of its MSTR common stock between Nov. 9 and Nov. 15 through an at-the-market program, raising $244.7 million after commissions. Only $33.4 million of it bought bitcoin, at an average of $68,887 each. Another $206.3 million went to repurchasing its STRC preferred stock, and $5 million went to cash, the filing says.

An at-the-market program lets a company sell new shares into the market a little at a time, at current prices, instead of in one large offering. STRC is Strategy's variable-rate perpetual preferred stock: it pays holders a dividend and has no maturity date. Buying it back cuts the number of those shares outstanding.

Bitcoin's slide sets the backdrop

Bitcoin fell as low as about $62,000 late Saturday, its lowest since August, per Coin Metrics data cited by CNBC. CoinGecko's hourly data puts the Saturday low near $62,600 at 9 p.m. ET. CNBC tied the drop to forced selling of leveraged bets and a stronger dollar. Bitcoin is down about 14% from $75,270 a week earlier, per CoinGecko.

Strategy wasn't the only treasury company buying. Bitmine Immersion said Monday it added 7,653 ether last week and now holds 6,077,745 ETH.

In our opinion: the share sale tells the real story. Of every dollar Strategy raised last week, roughly 14 cents went to bitcoin and about 84 cents went to buying back its own preferred stock. Next Monday's 8-K will show whether that mix holds while the stack trades below cost.
