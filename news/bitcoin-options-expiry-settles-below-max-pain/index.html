Bitcoin Options Worth $6.8 Billion Settle Below Max Pain

Deribit's monthly bitcoin options settled at $61,600 at 3 a.m. ET, about $8,400 below the $70,000 level where buyers would collect the least.

Bitcoin options worth about $6.8 billion on Deribit settled at 3 a.m. ET Friday at a bitcoin index price of $61,600.17, according to the exchange's public data. That was $8,400 below the $70,000 "max pain" level that analytics firms CryptoQuant and Castle Labs calculated from open positions before the expiry.

How a Monthly Expiry Works

A bitcoin option is a contract that gives its buyer the right to buy (a call) or sell (a put) bitcoin at a set price, called the strike, until a set date. Deribit's bitcoin options are cash-settled, paid in bitcoin, against its index price at 08:00 UTC on expiry day. Contracts whose strike is on the wrong side of that price expire worthless.

"Max pain" is the strike at which the open calls and puts together would pay their holders the least. Both firms describe it as a gauge of where positions sit, not a forecast.

The Positions Going Into Friday

Castle Labs counted 112,794 BTC of open interest in the Nov. 27 contract, worth $6.83 billion at the index, out of about 443,700 BTC open across all Deribit bitcoin expiries. CryptoQuant put the expiry at 25.4% of all bitcoin options open interest. CryptoQuant and Castle Labs each put the put-to-call ratio at 0.83, meaning there were 83 bitcoin of puts open for every 100 of calls.

CryptoQuant tied the gap to the speed of the drop. It noted that bitcoin traded above $75,000 for most of October, and that much of the open interest still sat at strikes from that range. By CryptoQuant's count, the expiry's largest single strike, $65,000, held 5,428 BTC, mostly puts, which finished in the money (worth exercising) with the index below that level.

Price After Settlement

Bitcoin traded at $61,561 at 5:48 a.m. ET, up 1.8% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko. Deribit's daily settlement price was $60,512.14 a day earlier and $58,754.83 on Wednesday.

In our opinion: For a level called "max pain," $70,000 stayed comfortably out of reach, and the index settled $8,400 below it. The stake was real money for holders of puts struck above $61,600, which finished in the money, and for the sellers on the other side who paid them. The number to watch next is the Dec. 25 quarterly expiry, which Castle Labs lists as the largest single expiry on Deribit's board.
