Fed's Cook Doubts September's Rate Hike Is Enough to Slow Inflation

Fed Governor Lisa Cook explained her October dissent in a speech in Chile, saying inflation has topped the 2% target for 67 months and policy is too easy.

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said Friday she is "skeptical that September's single 1/4 point rate increase will be enough to change the path of inflation," in a speech in Santiago, Chile.

Cook spoke at the Central Bank of Chile's annual conference. She said inflation has been above the Fed's 2% target for 67 months. She cited estimates that the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index rose 3.8% in the 12 months to October, and 3.2% excluding food and energy.

Why she voted for a hike in October

The Fed raised its target range for the federal funds rate to 3.75% to 4% in September, then held it there at its October meeting. Cook was one of two officials who dissented from the hold, Reuters reported. She said she preferred another quarter-point increase.

She gave two reasons. The first was that financial conditions are "too accommodative." The second was urgency: "Waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve," she said, adding that larger increases might be needed later.

What "accommodative" means

The federal funds rate is the rate banks charge each other to lend reserves overnight, the Fed says. Calling conditions accommodative means borrowing and investing are still easy enough that, in Cook's view, the current rate will add to inflation pressure rather than cool it. Cook said current policy "does not appear to be curtailing the AI buildout," and that rising stock prices keep supporting consumer spending.

She also described the economy as "strong—and accelerating," with third-quarter growth at a 3.4% annual pace and unemployment at 4.1% in October. She said the views were her own and not necessarily those of her colleagues. Financial markets broadly expect a hike at the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting, Reuters reported.

Bitcoin traded at $56,397 at 11:53 p.m. ET Friday, down 8.2% in 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: Cook is telling markets that one December hike may not be the end of it. That matters for anyone holding assets that pay no interest, bitcoin included, because each hike raises what cash and Treasury bills pay instead. The Dec. 8-9 vote, and how many officials join her case for more, is the next thing to watch.
