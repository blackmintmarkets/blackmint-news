Ethereum Developers Set Dec. 15 Hoodi Test for Glamsterdam Upgrade

Core developers kept the contested EIP-8037 storage-fee change as written and set the last public test for Dec. 15, seven weeks after the original date.

Ethereum's core developers on Thursday set Dec. 15 at 2:12 p.m. ET as the date the Glamsterdam upgrade goes live on the Hoodi test network, after teams agreed to keep a disputed storage-fee rule unchanged. The test had first been planned for late October, according to EtherWorld's summary of the All Core Developers Consensus call No. 192, listed on the project's GitHub.

The Rule Nobody Reopened

The fight was over EIP-8037, which raises the gas cost of creating new "state," the permanent data every Ethereum node has to keep. Gas is the unit Ethereum uses to price computing work. Under the proposal (as written on GitHub), each new byte of state costs 1,530 gas and a new account counts as 120 bytes, which works out to 183,600 gas for that part alone.

The authors say the goal is to stop the database from growing too fast as blocks get bigger. The EIP says a Geth node's state was about 390 gigabytes in January and could pass 650 gigabytes, the level where nodes start to slow down, in under a year at a 200 million gas limit.

The catch is older smart contracts. Programs written around today's prices can run out of gas partway through a transaction. No alternative won enough support, so teams kept the specification as written, EtherWorld and CoinDesk reported.

No Mainnet Date Yet

Developers have not set a date for the main network. The Sepolia test network already upgraded in October.

The same call reopened a debate over FOCIL, a feature planned for the following upgrade, Hegota, meant to stop block builders from leaving out valid transactions. Erigon, Geth, Prysm and other teams opposed keeping it in Hegota, EtherWorld reported, and Ethlabs researcher Caspar Schwarz-Schilling argued the engineering time is needed for quantum-resistant cryptography. A decision is expected on the next consensus call around Dec. 10, EtherWorld said.

Ether traded near $1,734 at 11:50 p.m. ET Thursday, up 1.9% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: Seven weeks late, Glamsterdam now has a dress rehearsal on the calendar, and the cast was told the script will not change. The people carrying the risk are teams running older contracts that assume cheap storage, since they have until Dec. 15 to check their code. Watch the Hoodi fork at 2:12 p.m. ET that day, then whether developers pick a mainnet date.
