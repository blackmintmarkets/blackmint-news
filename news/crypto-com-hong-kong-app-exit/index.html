Crypto.com to Close Its Hong Kong Consumer App on Feb. 1

Retail users get about three months to withdraw. Crypto.com's Hong Kong unit is still seeking an SFC license for its institutional exchange.

Crypto.com will stop offering its consumer app to users in Hong Kong on Feb. 1, 2027, the company said Monday, Bloomberg reported. That leaves retail customers about three months to close accounts.

The company framed it as a business call. "This decision reflects our ongoing efforts to optimize global operations and allocate resources where we see the most sustainable long-term opportunities," a spokesperson said, according to Bloomberg. Customers have been notified and will get instructions for closing accounts and withdrawing funds, the spokesperson said.

Not a full exit

Crypto.com is keeping a foot in the city. Its unit Foris DAX HK Limited is still seeking a virtual asset trading platform license from the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), Hong Kong's markets regulator, for its institutional Crypto.com Exchange, The Straits Times reported, carrying the Bloomberg story. The app closure does not affect that application, Investing.com reported.

How Hong Kong's licensing works

Hong Kong requires any platform that trades crypto for customers in the city to hold an SFC license. Licensed venues face rules on custody, which tokens retail users can buy, and how client money is kept apart from company money. Serving everyday traders under those rules costs more than serving a smaller set of professional clients.

The reports did not include comment from the SFC.

In our opinion: A global exchange giving up a city's retail users while still chasing that city's license tells you where the money is. Hong Kong has courted crypto firms for years, so the stake is whether its rules leave room for retail apps at scale. Watch the SFC's public license register for a decision on Foris DAX HK.

For users, the practical step is simple. Move funds before the Feb. 1 cutoff, following the company's instructions.
