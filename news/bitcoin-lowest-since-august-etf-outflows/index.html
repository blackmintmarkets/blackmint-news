Bitcoin Falls to Lowest Since August After $1.07 Billion ETF Exit

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost money in all five sessions last week, led by BlackRock's IBIT at $908 million. A fear gauge fell to 13 of 100.

Bitcoin fell to about $65,380 at 11:58 a.m. ET on Saturday, down 4.7% in 24 hours, per CoinGecko. That is its lowest daily reading in CoinGecko's data since Aug. 19, when it closed near $64,690.

The slide extends a rough week. Bitcoin sat near $75,780 on Nov. 8, so Saturday's price is about 13.7% lower, and its market value is down to roughly $1.31 trillion.

Five straight days of ETF outflows

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs posted net outflows in every session from Nov. 9 through Nov. 13, about $1.07 billion in total, according to Farside Investors data:

  • Monday: $201.0 million out
  • Tuesday: $95.0 million
  • Wednesday: $299.8 million
  • Thursday: $284.1 million
  • Friday: $185.4 million

BlackRock's IBIT accounted for about $908 million of that. Fidelity's FBTC lost $41.9 million on Friday alone. (Source: Farside Investors.)

How a redemption reaches the market

A spot bitcoin ETF holds actual bitcoin. When more shares are cashed in than created, the fund's authorized participants (the big trading firms that swap shares with the fund) hand shares back and the fund releases bitcoin or sells it for cash. So a week of outflows means less bitcoin held for brokerage-account investors. It is one of the few daily, public measures of that demand. Weekend prices move with no ETF trading at all, because the funds only trade Monday to Friday.

Sentiment followed the price. Alternative.me's Crypto Fear & Greed Index, a 0-100 gauge where lower means more fear, read 13 on Saturday, down from 20 a day earlier. Ether fared worse, falling 5.3% to about $1,826, per CoinGecko.

No single headline explains Saturday's leg down.

In our opinion: the number to track is Monday's ETF tally. Five red sessions took about $1.07 billion out, yet Farside still shows the funds roughly $54.7 billion ahead in net inflows since launch; last week's exits equal about 2% of that total. Bitcoin doesn't keep banker's hours, though, and Saturday's drop came with every one of those funds closed.
