Oil Falls More Than 8% as Traders Bet on an Exit From the Iran War

Brent settled at $94.62 and WTI at $88.12 Wednesday, both down more than 8%, as traders bet the midterm results could speed an end to the Iran war.

Brent crude settled at $94.62 a barrel Wednesday, down 8.7%, and U.S. West Texas Intermediate fell 8.3% to $88.12, Reuters reported. Both closed at their lowest since Aug. 31.

Oil futures have fallen more than 14% this week, CNBC reported, after gaining more than 10% in October on fears of a bigger conflict.

Why traders sold

Democrats won control of the House and the Senate in Tuesday's midterms. Traders bet the new balance could hasten an end to the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran, which has disrupted energy supplies for eight months. President Donald Trump told the Financial Times on Tuesday that the U.S. had no immediate plans to strike Iran. He also said gasoline prices hurt his party with voters. Vice President JD Vance said on social media that the war was the reason Republicans lost.

Not everyone reads it that way. "I'm more concerned that we can see an escalation in Iran sooner than a resolution," said Carl Larry of Enverus, per Reuters. Rapidan Energy's Bob McNally told CNBC the odds still favor military escalation. Iran's supreme leader said U.S. hostility would continue whichever party holds power.

Here is how a war moves oil. Prices carry a risk premium, an extra amount traders pay for the chance that supply gets cut. Gulf exports averaged 17.1 million barrels a day in October, roughly three-quarters of pre-war levels, Reuters reported, citing Vortexa. When a deal looks likelier, that premium shrinks fast.

Pump prices lag. The national gasoline average was $4.12 Wednesday morning, NBC News reported.

In our opinion: An 8% drop in a day prices in a lot of peace that hasn't been signed yet. Analysts on both sides have a point, and the gap between them is where the next swing comes from. Watch diesel and gasoline over the next few weeks, because that is where any relief would show up for households.

For crypto, cheaper oil can cool inflation, which shapes the path for interest rates that also moves bitcoin.
