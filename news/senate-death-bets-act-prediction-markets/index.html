Senate Passes DEATH BETS Act to Ban War and Death Prediction Bets

The bill, passed by voice vote Thursday, would bar CFTC-registered exchanges from listing contracts tied to war, terrorism or a death. It now goes to the House.

The U.S. Senate on Thursday passed S. 4035, the DEATH BETS Act, a bill that would ban CFTC-registered exchanges from listing prediction market contracts tied to war, terrorism, assassination or a person's death. It passed by voice vote at 6:47 p.m. ET, according to the Senate floor log.

The bill now goes to the House. It is not law until the House passes it and the president signs it.

What the bill would do

Under the Senate-passed text, a registered exchange could not list or clear a contract that "expressly references" terrorism, assassination, war or U.S. military operations, RG reported from the passed version. It also could not list a contract expected to settle mainly on someone's death. The version adds U.S. military operations to the bill Sen. Adam Schiff, D-Calif., introduced in March.

A Schiff-Curtis amendment, named for Schiff and Sen. John Curtis, R-Utah, adds a rule of construction. It says the bill does not change existing law for other event contracts, including those tied to gaming, according to DeFi Rate and RG.

How these markets work today

A prediction market lets people trade contracts that pay out if an event happens, such as a rate cut or an election result. In the U.S., the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) oversees such exchanges. Under the Commodity Exchange Act, the CFTC can already block war, terrorism and assassination contracts it finds contrary to the public interest, but that call is at its discretion, Schiff's office said in March. The bill would write the ban into law.

"There is no justification for gambling on lives," Schiff said in that March statement.

What comes next

A House companion bill, H.R. 7942, has sat in a House Agriculture subcommittee since April, DeFi Rate reported. The current Congress ends on Jan. 3, 2027. Black Mint did not find public responses to the vote from prediction market operators by 6 a.m. ET Friday.

In our opinion: the question worth chasing is whether the House moves before Jan. 3, because a bill that stalls there starts over in the new Congress. For exchanges, the stake is a narrow but hard line in law on which contracts they can list. The CFTC would lose some of the case-by-case discretion it has today.
