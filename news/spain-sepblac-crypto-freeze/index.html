Spain Decree Lets Sepblac Freeze Crypto Accounts for Five Days

The anti-money laundering unit can act on its own suspicion from Jan. 25, 2027, and hold transactions for up to 10 business days before a judge must step in.

Spain's financial intelligence unit will be able to freeze crypto accounts for up to five business days without a court order, under a decree published in the official gazette on Nov. 25.

The change takes effect Jan. 25, 2027. Crypto firms serving Spanish customers will then have to follow suspension orders sent directly by the unit. Firms that fail to comply commit an offense under Spain's anti-money laundering law, while those that comply are shielded from liability, the decree says.

Five days for accounts, 10 for transactions

Royal Decree-law 44/2026, a consumer-credit measure, adds Article 46 bis to Spain's 2010 anti-money laundering law. The unit, Sepblac, can suspend a crypto, bank or payment account, or a business relationship, for up to five business days when it sees signs of money laundering or terrorist financing. A transaction in progress can be held for up to 10 business days.

When the suspicion comes from a firm's suspicious-activity report, Sepblac must issue the order within three business days. Any hold ends at the deadline unless a judge ratifies or extends it at the request of prosecutors, according to the decree and Decrypt.

How a freeze order works

A financial intelligence unit collects reports of suspicious payments from banks and other firms and decides which ones to pass to police or prosecutors. A suspension does not seize the money. It pauses it so analysts can check whether the suspicion holds before the funds move again.

Until now, Sepblac could use this power only at the request of a foreign intelligence unit, the decree's preamble says, a point CoinDesk also reported. The government said the change follows standards from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), the global anti-money laundering standard setter, and transposes Article 24 of EU Directive 2024/1640. The preamble calls the current limit a deficiency that FATF will assess.

As a royal decree-law, the measure must still be validated by Spain's parliament within 30 days, according to Decrypt and CoinDesk.

In our opinion: The shift is from acting only on foreign requests to acting on Sepblac's own analysis, and it covers bank and payment accounts as well as crypto. Customers of crypto providers operating in Spain carry the new risk of a five-day pause, while judges keep the final say on anything longer. The parliamentary validation vote, due within 30 days, comes first; the Jan. 25 start date follows.
