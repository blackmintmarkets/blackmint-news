Fed's Waller Says a Half-Point Hike Is Probably Needed in December

Futures price a quarter-point Fed hike on Dec. 9. Waller told CNBC the Fed may need to double that, or spell out a January move, to curb inflation.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said Wednesday that a half-point interest rate increase is probably needed at the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting, telling CNBC the central bank "might need to, like, shock people."

Futures already fully price a quarter-point hike in December, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool as cited by CNBC. A half-point move would be double that.

Waller's Two Options

Waller said the Fed could instead raise rates by a quarter point and clearly flag another hike in January, either in its statement or at Chairman Kevin Warsh's press conference, Reuters reported. Without that, he said, "you have to go 50 in December." CNBC noted Warsh has opposed that kind of forward guidance.

"I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," Waller said. He told the network the Fed needs to reverse its three quarter-point cuts from 2025 and may have to go further.

Reuters reported Waller is worried that businesses now treat 3% inflation as normal and pass it on to customers.

How a Bigger Hike Reaches Crypto

The Fed's benchmark rate sets the floor for what cash and short-term Treasuries pay. When that floor rises, assets that pay no interest, such as bitcoin, have to compete with a safer return. Crypto's basis trade, which pays traders for holding bitcoin against short futures, now yields about 3% to 5% a year, near or below Treasury yields, according to Talos data cited by CoinDesk.

Bitcoin traded at $62,311 at 11:47 a.m. ET, down 2.2% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

Not every official agrees. New York Fed President John Williams said Tuesday, "I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action," CNBC reported. The Fed releases minutes of its October meeting at 2 p.m. ET Wednesday.

In our opinion: The Fed raised rates once in September and held in October, so a half-point move would mark a clear change in pace. Waller's real target is the gap between what markets price and what he thinks is needed. The October minutes due at 2 p.m. ET are the first look at how many colleagues lean his way before the Dec. 9 decision.
