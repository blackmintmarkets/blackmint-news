CFTC Approves Backpack's U.S. Exchange and Clearinghouse for Crypto Perps

The CFTC designated Backpack's exchange on Nov. 16, three days after registering its clearinghouse. No U.S. launch date has been set.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission approved Backpack's U.S. derivatives exchange on Monday, Nov. 16, three days after it registered the firm's clearinghouse. That gives the crypto company both federal licenses it needs to offer leveraged perpetual futures to American traders. It has not said when trading starts.

The agency designated TLD Markets, Inc., which does business as Backpack Exchange, as a contract market in an order of designation dated Nov. 16. Its affiliate, TLD Clearing, Inc., or Backpack Clearing, was registered on Nov. 13 to clear margined futures and fully collateralized futures, options on futures and swaps, the CFTC's clearinghouse registry shows.

Two licenses, two jobs

A designated contract market is a federally supervised exchange that can list futures and options. A derivatives clearing organization is the clearinghouse behind it: it stands between buyer and seller on each trade, collects margin and guarantees settlement if one side can't pay. Perpetual futures, the product Backpack plans to list, are futures with no expiry date, so a trader can hold a leveraged position open as long as margin holds up.

Backpack said in a press release that U.S. users will be able to trade those contracts 24/7, post cash or crypto as collateral, cross-margin positions and trade in the same global order book as its overseas customers. The company called it the first blockchain-based derivatives exchange in the U.S. and said it serves users in more than 150 countries and regions, with more than $450 billion in trading volume. The Defiant reported the U.S. service is still taking waitlist sign-ups.

"From a policy perspective this is a really big deal," said Mark Wetjen, president of Backpack US, adding that it moves "price discovery from offshore unregulated venues to the United States."

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said in a statement that the law sets core principles "without prescribing a particular technological architecture." He added that firms built on blockchains must meet the same standards as everyone else, including anti-money laundering programs, segregation of customer funds, market surveillance and system safeguards.

In our opinion: Backpack's pitch is that leveraged crypto trading belongs onshore, under a U.S. regulator's rules on margin and customer money. It now holds both licenses to test that idea. The paperwork is done, but the order book is still a waitlist, so the launch date and how much volume shows up are the numbers to watch.
