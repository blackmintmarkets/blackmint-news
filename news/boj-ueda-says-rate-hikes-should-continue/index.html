BOJ's Ueda Says Rate Hikes Should Continue as Policy Stays Easy

Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda told parliament the bank should keep raising its 1.25% policy rate because financial conditions are still easy.

Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda told parliament on Wednesday, Nov. 18, Tokyo time, that the central bank should keep raising interest rates, Reuters reported. Japan's policy rate stands at 1.25%.

The yen matters to crypto because of borrowing. For years, cheap yen loans helped fund bets on higher-yielding assets abroad. When Japanese rates rise, that funding costs more, and some of those trades get closed.

Ueda Points to Easy Financial Conditions

"Given that financial conditions are still accommodative, it would be appropriate to continue raising the policy rate," Ueda said, according to Reuters. He added that the bank wants underlying inflation anchored around its 2% target over time.

He also addressed the government's planned cut to the consumption tax on food. Ueda said it would probably support growth by lifting households' real buying power, and that the BOJ would study its effect on underlying inflation once the details are final. investingLive reported that he expected the impact to stay limited but called it highly uncertain for now.

How a Policy Rate Hike Works

The policy rate is what the BOJ sets for overnight lending between banks. Raising it pushes up borrowing costs across the economy, from mortgages to company loans. "Accommodative" means rates are still low enough to encourage borrowing and spending, even after past hikes.

What He Did Not Commit To

Ueda did not name a date for the next move. At the Oct. 30 meeting, the BOJ held at 1.25% while saying underlying inflation had reached its target, and board member Hajime Takata voted for a hike to 1.5%, Reuters reported at the time. On emergency bond buying, Ueda said such operations could backfire if markets saw the BOJ as financing government spending: "In that case, long-term interest rates could become even more volatile."

The BOJ's next policy meeting is in December.

In our opinion: Ueda repeated the bank's direction rather than adding anything new, so the December meeting and the food tax details now carry more weight. A higher Japanese rate makes yen-funded trades more expensive to hold, and crypto has been on the receiving end of those unwinds before. The number to watch is whether the BOJ moves the 1.25% rate next month.
