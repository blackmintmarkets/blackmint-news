Sky's USDS Supply Falls 38% After Report on Illicit-Linked Swaps

About $2 billion of USDS left circulation in a day as the token held near $1. Sky said its peg reserve is working as designed.

The supply of Sky's USDS stablecoin fell about 38% in one day, to $3.24 billion at 5:47 p.m. ET Thursday from $5.24 billion a day earlier, DefiLlama data show. The token held its peg, trading at $1.00 on CoinGecko.

The exits followed a CoinDesk report Thursday morning on research by risk firm Inca Digital. Inca traced $518 million in transactions through Sky's Peg Stability Module that involved wallets it linked to hackers, sanctioned entities and other illicit actors. Inca tied about $101.9 million of that to North Korean actors, including wallets funded from the February 2025 Bybit hack, according to the report.

How the reserve works

The Peg Stability Module, or PSM, is a pool that lets anyone swap Circle's USDC for Sky's USDS or DAI at about one to one. When USDS slips below $1, traders can turn it in for USDC, which pulls the price back up. Circle can freeze USDC; USDS has no such switch. Inca's concern, as CoinDesk reported it, is that the USDC sits in one shared address. A court-ordered freeze could lock the whole pool along with the flagged money.

One holder drove most of the exits

More than $1.5 billion in USDC left the PSM after the report, most of it from one large holder whose address is attributed to the Poloniex exchange, CoinDesk reported, citing blockchain data. The report noted the label doesn't show who started the trades.

Sky's response

Sky said on X that the PSM has processed more than $968 billion since launch, so the alleged figures equal about 0.03% of volume. It said the activity ran mostly through third-party platforms, and its own front ends block flagged wallets.

Rune Christensen, founder of Sky, formerly MakerDAO, wrote that USDS is backed by about $10 billion in collateral and that Sky "handled over $2B in outflows in the past day." He said peg liquidity "never dropped below $2B." He added that Sky's Core Council announced a 5.25 million USDS buyback of SKY tokens over seven days.

In our opinion: the peg held on Thursday while about $2 billion left. The harder question is legal. If a court or police agency ever asks Circle to freeze the pooled USDC, every holder who relies on that pool would share the hit along with the flagged wallets. The next thing to watch is whether Sky adds wallet screening at the protocol level.
