Fed's Williams Says Inflation Near 3.5% Leaves 'Work to Do'

The New York Fed chief expects inflation to slow to just above 2% next year and hit the 2% goal in 2028. The Fed's next decision is Dec. 9.

New York Fed President John Williams said Monday that inflation of about 3.5% is "simply too high" and that the central bank has more work to do to bring it back to its 2% goal, in prepared remarks in the Bronx.

His remarks came about two weeks before the committee's Dec. 8-9 meeting, and he didn't say what he wants there.

Three causes, one target

Williams tied the inflation to higher tariffs, energy and supply-chain shocks linked to conflicts in the Middle East, and strong demand for chips and other goods used in the AI buildout. He said tariff effects "have largely abated." He expects inflation of about 3.5% this year, just above 2% next year and 2% in 2028, with growth near 2.75% and unemployment edging down to about 4%.

"We must take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2 percent goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis," he said. CNBC said the remarks indicate higher rates are likely over time.

Why rates reach crypto

The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% in October. A higher rate raises the return on cash and Treasury bills. Bitcoin pays no interest, so higher yields raise the cost of holding it instead of those assets. Williams also said policy takes one to two years to have its full effect, so he said the Fed must stay forward-looking.

Bitcoin traded near $61,047 at 5:49 p.m. ET, up 0.5% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The phrase worth chasing is "over time." Williams laid out a slow, steady path back to 2% by 2028, and he kept his December view to himself. That leaves the Dec. 9 decision and the Fed's new projections as the next test for every asset that pays no yield, bitcoin included.
