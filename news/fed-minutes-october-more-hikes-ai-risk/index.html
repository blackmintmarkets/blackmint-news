Fed Minutes Show Almost All Officials Expect More Rate Hikes

The October minutes show a 10-2 vote to hold at 3.75%-4%, two dissents for a hike, and wide concern that AI spending is feeding inflation.

Almost all Federal Reserve officials judged at their Oct. 27-28 meeting that further interest rate increases "would likely be appropriate," according to minutes released Wednesday. The committee still held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% in a 10-2 vote.

Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented, preferring a quarter-point hike at that meeting, the minutes show. A couple of officials said an increase of more than 25 basis points, or a quarter of a percentage point, could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data came in unfavorable.

How Fed Minutes Work

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), the Fed group that sets short-term rates, publishes minutes three weeks after each decision. They summarize the debate without naming speakers. They count heads in words, from "a couple" up to "almost all." Officials who backed the October hold pointed to the benefit of gathering more information first, the minutes said.

AI Spending Enters the Inflation Debate

"Many participants viewed the AI buildout as a material source of inflation risk," the minutes said. The worry is that the data-center boom raises demand faster than capacity can expand. Officials also looked at the debt behind it: most said AI-related debt "had grown rapidly," much of it through "complex and opaque financing structures." Several noted the staff view that the financial system remained resilient and banks were well capitalized.

From Rates to Crypto

A rate hike raises what savers earn on cash and Treasury bills. That raises the bar for assets that pay no interest, such as bitcoin. Bitcoin traded at $62,196 at 5:55 p.m. ET, down 3.9% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

Futures markets fully price a quarter-point hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting, CNBC reported, citing CME Group's FedWatch tool.

In our opinion: The October hold read as patient on the day, but the minutes show a committee with its coat already on, waiting for one more look at the data. The new wrinkle is AI: the Fed now treats the data-center boom as both an inflation source and a debt risk, which ties the rate story crypto traders follow to tech balance sheets. The date to circle is Dec. 9, and the detail to watch is whether more than "a couple" of officials back a half-point move.
