French Senate Rejects Crypto Wallet Declaration and Stablecoin Tax

Senators turned down three crypto amendments to France's 2027 budget, including a rule to report self-held crypto worth over 100,000 euros and an exit tax.

France's Senate on Tuesday rejected three crypto tax amendments to the 2027 budget, including a rule that holders declare self-custodied crypto worth 100,000 euros or more, Unchained and CoinDesk reported. Senators also turned down a tax on swaps into stablecoins and a plan to extend France's exit tax to crypto.

How French crypto tax works now

Today, trading one crypto for another is not taxed in France. Tax is due when a holder sells for euros or spends crypto on goods. That deferral also covers swaps into stablecoins, tokens pegged to a currency such as the euro or dollar. The amendment from Senator Pascal Savoldelli would have taxed gains on those stablecoin swaps.

Self-custody means holding crypto in a wallet you control, not at an exchange. The declaration rule would have made owners list such holdings (above the 100,000-euro line) with the tax office.

The government's reasons

Public Accounts Minister David Amiel opposed the stablecoin tax, saying the tokens are not legal tender, both outlets reported. On the wallet rule, he said a declaration would not help authorities track crypto used in money laundering or tax fraud. He pointed to DAC8, the EU rule that already makes crypto platforms report user data to tax authorities.

CoinDesk reported that some crypto holders had warned a wallet list could expose owners to criminals if the data leaked.

The National Assembly, France's lower house, had approved a stablecoin swap tax in October, both outlets reported. None of the three passed. Current crypto tax rules stay in place for now.

In our opinion: For now, French holders keep the right to move into stablecoins without a tax bill, and they avoid a new registry of their self-held coins. The state gives up a revenue source senators wanted, and it relies on DAC8 reporting instead. The 2027 budget is still moving through Parliament, so watch whether any of these measures returns before final passage.
