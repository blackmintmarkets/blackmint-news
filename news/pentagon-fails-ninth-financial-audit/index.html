Pentagon Fails Ninth Straight Audit of Over $4.8 Trillion

The inspector general issued a disclaimer of opinion. One retiree health fund got its first clean opinion; four more reviews are due in February.

The Pentagon failed to get a clean, department-wide financial audit for the ninth straight year, the inspector general said Monday, Nov. 16. The review covered more than $4.8 trillion in assets across nearly 30 components and involved about 1,500 auditors, the inspector general's office said.

Congress set a Dec. 31, 2028, deadline for a clean, or unqualified, opinion on the department's books. The requirement is in the fiscal 2024 National Defense Authorization Act, the Government Accountability Office and a Congressional Research Service primer both note.

What a disclaimer of opinion means

A disclaimer of opinion means the department could not give auditors enough evidence to say whether its financial statements are reliable. That is not a finding that the figures are wrong, and it is not a passing grade. A clean, or unmodified, opinion means auditors had enough evidence to say the statements fairly present the department's finances.

The audit's 2028 target, and a caution from the watchdog

The Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund received its first unmodified opinion this month, the inspector general and the department said. The department said it also expects improved opinions in February on four more components. Those are the Navy Working Capital Fund, the Defense Information Systems Agency's general fund, the Defense Intelligence Agency and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.

"For the first time, by February 2027, we will have clean opinions on a majority of our assets and over 90% of our liabilities," Comptroller Jules W. Hurst III said in the department's release. Those shares include the opinions expected in January and February.

The inspector general's reports listed 14 material weaknesses in internal controls and five instances of noncompliance with laws and regulations. Brett Mansfield, the deputy inspector general for audit, said the apparent reduction "stems from consolidation, not resolution," Just the News reported.

Hurst said the department is consolidating 25 statements into eight and has hired an independent public accountant. Several component audits remain open, the inspector general said.

In our opinion: A disclaimer is not proof that money is missing, and it is not a passing grade. After nine years, auditors still lack the evidence to sign the books, even as one fund earned its first clean opinion. The legal deadline is Dec. 31, 2028. Watch the four opinions due in February, and whether that strategy later yields a clean department-wide opinion for fiscal 2028.
