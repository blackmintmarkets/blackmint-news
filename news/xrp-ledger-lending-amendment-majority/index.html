XRP Ledger Lending Feature Wins Validator Majority, Eyes Dec. 11

LendingProtocol now needs two weeks above 80% validator support. It would let brokers make uncollateralized, fixed-term loans from single-asset vaults.

Native lending on the XRP Ledger moved a step closer on Friday. Validators gave the LendingProtocol amendment, and a companion update, majority support at 9:22 a.m. ET, starting a two-week clock that ends Dec. 11, according to data from a public XRP Ledger server queried by Black Mint.

If support holds, the ledger would gain built-in, fixed-term loans funded from pooled deposits. Lenders could then put stablecoins to work inside the ledger itself. The network holds more than $800 million of them, per DefiLlama at 11:55 a.m. ET.

How an XRPL Amendment Passes

Changes to how the XRP Ledger processes transactions ship as amendments. Under the ledger's rules, one passes only after it keeps support from more than 80% of trusted validators for two straight weeks. If support drops to 80% or below, the clock restarts.

The ledger checks votes at a "flag ledger," every 256th ledger, roughly every 15 minutes. The server showed both lending amendments with majority status from that checkpoint, but not yet enabled.

What the Lending Feature Does

According to the XRPL documentation, the protocol (spec XLS-66) offers fixed-term loans with no on-chain collateral. A loan broker checks borrowers' credit off the ledger, then lends from a vault, a pool that holds one asset.

That means the lender's protection depends on the broker's underwriting, not on automatic liquidation. The XLS-66 specification lets a broker post optional first-loss capital, its own money that absorbs part of a default before depositors do.

The V1_1 update makes new brokers use closed-ended vaults, with set phases for deposits, lending and withdrawals. It also counts interest only when borrowers actually pay it.

The vaults themselves come from a separate amendment, SingleAssetVault. It reached majority at 2:38 p.m. ET on Nov. 25, which points to Dec. 9 if support holds.

Ripple's RLUSD makes up about $742 million of that, per DefiLlama.

In our opinion: The question worth chasing is who ends up holding the credit risk. With no collateral to sell, vault depositors lean on a broker's off-chain checks and whatever first-loss cover it posts. The first test is Dec. 9 for vaults and Dec. 11 for lending, and only if support stays above 80% until then.
