Japan's Core Inflation Rises to 2.2%, Its Highest Reading of 2026

Power and gas bills drove the rise, while prices excluding energy cooled to 2.2% from 2.4%. Bitcoin was down 3.4% over 24 hours.

Japan's core consumer prices rose 2.2% in October from a year earlier, up from 2.1% in September, the Statistics Bureau of Japan said Friday morning Tokyo time, Thursday evening ET. That's the highest reading of 2026.

Economists polled by Reuters had expected 2.3%, CNBC reported. Headline inflation rose to 2.3% from 2.1%.

Energy did the lifting

Energy prices rose 2.2% from a year earlier after falling 0.6% in September, the bureau's report shows. Electricity went from a 2.4% drop to a 1.6% gain, and city gas rose 5.3% after 0.2%. Household energy subsidies expired in September, CNBC said.

Strip out energy and the picture cooled. Prices excluding fresh food and energy rose 2.2%, down from 2.4%. Rice other than the Koshihikari variety fell 29.8%.

How Japan measures inflation

The consumer price index tracks what a set basket of goods and services costs each month. Japan's "core" measure leaves out fresh food, because weather swings those prices around. The Bank of Japan aims for 2% inflation. A year ago, core inflation ran at 3.0%; it fell to 1.4% in April and May before climbing back.

Market reaction

The Nikkei 225 fell 2.1% after the data, and the yen traded at 159.59 per dollar, CNBC reported. LSEG data put the odds of a quarter-point BOJ hike in December at 72%, per CNBC. Higher Japanese rates raise the cost of borrowing in yen.

Bitcoin traded at $61,410 at 11:54 p.m. ET Thursday, down 3.4% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: the headline rose, but the measure without energy slowed, so October's increase came mostly from power and gas bills. That hands the BOJ a mixed read heading into December. Households carry the near-term cost; yen borrowers carry the rate risk if the hike comes. Tokyo's November CPI on Nov. 27 is the next data point.
