India ED Searches CoinDCX, Delta Over Alleged FEMA Violations

Searches hit three Bengaluru sites Nov. 4-5. Both firms say they cooperated; CoinDCX called it a routine enquiry, Moneycontrol reported.

India's Enforcement Directorate searched three Bengaluru premises linked to CoinDCX and Delta Exchange on Nov. 4 and 5 over alleged violations of the Foreign Exchange Management Act, the agency said on Nov. 6.

The probe targets Neblio Technologies Private Limited, which operates CoinDCX, and Excelium Technologies Private Limited, which operates Delta Exchange India, Moneycontrol and CNBC-TV18 reported, citing the agency. The ED alleges the platforms offered crypto futures and options and trading in tokenised foreign stocks without authorisation for the cross-border flows involved.

What the agency alleges

According to the ED, CoinDCX users deposited Indian rupees, which the platform converted into USDT and sent to overseas exchanges including Binance, KuCoin and Gate.io to fill spot orders. The agency said those transfers ran through an account in the name of a Costa Rica-based entity it called a paper company, and that the volume ran into thousands of crores of rupees. It also alleged CoinDCX opened matching futures and options positions on overseas venues, and that some global stock futures were actually placed on Hyperliquid, a foreign decentralised platform. Delta Exchange India, the ED said, transferred hundreds of crores of rupees to overseas associates and routed a large volume of derivatives and tokenised-stock trades through CoinDCX. None of those claims has been proven in court.

How the platforms answered

A CoinDCX spokesperson said ED officials visited the Bengaluru office as part of a routine enquiry, that the firm cooperated fully, and that as an FIU-IND-registered entity it complies with applicable Indian laws including FEMA. Delta Exchange India said trading, deposits and withdrawals continue normally, user funds remain in Indian banks, and it does not facilitate cross-border virtual-digital-asset transfers. The ED said further investigation is underway (no charges have been filed in the reports we checked).

USDT is a dollar-pegged stablecoin issued by Tether. Converting rupees into USDT and sending them abroad can, under FEMA rules, count as a cross-border capital transfer that needs authorisation. That is the legal hinge the agency is testing.

In our opinion: the hinge is whether rupee-to-USDT routes to foreign exchanges violate FEMA. India already allows domestic crypto trading under tax and FIU rules. Both firms say they are cooperating; the next checkable step is whatever the ED files or freezes next.
