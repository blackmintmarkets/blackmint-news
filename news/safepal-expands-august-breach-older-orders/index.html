SafePal Adds 37,542 Older Order Records to August Data Breach

The wallet maker says it deleted the records needed to email this group, so buyers from Sept. 2022 to March 2025 should treat their details as exposed.

Crypto wallet maker SafePal said in an update dated Nov. 28 that a data breach it first disclosed in August reached further back than it reported, adding 37,542 order-related records from purchases made between Sept. 6, 2022, and March 1, 2025.

The update, posted to SafePal's security page, says the company began looking again after reports on Nov. 25 of phishing emails sent to customers whose orders fell outside the original window. The figure counts records, including refunds and failed orders, and is "not 37,542 unique customers," SafePal said.

What was exposed

For the newly found group, SafePal said the data was limited to names, email addresses, countries, order IDs, products bought and payment IDs. It said shipping addresses, phone numbers, card or bank details, wallet payment addresses, recovery phrases and private keys were not part of it. TechFlow and The Defiant also reported the update.

SafePal said this is an expansion of the same incident, not a new breach. It said the original authorization flaw, a gap in who could view order data, was fixed in August, and that security firm Cure53 found no remaining vulnerabilities in an audit completed Oct. 30. The earlier disclosed window, orders from March 2, 2025, to April 11, 2026, is unchanged.

Why buyers will not get an email

A recovery phrase is the list of words that can restore a wallet and move its funds on any device. Order data cannot open a wallet, but it can make a fake message look real. SafePal said it deleted the customer records needed to contact this group under its data policy, so it cannot notify them one by one. It asked buyers from that period to "consider their information potentially affected." On Nov. 12, the company warned of physical letters with QR codes leading to phishing sites.

SafePal said it will never ask for a recovery phrase or private keys, and urged users to ignore unofficial firmware-update instructions.

In our opinion: What raises the risk for buyers is the combination of real order details and no direct warning. Anyone who bought from SafePal between September 2022 and March 2025 has to find this notice on their own. The practical step is the one the company lists: never type a recovery phrase into any site, email or QR-code page, whatever order number it quotes.
