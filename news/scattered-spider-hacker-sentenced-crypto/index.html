Scottish Hacker Gets Six Years in $11.6 Million SIM-Swap Crypto Case

Tyler Buchanan, 24, got 72 months in U.S. federal prison over a phishing and SIM-swap scheme that took at least $11.6 million in crypto, prosecutors said.

A federal judge in Santa Ana, California, sentenced Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, to 72 months in prison on Tuesday for a scheme that stole at least $11.6 million in crypto from U.S. victims, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Central District of California said.

Buchanan pleaded guilty on April 17 to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and one count of aggravated identity theft. U.S. District Judge John W. Holcomb set a restitution hearing for Dec. 11. Buchanan is the fifth and final defendant sentenced in the case, prosecutors said.

How the Scheme Worked, According to the Plea Agreement

From September 2021 to April 2023, Buchanan and others sent hundreds of text messages to employees of at least a dozen companies, including telecom, tech and crypto firms, prosecutors said. The texts linked to fake login pages that captured usernames and passwords.

The group then used data taken from those companies to find individual crypto holders and carry out SIM swaps. In a SIM swap, a criminal tricks a mobile carrier into moving a victim's phone number to a SIM card the criminal controls. That lets the criminal receive the one-time security codes sent by text and get into the victim's accounts.

At his plea in April, prosecutors put the losses at $8 million. The figure has since risen to at least $11.6 million. The largest single theft was $1.67 million in December 2022, the BBC reported, citing prosecutors.

Who Else Was Sentenced

Buchanan was charged alongside four American co-defendants, who have all pleaded guilty and are serving prison terms, the BBC said. U.S. authorities have linked the defendants to Scattered Spider, a loose group of mostly young, English-speaking hackers blamed for several large corporate breaches, the BBC said.

Prosecutors said the thefts had "severe, life-altering consequences" for victims. The prosecutors' release and the news reports Black Mint reviewed did not include a statement from Buchanan or his lawyer.

In our opinion: The Dec. 11 restitution hearing is the next date that matters for victims, because it sets what they are owed out of at least $11.6 million taken. Security codes sent by text go to whoever controls the phone number, and in this case that was the thieves. Where an exchange offers an authenticator app or hardware key, that option does not depend on the carrier.
