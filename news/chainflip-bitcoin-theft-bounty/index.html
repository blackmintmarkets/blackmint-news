Chainflip Says Attacker Also Took 122.86 Bitcoin, Offers 10% Bounty

The swap protocol's own tally puts the loss near $12.3 million, up from $4.86 million first traced on Ethereum. Its return deadline is 1:38 p.m. ET Monday.

Chainflip said Saturday the attacker who drained its vaults also took 122.86 bitcoin, lifting the loss to about $12.3 million at 5:51 p.m. ET prices. The project offered a 10% bounty for the rest.

That is more than double the roughly $4.86 million first traced from its Ethereum vault early Saturday. In a message to the attacker on its website, Chainflip Labs listed 122.86 BTC, 520.22 ETH, 3,249,631 USDC and 758,141 USDT. The Defiant also reported the bitcoin figure.

Black Mint valued the haul using CoinGecko prices at 5:51 p.m. ET, with bitcoin at $60,060 and ether at $1,679. The bitcoin alone comes to about $7.4 million.

Keep 10%, return 90%

Chainflip said in an X post at 2:22 p.m. ET that it had sent the offer as an onchain message to the address holding the bitcoin. The terms ask for 110.58 BTC and 2,618.1 ETH by 1:38 p.m. ET on Monday, Nov. 23. If both arrive, Chainflip says it will treat the case as a white-hat recovery and withdraw its complaints to law enforcement.

What Chainflip says it found

The company said it traced how the attack was set up: funding through the Tornado Cash mixer on Nov. 11, a purchase of FLIP tokens and a validator account registered on Nov. 20. It said it has identified how the signing credentials were exposed and has blocked that route. Those are Chainflip's statements. It has not published a full post-mortem, and no one has been named or charged.

How the vaults are guarded

Chainflip's validators must bond FLIP as collateral to join the network, according to its documentation. The vaults move funds only with an aggregate key controlled by 100 of 150 validators through a threshold signature scheme, a method that splits signing power so no single party holds it whole.

FLIP traded at $0.308 at 5:51 p.m. ET, down 35.9% in 24 hours, with a market value of about $26.5 million, CoinGecko data show.

In our opinion: The new tally puts the loss at nearly half of FLIP's entire $26.5 million market value. Chainflip's posts and return page don't say who will cover the shortfall: users, liquidity providers or the company. Monday's 1:38 p.m. ET deadline is the first date to watch, and the post-mortem explaining the breach is the second.
