World Bank to Test Circle's USDC and Citi Rails for Development Payouts

The World Bank Group said it will explore paying out development funds in USDC, with Citi linking bank rails and Circle minting and settling the stablecoin.

The World Bank Group said Tuesday it will explore using Circle's USDC stablecoin and Citi's banking network to send development money, building on a blockchain ledger already used by more than 350 projects in 96 countries, according to its press release.

The lender said the test will look at whether regulated dollar stablecoins can work alongside, or improve on, the way it pays out funds today. Citi would supply its links to existing bank rails, and Circle would mint and settle the funds in USDC, Decrypt reported, in line with the release.

How the money moves now

World Bank disbursements currently run through correspondent banking: a chain of banks that each hold accounts with one another and pass an international payment along, one step at a time. A stablecoin is a token pegged one-to-one to the dollar and backed by reserves, so it can move on a blockchain at any hour. The catch is that someone still has to turn it back into local money at the other end, which is the job Citi's bank connections would fill.

The first phase will use an "intermediated and interoperable" model and is limited to borrower countries where digital assets are permitted, the release said. Other banks are expected to join later. No dollar amounts, launch date or list of countries were given.

The effort extends FundsChain, the World Bank's existing blockchain platform that lets everyone on a project see how money is spent. President Ajay Banga said the aim is that "a farmer gets paid before the harvest season ends."

USDC had a market value of about $68.9 billion at 5:47 p.m. ET, according to CoinGecko. Circle and Citi are the only named partners.

In our opinion: The release describes an exploration with no dollar figure attached, so the size of the test will matter more than the logos. Still, a lender that moves money into 96 countries is weighing a stablecoin against correspondent banking, a sign of how far regulated stablecoins have moved into mainstream finance. Watch which borrower countries sign on first and whether a second bank joins Citi.
