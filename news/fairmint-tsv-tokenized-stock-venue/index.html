Fairmint Plans Tokenized Stock Venue Under SEC's Innovation Exemption

Fairmint said its new subsidiary will trade issuer-sponsored tokenized shares through automated market makers, starting on Canton. Trading hasn't begun, and no launch date is set.

Fairmint, a company that records company shares on blockchains, said Friday, Nov. 20, that it plans to run a trading venue for tokenized stocks under the Securities and Exchange Commission's Innovation Exemption, according to its press release. It would become at least the fifth firm to signal plans under the program.

What the venue would trade

The subsidiary, Fairmint TSV LLC, will start on the Canton Network, with Ethereum planned next. It will trade issuer-sponsored shares, meaning the company itself has authorized putting its stock on a blockchain. Fairmint said most tokenized stocks today are tokens that track a share, while its venue trades the share itself.

Trades will run through automated market makers, or AMMs: software that prices and fills orders from pools of assets, with no traditional order book. Tradecraft, a protocol built on Canton, will be the first. Fairmint's SEC-registered transfer agent, Fairmint TA, will verify holders and keep the official ownership record onchain. A transfer agent is the firm that tracks who legally owns a company's shares.

"To trade real shares, they need what every market needs: a clear answer to who owns what, and someone accountable for it," CEO Joris Delanoue said in the release.

The rules behind it

The SEC issued the Innovation Exemption order on Sept. 17, 2026. It gives tokenized securities venues conditional, temporary relief from registering as exchanges. A venue must post a public notice at least 30 calendar days before it operates and then notify the SEC. The agency's list shows four firms that have done so: TSV LLC, OKXICE TSV, Perpetuals.com and Formation Securities. Fairmint is not on it yet.

Fairmint said trading has not begun, and that initial stocks, eligible participants and a launch date will come later, a point CoinDesk also reported.

In our opinion: the detail that matters is the transfer agent, because it means buyers would own the share itself, recorded on the company's own register. The 30-day notice rule also sets the clock, since no Fairmint trading can start until a month after its notice goes public. For a market built on instant settlement, the paperwork still moves at the speed of a calendar.
