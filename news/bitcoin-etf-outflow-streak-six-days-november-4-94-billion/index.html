Bitcoin ETFs Lose $231 Million, Sixth Straight Day of Outflows

Spot bitcoin ETFs lost $231.4 million on Tuesday, the smallest of six straight outflow days. IBIT led again; three funds took in money.

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost a net $231.4 million on Tuesday, their sixth straight day of outflows, pushing November's total to $4.94 billion, Farside Investors data show.

It was also the smallest withdrawal of the six. Monday's was $569.7 million, and last Friday's $669.2 million was the month's largest.

BlackRock's fund again led the exits

  • BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT): $175.3 million out
  • Fidelity's FBTC: $44.0 million out
  • Ark's ARKB: $15.8 million out
  • Bitwise's BITB: $10.3 million out
  • Franklin's EZBC, Grayscale's Bitcoin Mini Trust and Morgan Stanley's MSBT took in a combined $14.0 million

SoSoValue, a second data provider, put the day at $231.56 million in net outflows, TokenPost reported, and counted the same six-day streak.

A spot bitcoin ETF holds actual bitcoin. When investors redeem more shares than they buy, the fund hands back or sells bitcoin to pay them, and that net figure is the "outflow." These funds have been trading since January 2024.

A monthly record, now bigger

November's $4.94 billion is the largest monthly outflow in Farside's records, above June 2026's $4.51 billion. Fifteen of the month's 17 trading days have been negative. Net inflows since launch still total about $52.1 billion, Farside's figures show.

Total net assets across the funds stood at $72.01 billion, about 5.99% of bitcoin's market value, according to SoSoValue data cited by TokenPost.

Bitcoin traded at $58,852 at 5:54 a.m. ET, down 0.95% over 24 hours, CoinGecko data show.

In our opinion: A smaller outflow is still an outflow, and the November tally grows with each red day. Even so, about $52.1 billion of net money is still in these funds, so the month has taken back less than a tenth of what came in before it. The next figure is Wednesday's: an inflow would be the funds' first since Nov. 16, when they took in $7.9 million.
