Wells Fargo Reports $2.35 Billion in BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF, Up From $812 Million

Wells Fargo's 13F shows 49.58 million IBIT shares on Sept. 30, about double June's count. The filing includes client accounts, not just the bank's own money.

Wells Fargo reported 49.58 million shares of BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) worth $2.35 billion at the end of September, up from 24.40 million shares worth $812 million three months earlier, according to its quarterly 13F filing made Monday, Nov. 16. The share count roughly doubled. The dollar value nearly tripled.

The gap between those two numbers is mostly price. Bitcoin rose about 42% from June 30 to Sept. 30, based on BlackRock's figures, CoinDesk reported. The same filing also lists options on IBIT: calls covering 717,200 shares and puts covering 540,000 shares.

What a 13F does and doesn't show

A 13F is a snapshot that large investment managers file with the SEC 45 days after each quarter ends, listing the U.S.-traded stocks and funds they oversee. Wells Fargo's filing covers the bank and several of its investment managers, and many of the positions sit in accounts run for clients. So it tells readers how much IBIT runs through the Wells Fargo system, not how much bitcoin exposure the bank carries on its own balance sheet. The June-quarter filing gives the earlier figures.

CoinDesk estimated the position equals about 28,070 bitcoin, based on IBIT holding 801,033 BTC against 1.415 billion shares on Sept. 30.

The numbers are seven weeks old. Bitcoin traded at $64,212 at 5:52 a.m. ET Tuesday, according to CoinGecko, down 0.4% in 24 hours. The filing says nothing about whether those accounts bought or sold since.

In our opinion: The more telling detail is the 103% jump in share count. Price alone can't explain that, so the bank's advisers and clients were adding IBIT through the summer. The next real check is the December-quarter 13F, due in mid-February, which will show what they did during the slide.
