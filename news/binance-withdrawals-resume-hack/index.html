Binance Lifts Withdrawal Halt Four Weeks After $6.3 Billion Hack

The exchange said recovery work is complete. Researchers cited by Reuters put stolen funds routed through THORChain above $600 million.

Binance said Thursday it has fully lifted the withdrawal suspension it put in place after hackers took an estimated $6.3 billion from the exchange on Oct. 29, 28 days earlier.

"Our withdrawal recovery work is completed," the exchange said in a service update. It said withdrawals on a small number of networks remain paused for maintenance or planned upgrades, separate from the recovery, affecting "a very small percentage of regular traffic." Reuters also reported the reopening. Binance said in the update that user assets "remain fully backed and accounted for."

Why Exchanges Freeze Withdrawals

Exchanges keep part of customer funds in hot wallets, which stay connected to the internet so withdrawals can go out quickly. The October attack drained Binance hot wallets on more than 10 blockchains in about 20 minutes, according to researchers cited by Crypto Briefing. A withdrawal freeze stops more money from leaving while a platform rebuilds and checks its systems.

The FBI attributed the theft on Oct. 31 to TraderTraitor, a hacking group linked to North Korea, Crypto Briefing reported. Reuters also described the attackers as North Korean hackers.

Stolen Funds Move Through THORChain

More than $600 million of the stolen funds has passed through THORChain, Reuters reported, citing blockchain researchers, according to The Defiant and Crypto Briefing. THORChain is a decentralized exchange that swaps tokens between blockchains without a company holding the money in between. The Defiant noted the estimate may count some funds more than once, and that the FBI had asked THORChain node operators to block transactions tied to the theft.

A response from THORChain to the figures was not included in the reports Black Mint reviewed.

BNB, the token tied to Binance's ecosystem, traded near $555 at 5:50 p.m. ET, up 0.4% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: Reopened withdrawals answer the first question for Binance customers, though the update itself says a few networks are still paused. The trail of the stolen money is still being mapped, and the $600 million THORChain estimate comes with a double-counting caveat. Binance points users to its network status page before moving funds, which is worth checking first.
