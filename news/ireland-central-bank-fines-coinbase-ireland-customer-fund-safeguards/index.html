Ireland's Central Bank Fines Coinbase Unit €3.25 Million Over Customer-Fund Safeguards

Coinbase Ireland admitted it failed to properly protect customer funds between 2020 and 2025. The fine still needs High Court approval.

Ireland's financial regulator has fined Coinbase's Irish e-money business over how it protected customer money.

The penalty

The Central Bank of Ireland said on Thursday that it fined Coinbase Ireland Limited €3,252,618 (about $3.6 million, per CoinDesk and The Block) for breaking safeguarding and outsourcing rules between Nov. 1, 2020, and Aug. 19, 2025. The penalty started at €4,646,597 and was cut by 30% under a settlement. Coinbase Ireland admitted the breaches and accepted a reprimand. The sanctions take effect only once the High Court confirms them.

What went wrong

The regulator said the unit leaned too heavily on its U.S. parent to run its safeguarding controls and lacked enough local expertise to oversee them. It also said the dedicated account meant to hold customer funds was underfunded for long stretches. According to the settlement notice, as reported by The Block, daily shortfalls reached €5.2 million in 2024. About 30 institutional clients also paid dollars into accounts held by Coinbase Inc. rather than the Irish entity between March 2023 and October 2024.

Coinbase said the issues are fully resolved and no customers lost money or were unable to reach their funds, The Block reported. The company said it reported several of the problems itself in January 2024. The Block noted this follows a €21.5 million fine on Coinbase Europe in November 2025 over transaction monitoring.

Why it matters for crypto

Keeping customer money separate and fully backed is one of the basic protections regulators expect from exchanges. This case shows Ireland's regulator holding one of the world's largest trading platforms to those standards.
