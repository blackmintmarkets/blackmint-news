Bitcoin ETFs Shed Record $5.78 Billion in November; BTC Under $60K

Friday's $389 million exit made eight straight days of outflows and pushed November past June's $4.51 billion record. Bitcoin traded near $59,150.

U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds have lost a net $5.78 billion in November, the most for any month since they launched in January 2024, Farside Investors data through Friday show. The prior record was $4.51 billion, in June 2026.

Friday added $389.0 million of outflows, the eighth straight trading day of net withdrawals. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $327.2 million of that, Farside data show. The Crypto Times, citing SoSoValue and Farside, reported the same Friday total and monthly record.

Bitcoin traded at $59,150 at 5:55 a.m. ET Saturday, down 4.0% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko. It ranged from $58,910 to $61,752 in that window. On Friday it gave up its Thanksgiving gains, falling from above $62,000 to below $59,000, Investing.com reported. The coin is up 2.1% over seven days but down 26.2% over 30 days, CoinGecko data show.

How fund outflows work

A spot bitcoin ETF holds actual bitcoin for its shareholders. A net outflow means more shares were redeemed than created that day, so the funds shrink and the bitcoin behind those shares leaves them. Farside tallies those daily changes fund by fund.

Borrowed bets felt the drop too. More than $400 million of leveraged crypto positions were forcibly closed over 24 hours, most of them bets on higher prices, according to CoinGlass figures cited by The Crypto Times ($411.69 million as of 4:19 a.m. ET) and Crypto Briefing ($407 million). A liquidation happens when an exchange closes a trader's position because the posted collateral no longer covers the loss.

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 10, its "Extreme Fear" band, on Saturday, according to Alternative.me. The Federal Reserve's next rate meeting is Dec. 8-9, per its calendar.

In our opinion: The number that matters is $5.78 billion, about $1.3 billion more than the old monthly record, with one trading day of November still to go. Bitcoin is still up for the week, but the funds have not shared in that bounce. Monday's Farside print and the Dec. 8-9 Fed meeting are the next two dates worth circling. The exits are well marked; the entrance could use a sign.
