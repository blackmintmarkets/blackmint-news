EBA Asks EU to Weigh Rules for Crypto Firms Offering DeFi Lending

An EBA report says some crypto firms market DeFi lending as a way to earn on stablecoins, though MiCA bans interest on e-money tokens.

The European Banking Authority on Wednesday asked the European Commission to assess whether crypto firms that steer clients into decentralized finance (DeFi), especially lending protocols, need extra rules, in a report on stablecoins in DeFi.

The EBA said it found evidence that some crypto-asset service providers, the exchanges and brokers licensed under the EU's MiCA law, market DeFi access as a way for users to "earn" on stablecoins. MiCA bans issuers and those firms from paying interest on e-money tokens (EMTs), stablecoins tied to one official currency such as the euro.

How the yield route works

DeFi lending protocols are blockchain apps that pool deposits and lend them to borrowers without a company in the middle. A stablecoin holder who deposits earns the interest borrowers pay. That is the yield. MiCA leaves out services run "in a fully decentralised manner," the EBA said, so the protocol sits outside the law while the licensed firm that routes a customer there does not.

Options on the table

In its earlier response to the Commission's MiCA review, the EBA listed possible measures for firms that offer this access:

  • suitability tests and extra risk disclosures
  • leverage limits
  • a ban on routing clients through DeFi to stablecoins not authorized under MiCA
  • a certification regime for DeFi lending protocols

The report names Tether's USDT among stablecoins with no MiCA authorization, which means it cannot be offered to the EU public. A 2026 survey cited by the EBA found 15% of people surveyed in Western Europe held it anyway.

EU use is small but growing

Germany, France and Spain lead the EU in DeFi activity but rank 18th, 19th and 26th worldwide, the report said, citing Chainalysis. It also cited a survey in which 42% of European-based investors expect to use DeFi by 2028. Assets locked in DeFi protocols stood near $67.2 billion on Wednesday, according to DefiLlama.

The Commission has not said whether it will add such rules in its MiCA review, CoinDesk reported.

In our opinion: The stake here is the on-ramp. Rules aimed at firms that route customers into DeFi would land on licensed exchanges and brokers, the one link in the chain EU supervisors already reach. The decision point is the Commission's MiCA review, and the EBA says it will keep feeding that review with evidence through 2027.
