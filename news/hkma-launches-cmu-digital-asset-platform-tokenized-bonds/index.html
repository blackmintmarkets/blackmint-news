Hong Kong Launches Blockchain Bond Platform With 10 Banks

The HKMA's CMU Digital Asset Platform settles tokenized bonds 24 hours a day with cash tokens. Links to regulated stablecoins are planned but not yet live.

Hong Kong's de facto central bank on Wednesday launched a blockchain-based platform for issuing and settling bonds, with 10 banks, including HSBC, Standard Chartered and Bank of China (Hong Kong), signed up as the first direct participants.

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) said the CMU Digital Asset Platform, or CMU DAP, handles a bond's whole life on a shared ledger: issuance, settlement of trades, coupon payments and repayment at maturity. It can also turn existing bonds into tokens.

Bond and cash move in one step

The platform runs on distributed ledger technology, a database that several institutions share and update together. It settles by "atomic delivery versus payment": the bond and the cash change hands in one step, or not at all. Cash on the ledger takes the form of "cash tokens," and the HKMA said the system connects directly to real-time gross settlement systems (where banks settle large payments one by one) and central bank digital currencies. It runs 24 hours a day. It handles several currencies.

The Central Moneymarkets Unit (CMU) is Hong Kong's existing depository for debt securities. It is run by CMU OmniClear, an operator set up in October 2024. CMU OmniClear said trial trades in tokenized Exchange Fund Bills, short-term HKMA debt, tested the platform earlier this year.

Stablecoin links come later, with no date

CMU OmniClear said it will "explore" adding funds and real-world assets and linking the platform to tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins. Neither release set a date for that, named a first bond issue on the system or listed fees.

CMU OmniClear's release lists the other seven: Bank of Communications (Hong Kong), China CITIC Bank International, China Construction Bank (Asia), CMB Wing Lung Bank, Crédit Agricole CIB, DBS Bank's Hong Kong branch and Industrial Bank. The Block also reported the launch and the 10-bank first batch.

"CMU OmniClear is implementing a strategic roadmap to establish the CMU as the core infrastructure for a multi-asset digital financial ecosystem in Hong Kong," HKMA Chief Executive Eddie Yue said in the statement.

In our opinion: Earlier this year this was a trial with HKMA bills. Now it is part of the city's official bond depository, with 10 banks connected on day one. The number worth chasing is the size of the first live bond issue on CMU DAP, and the first trade settled with a licensed stablecoin.
