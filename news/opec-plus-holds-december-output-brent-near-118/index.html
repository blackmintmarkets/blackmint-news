OPEC+ Holds December Output Steady With Brent Near $118

Seven OPEC+ producers changed nothing for December and set up a Nov. 29 showdown over 2027 quotas, with Brent near its highest since April.

Seven OPEC+ producers kept their December oil output targets unchanged on Sunday, pushing the hard choices to a Nov. 29 meeting while Brent crude sits near its highest level since April.

Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman met online and agreed to keep September's required production levels for December, OPEC said in a statement. The group will keep meeting monthly, and the next session is Nov. 29, the statement said. CNBC reported the same decision.

The price backdrop is steep. December Brent futures settled up $2.76, or 2.4%, at $117.64 a barrel on Friday before expiring, CNBC reported, and U.S. crude ended at $104.12. Traders were bracing for a possible escalation in the U.S.-Iran war after Tuesday's U.S. midterm elections, according to the report.

How the quota math works

Each month OPEC+ sets a required production level for each member, a ceiling meant to keep supply in line with demand. These seven spent much of 2026 raising those ceilings and finished unwinding a 1.65 million-barrel-a-day cut in September, CNBC reported. Another layer of about 2 million barrels a day of cuts runs through the end of 2026. For now the ceiling is not what holds supply back. Gulf members are still pumping well below their targets because the war has disrupted exports, per CNBC.

"Today's decision was the easy one," Jorge Leon, an analyst at Rystad, said in remarks carried by CNBC. The Nov. 29 meeting is expected to set 2027 quotas, and the International Energy Agency has warned of excess supply next year if Gulf flows return to normal, the report said.

The road from oil to bitcoin

Oil feeds into fuel, freight and airfares, so a price near $118 can keep inflation high. High inflation makes it harder for the Federal Reserve to cut rates, and rate expectations move crypto prices along with stocks. Bitcoin traded at $78,071 around noon ET, up 0.7% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: a meeting that changes nothing still sends a message, and this one says the group would rather wait out the war than guess at it. The real fight is Nov. 29, when 2027 quotas meet an IEA surplus warning. If Brent holds above $110 into that date, the Fed's inflation problem gets harder, and crypto traders will feel it in their rate bets.
