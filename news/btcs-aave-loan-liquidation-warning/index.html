BTCS Says It Can't Rule Out Liquidation of $54 Million Aave Loan

The Nasdaq-listed ether holder put its Aave debt at $54 million against $111 million of crypto. Its shares closed Friday at 65.3 cents, down 12.2%.

BTCS, a Nasdaq-listed company that holds ether, owed about $54 million on the Aave lending protocol as of Nov. 19 and said it can't promise the loan won't be liquidated, according to an 8-K filing with the SEC.

"We can provide no assurance as to the future amount of our borrowings, the value of our collateral, or that a liquidation will not occur," the company said in the statement, which it first posted on social media after investor questions.

What BTCS owes and owns

The filing lists about $111 million of crypto, valued at an ether price of $1,775, against the $54 million Aave debt and about $18 million of senior secured convertible notes (loans that can turn into shares). Ether traded at about $1,682 at 11:49 a.m. ET Saturday, roughly 5% below the price BTCS used, CoinGecko data show.

BTCS shares closed Friday at 65.3 cents, down 12.2%, Yahoo Finance data show. That gave the company a market value of about $34.6 million. CoinDesk also reported the filing and the share move.

The company said it borrows stablecoins against part of its ether to fund liquidity pools run through Imperium, its DeFi business. "We may increase borrowings when the value of our ETH collateral rises and reduce borrowings when it falls," it said.

How an Aave liquidation works

Aave gives every loan a health factor: collateral value times a liquidation threshold set by Aave governance, divided by the debt, per Aave's documentation. Below 1, the loan can be liquidated. An outside liquidator repays part of the debt and takes the same value of collateral plus a bonus. Up to half the debt can go in one step, or all of it if the health factor drops to 0.95 or lower.

The filing doesn't give the loan's health factor or the ether price that would trigger liquidation.

In our opinion: The number investors still don't have is the liquidation price, and that figure decides how much room BTCS has left. Ether already sits about 5% under the $1,775 the company used, and its $34.6 million market value is smaller than the $54 million it owes on Aave. The next update on that debt, in a filing or a post, is the one to read closely.
