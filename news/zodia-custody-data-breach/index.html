Zodia Custody Says Unauthorized Party Accessed Client Contacts

Standard Chartered's crypto custodian said it took its client API offline after detecting probing on Nov. 23 and has found no unauthorized transactions.

Zodia Custody, the crypto custodian owned by Standard Chartered, said Thursday that an unauthorized party accessed contact details for a small number of clients and employees on Nov. 24. It said no unauthorized transactions have been found so far.

Zodia has not reported any loss of client assets. Names and work email addresses can be used for phishing, where criminals pose as a trusted firm to trick staff into handing over logins or approving transfers.

Probing began on Nov. 23

Zodia detected attempts to probe its platform on Nov. 23, CEO Richard Clark said in a company statement. As a precaution, the firm took its B2B API offline, added security measures and then restored the service. Monitoring the next day found that an unauthorized party had accessed "limited client information."

That information consisted of publicly available organization details and, for a small number of clients and employees, a business contact name and email address, Clark said. "Based on our investigation to date, there have been no unauthorised transactions," he said. Zodia has contacted its clients and informed regulators, according to the statement and Finextra's summary of Reuters reporting.

What a custody API does

A custodian holds digital assets for funds, banks and companies and keeps the private keys that control them. An API, short for application programming interface, is the link that lets a client's own software connect directly to the custodian's platform. Taking it offline cuts that automated connection while engineers check for weak points.

Zodia did not say who was behind the incident or how the information was accessed, Decrypt reported. Standard Chartered recently raised its stake in Zodia to 100%, according to Finextra and CoinDesk.

In our opinion: The figure that carries this story is zero, the count of unauthorized transactions Zodia has found to date. The practical risk now sits with client staff, who could receive messages that look like they come from Zodia, so confirming any unexpected request through a known channel is sensible. The next thing to watch is whether Zodia or its regulators publish how the access happened.
