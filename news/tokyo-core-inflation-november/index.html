Tokyo Core Inflation Hits 2.9%, Fastest Pace Since July 2025

Tokyo core prices rose 2.9% in November from 2.6% in October as energy costs jumped 9.5%. The Bank of Japan meets Dec. 17-18.

Consumer prices in Tokyo excluding fresh food rose 2.9% in November from a year earlier, up from 2.6% in October, Japan's statistics bureau said on Friday local time. It was the fastest rise since July 2025, though slightly below the 3.0% median forecast in a Reuters poll, Reuters reported.

Power and Gas Bills Did the Lifting

Energy prices in the capital rose 9.5% from a year earlier, compared with 2.2% in October, according to the bureau's release. Electricity was up 9.5% and city gas 13.7%. Reuters said the jump came partly because government subsidies for power and gas bills ended.

Strip energy out as well, and the picture cools. The index excluding fresh food and energy, which the Bank of Japan watches as a gauge of underlying inflation, rose 2.5%, down from 2.6% in October. The all-items figure rose 2.8%.

Why Tokyo Numbers Get Watched

Tokyo's figures arrive ahead of the national data, and Reuters said they are widely regarded as a leading indicator (an early signal) for prices across Japan. Core inflation in the capital has now stayed above the BOJ's 2% target for three straight months, Reuters said.

The BOJ raised its policy rate to 1.25% in September and holds its next two-day meeting ending Dec. 18, according to Reuters. Why should crypto readers care? A higher Japanese rate makes it more expensive to borrow yen, the low-cost funding that some traders use to buy riskier assets abroad.

Bitcoin traded near $61,450 at 11:50 p.m. ET Thursday, up 0.8% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The headline went up, but the measure the BOJ cares about most slipped to 2.5%, so this report gives both sides of the December debate something to point to. The stake is the price of yen borrowing, which reaches well past Japan. The next number to watch is the national November inflation release, and then the Dec. 18 rate decision.
