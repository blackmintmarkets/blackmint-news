Hyperscale Data Flags Going-Concern Doubt Over $74.8M AI Build

The former bitcoin miner had $17.3 million in cash against $231.2 million in current liabilities on Sept. 30, its new quarterly filing shows.

Hyperscale Data (GPUS), a former bitcoin miner turning its Michigan site into an AI data center, said in a quarterly filing on Wednesday that it still needs about $74.8 million to finish the build, and that there is "substantial doubt" about its ability to continue as a going concern.

The Cash Gap

As of Sept. 30, the company had about $17.3 million in cash and cash equivalents and $27.8 million in restricted cash, against $231.2 million in current liabilities, the 10-Q shows. Current liabilities exceeded current assets by about $128.1 million. It reported a net loss of about $62.1 million for the first nine months of 2026. CoinDesk reported the same figures.

The project in Dowagiac, Michigan, is set to deliver about 20 megawatts of power capacity to an AI infrastructure customer. Hyperscale estimates the total cost at $122.1 million, with $47.3 million paid through Oct. 31. It expects to spend about $27.4 million in November and December and $47.4 million in the first quarter of 2027.

What a Going-Concern Warning Means

Auditing rules require management to say when it cannot show it has enough money to pay its bills for the next 12 months. The warning does not mean a company is shutting down. It means survival depends on raising money or selling assets. Hyperscale said it plans to use equity sales, debt, asset sales or other deals, but that those steps are not fully in its control.

Bitcoin Sold, Contract Terms

On Nov. 6 the company sold about 211.6 bitcoin for about $16 million and used part of the money to repay convertible notes, the filing says. CoinDesk reported that sale emptied its bitcoin holdings.

Hyperscale has cited a contract value of about $1.2 billion. The filing says that figure assumes the customer exercises two five-year extensions and that Hyperscale meets its obligations under the agreement.

In our opinion: The headline contract number is $1.2 billion, but the figure that decides this story is $74.8 million, due over the next few months. Miners moving into AI trade a volatile coin for big construction bills, and shareholders carry the funding risk in between. Watch for new financing filings before the first phase is due to go live.
