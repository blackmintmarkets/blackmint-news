NYSE Picks Avalanche Ledger to Clear Its Planned Tokenized Stock Venue

Avalanche will run the ledger behind the clearing firm for an NYSE-affiliated trading system planned for 24/7 trading. It still needs regulatory approval.

The New York Stock Exchange has picked Avalanche to supply the core ledger for the clearing firm behind its planned tokenized stock venue, Avalanche said in a blog post on Monday, Nov. 9. The NYSE flagged the deal in its pre-market update the same morning.

The venue will be an alternative trading system (ATS), a privately run market that trades securities under SEC rules without being a full exchange. Neither company gave a launch date, and the venue still needs regulators to sign off.

Where the blockchain sits

Trading a stock has two halves. First, an engine matches a buyer with a seller. Then comes clearing and settlement, the step where shares and cash actually change owners, with a clearing firm standing between the two sides until it's done.

That second half is the part moving onto Avalanche. The NYSE's January announcement from parent Intercontinental Exchange said the platform would pair its Pillar matching engine with blockchain-based post-trade systems, aiming for 24/7 trading, instant settlement, orders sized in dollars and funding in stablecoins. It also said tokenized shareholders would keep dividends and voting rights.

According to Avalanche, Dennis Lee, the NYSE's head of digital issuance, said the settlement ledger had to be fast, reliable, predictable in cost and able to control who takes part. Decrypt and CoinDesk also reported that the venue is designed to accept stocks issued on other blockchains.

What the posts leave out

Neither the Avalanche post nor the NYSE update gives fees, the name of the clearing firm, trading volumes or a timetable. AVAX, Avalanche's token, traded at $8.73 at 11:55 a.m. ET, up 6.9% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: the bigger story is which layer moved. Tokenized funds such as BlackRock's BUIDL already sit on Avalanche, Avalanche says, but this puts a blockchain under the step where ownership changes hands for an NYSE venue. The document worth waiting for is the regulatory filing, because that's where fees, the clearing firm and a start date have to show up.
