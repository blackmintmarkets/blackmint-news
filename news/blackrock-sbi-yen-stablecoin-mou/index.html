BlackRock and SBI Study Linking Tokenized Funds to a Yen Stablecoin

BlackRock signed an MOU with SBI and Startale to explore pairing tokenized money market funds with JPYSC, a $131 million yen stablecoin. No launch date was set.

BlackRock wants to see whether its tokenized money market funds can trade against a $131 million yen stablecoin. The asset manager signed a memorandum of understanding with SBI Holdings and Startale Group, the companies announced Tuesday in Tokyo, Monday evening ET.

The target is JPYSC, a yen stablecoin issued through SBI Shinsei Trust Bank. Its market value was about $131.3 million near midnight ET, per CoinGecko. BlackRock's dollar fund BUIDL was near $2.18 billion.

How the pairing would work

A money market fund holds short-term, low-risk debt. A tokenized version puts fund shares on a blockchain. A stablecoin can act as the cash side, so an investor could move between a fund that pays yield and a token used for payments without waiting on bank wires.

The partners will study three areas: technology, business and distribution, and regulation. SBI's release and Startale said the effort is open to other banks and businesses in Japan and abroad.

"The shift to onchain finance is becoming an unavoidable global trend," SBI Chairman Yoshitaka Kitao said in the announcement.

Japan set legal rules for stablecoins through amendments to its Payment Services Act, and the companies said the next step is building the infrastructure, distribution channels and regulatory frameworks that would connect tokenized assets with those coins at scale.

What's missing

The MOU is an agreement to explore.

It names no product, fees or timeline, and no JPYSC integration has been confirmed.

In our opinion: an MOU is a promise to keep talking, and this one comes without a calendar. Watch who else signs up, since the door was left open for other banks.
