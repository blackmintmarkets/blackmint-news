Kalshi Faces Monday Hearing on NY Order After Judge Denies CFTC Bid

A state judge hears New York's restraining-order request at 11:30 a.m. ET. Kalshi says the wording could close its exchange everywhere; the state says it won't.

A federal judge on Friday denied the CFTC's second emergency request to stop New York's case against Kalshi. That leaves a state judge to hear New York's restraining-order bid at 11:30 a.m. ET Monday.

U.S. District Judge Lorna Schofield ruled after a telephone hearing Friday evening, The Block and Gambling Insider reported. Gambling Insider said she found the agency had not shown a strong chance of winning its argument that federal commodities law overrides state gambling rules; The Block cited a letter from James' office describing the same finding.

Why the scope matters

Kalshi is headquartered in New York. The state's proposed order would bar it from operating within or from New York, both outlets reported. The Block said it also covers contracts offered to New Yorkers.

Kalshi's lawyers told the state court that because of that wording, the order would probably force all open positions to be closed, ABC News reported. A CFTC lawyer told Schofield the order would shut Kalshi down nationwide. New York's attorney general's office replied that it is not seeking a nationwide shutdown: Kalshi could still offer lawful futures and swaps, it said.

The state's case

Attorney General Letitia James sued Kalshi in July. She alleges it breaks state laws that bar betting on games involving New York college teams and sports betting by anyone under 21, ABC reported. Kalshi says its contracts are derivatives under the CFTC's exclusive oversight, so state gambling laws don't apply.

How the fight works

A temporary restraining order is a short court order that freezes conduct until a fuller hearing. Kalshi is a designated contract market, an exchange registered with the CFTC. The dispute turns on preemption, the question of whether a federal law, here the Commodity Exchange Act, overrides state rules.

Federal appeals courts have split on that question. Three have ruled against Kalshi and one in its favor, according to Gambling Insider and The Block. Justice Melissa Crane of the New York County Supreme Court will hear Monday's request.

In our opinion: Monday's hearing turns on two small words, "from New York," and a lot of open positions ride on them. If the order reaches past state lines, customers in every state carry the risk; if it covers only New Yorkers, the state gets its rules and Kalshi keeps running elsewhere. Watch the scope Justice Crane writes, then whether either side files an appeal.
