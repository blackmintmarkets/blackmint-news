Hyperliquid Strategies Files to Become a CFTC FCM

Q3 net income hit $620.6 million on unrealized HYPE gains. The firm holds about 38.5 million HYPE and says approval isn't assured.

Hyperliquid Strategies filed with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission to become a futures commission merchant, and reported $620.6 million of third-quarter net income, the Nasdaq-listed HYPE treasury firm said Thursday.

The FCM application, disclosed in SEC earnings materials, would let the firm carry U.S. client accounts for permissioned perpetual futures markets on Hyperliquid infrastructure, pending approval. For the quarter ended Sept. 30 it also booked about $13.3 million of interest plus staking and validator commission revenue. Net income was driven mainly by an $803 million unrealized gain on HYPE holdings, the presentation shows.

What an FCM does

A futures commission merchant is a CFTC-registered firm that holds customer funds and executes futures or swaps for clients. Hyperliquid Strategies says Hyperliquid's HIP-3 design lets independent deployers launch permissioned markets on the chain while the chain stays a neutral layer. The firm said an approved FCM role would let it "play a role" in bringing perpetual futures to U.S. customers inside that framework. It also said there is no assurance on timing or approval.

The Block reported the filing and the quarterly figures on Thursday. As of Sept. 30 the company held about 38.5 million HYPE tokens and $255 million of cash and cash-like instruments, with no debt, the SEC exhibit says. HYPE traded near $77 at about 6 p.m. ET Thursday, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: an application is not a license. The question worth chasing is whether CFTC registration closes before other regulated stacks tied to Hyperliquid try the same path. The next document to watch is the agency's response to the FCM filing.
