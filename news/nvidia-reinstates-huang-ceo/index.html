Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO; Nasdaq Rises 6.4%

The board reversed a Nov. 17 statement that it did not intend to bring him back. Bitcoin rose to about $63,700 as the news hit.

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive Monday, effective immediately, the company said. The Nasdaq Composite was up 6.4% at 12:52 p.m. ET, per Yahoo Finance, after falling 10% last week. CNBC showed the index up 6.3%, at 24,053.11, at 12:51 p.m.

Nvidia shares traded at $163.84 at 12:52 p.m. ET, up $38.31, or 30.5%, from Friday's close of $125.53, according to CNBC. Yahoo Finance showed $163.83 at the same minute. An 8-K filed Monday said the board took the action on Sunday, Nov. 22.

How the board reversed course

Nvidia announced on Nov. 12 that Huang had resigned as president, CEO and a director, and that chief financial officer Colette Kress would serve as interim CEO. An 8-K listed Nov. 11 as the date the board accepted the resignation.

Board chair Harvey C. Jones told investors on the Nov. 17 earnings call that Huang "voluntarily resigned" after disclosure of "personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms," and that "the Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," NPR and The Verge reported.

Monday's release said the board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang after meetings that left it "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead NVIDIA." It said the board "received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk."

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Jones said in the release. Huang said he was taking the role "with the greatest humility and renewed commitment." Kress will continue as CFO, and Jones will remain board chair.

What led to the resignation

News reports in late October and early November described extramarital relationships involving Huang, including one with a woman whom U.S. officials have alleged was working with Chinese intelligence. The Associated Press and Reuters have reported those accounts. The reports have not been proven in court. Nvidia's statement refers to "recent reporting that Mr. Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort."

On Nov. 17, Huang said in a social media statement that he "did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA" and had "made serious mistakes in my personal life," NPR and The Verge reported. He also said he would be honored to return if the board asked him.

The board said an independent-director committee's investigation "will proceed to conclusion," and that Huang has committed to cooperate. It also said it had formed a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors, is searching for a chief compliance officer, and will continue to cooperate with government investigations and inquiries.

President Donald Trump said in a social media post that the board had made the "correct and proper decision," Reuters and Politico reported.

The market was already attempting a rebound

The rebound did not start with Monday's announcement. Nasdaq 100 futures were up 3.3% by 3:51 a.m. ET, Reuters reported, after South Korea readied a stock stabilization fund, Taiwan limited some short sales, and China cut a margin requirement, as Black Mint wrote earlier Monday.

Last week the Nasdaq Composite fell 10.02%, or 2,518.54 points, to 22,617.73, the largest one-week percentage drop since the week ending March 20, 2020, Dow Jones Market Data said. On Friday the Nasdaq fell 5.28%, the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.93% to 48,147.33, according to Investing.com and the prior closes carried by Yahoo Finance and CNBC.

Nvidia closed Friday at $125.53, down 14% on the day and 32.7% below its Nov. 6 close of $186.64, the last session before the Nov. 8 weekend reports, per Yahoo Finance and Quantellect. Monday's 12:52 p.m. price was still about 12% below that Nov. 6 close.

What happened after the 9 a.m. release

Nvidia issued its statement at 9 a.m. ET, Unchained reported. At 9:43 a.m., the Dow was up 1,059.73 points, or 2.20%, at 49,208.04, the S&P 500 was up 278 points, or 4.04%, at 7,188.39, and the Nasdaq Composite was up 1,328.21 points, or 5.87%, at 23,945.94, Reuters reported. Nvidia was up more than 28% at that time. Advanced Micro Devices was up 6%, Micron Technology was up 13.7% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was up 12.5%. CoreWeave was up 25%, IREN was up 28% and Nebius was up 24.4%, Reuters said.

By 10:26 a.m., the Nasdaq's gain had extended to 7.6%, or about 1,717 points, at 24,335.01, per CNBC, with Yahoo Finance showing 24,339.57 at 10:32 a.m. Those gains later eased. At 12:52 p.m. ET, Yahoo Finance had the Nasdaq up 6.4% at 24,069.96, the S&P 500 up 4.6% at 7,228.27 and the Dow up 2.9% at 49,561.43. CNBC showed similar levels at 12:51 p.m. to 12:52 p.m.: the Nasdaq up 6.3% at 24,053.11, the S&P 500 up 4.6% at 7,226.39 and the Dow up 2.9% at 49,553.91.

Bitcoin moved with the news

Bitcoin's rebound began before the announcement. It bottomed near $55,700 early Saturday, Unchained said, matching CoinGecko data that shows a low of about $55,711 at 1 a.m. ET Saturday. It was already near $61,200 by 8 a.m. ET Monday, Unchained said.

CoinGecko's five-minute data, as reviewed by Unchained, showed bitcoin rising about 4% from roughly $61,100 at 8:55 a.m. ET to roughly $63,700 at 9 a.m., when Nvidia put out the release. CoinGecko's public chart showed a high of about $63,697 at 9 a.m. ET. Bitcoin later gave back that jump and traded near $61,700 at 12:52 p.m. ET, per Yahoo Finance and Coinbase.

The open investigation and the PCE report

Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter are scheduled to answer questions behind closed doors from the House Foreign Affairs and Intelligence Committees and the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party on Dec. 1 and on Dec. 2, NPR reported. The Verge has reported the same dates.

The board's investigation is still open. October core PCE inflation and revised third-quarter GDP are due Wednesday, a test for the same rate-sensitive stocks and for bitcoin, as Black Mint noted Monday morning.

In our opinion: the board put Huang back in the job and said an independent investigation will still run to its conclusion. That inquiry, and Huang's closed-door House meetings on Dec. 1 and 2, are the next company-specific events. Wednesday's core PCE report is the next scheduled test for the same stocks that recouped a large part of last week's drop, and for bitcoin, which rose with the 9 a.m. news after already climbing from Saturday's low.
