Anthropic Prices Record IPO at $83 a Share, Raising $81.3 Billion

The Claude maker sold 980 million shares above its $70-$78 range, valuing it near $1.5 trillion. Trading on Nasdaq as ANT is set for Thursday.

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday, raising about $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record, the company and CNBC said.

The shares are expected to start trading Thursday on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker "ANT," and the offering is expected to close on Nov. 20, according to Anthropic's statement.

Priced Above the Range

The company had marketed the shares at $70 to $78 and planned to sell 825 million, CNBC and TechCrunch reported. It instead sold 980 million. Of those, 956,351,354 came from Anthropic and 23,648,646 from selling stockholders and Anthropic receives no money from their sales.

At $83, Anthropic is valued at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, AP and CNBC reported. The previous record belonged to SpaceX, which raised $75 billion in its June IPO before underwriters bought extra shares, CNBC and TechCrunch said.

How IPO Pricing Works

Banks running an IPO collect orders from big investors before the listing, then set one price for everyone. Pricing above the marketed range means demand ran ahead of supply. Underwriters here also hold a 30-day option to buy up to 147 million more shares, a tool used to steady trading in the first weeks.

The Crypto Backdrop

The deal lands in a weak week for digital assets. Bitcoin traded at $63,487 at 11:47 p.m. ET Wednesday, down 1.3% in 24 hours, per CoinGecko. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had net outflows of $157.8 million on Nov. 18, according to Farside Investors.

Anthropic reported $33.24 billion in revenue for the nine months through Sept. 30 and a $104 billion net loss, mostly from fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives, CNBC reported from the prospectus.

In our opinion: An $81 billion check is a lot of risk appetite pointed at one ticker, in the same week bitcoin funds saw money leave. It shows where big investors are placing bets right now. Thursday's first trade on Nasdaq is the number to watch, and the underwriters' 147 million-share option will show whether demand holds past day one.
