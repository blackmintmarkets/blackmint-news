South Korea to Release Draft Digital Asset Law Tuesday, Reports Say

Reports say the draft could cap big shareholders' exchange stakes, favor bank-led won stablecoins and make custody its own licensed business.

South Korea's Financial Services Commission plans to release the main points of its draft Digital Asset Basic Act on Tuesday, Nov. 24, the Digital Times reported. Yonhap also said the draft is due this week.

The draft would put exchanges, custodians and won-pegged stablecoins under one licensing law. It could also cap ownership of the largest exchanges.

Ownership caps and bank-led stablecoins

The Digital Times said the regulator will first take the bill to its Virtual Asset Committee, a statutory advisory body. A bloomingbit calendar lists the release for 3 p.m. Korea time (1 a.m. ET Tuesday). Both outlets said officials are expected to meet the heads of the five won-trading exchanges, including Upbit and Bithumb, first.

Measures under discussion, per both reports:

  • a cap on how much of an exchange its largest shareholders can own, with a grace period for current operators, or a limit on their voting rights instead
  • won stablecoins (tokens pegged to one won) issued mainly by bank-led groups, the model the Bank of Korea has favored
  • custody, meaning holding customers' coins, split out as its own licensed business
  • liability for customer losses from hacks or system failures, even when the firm was not negligent

How the rules work today

Korean crypto firms now answer to two laws. One requires a filing with regulators and anti-money-laundering checks. The other covers customer asset safety and unfair trading.

The new act would define digital assets in law. Capital, staffing and security requirements would vary by business type, and the papers said some types are likely to need full authorization instead of a simple filing.

Disagreements ahead

The commission has not published the text, so these points are reported, not final. Both outlets said industry and government agencies still disagree on the ownership cap and on stablecoin issuers. The draft is expected to reach the National Assembly as a lawmaker-sponsored bill and be reviewed with digital asset bills from ruling and opposition members.

In our opinion: Korea has policed crypto through money-laundering and user-protection rules; this draft would also decide who owns an exchange and who issues a won stablecoin. Founders and large shareholders of the big exchanges carry the most risk if a stake cap survives, though a grace period is reportedly on the table. The number to look for on Tuesday is the cap itself, and whether it limits shares or only votes.
