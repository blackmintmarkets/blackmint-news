HSBC's RedCoin Stablecoin Passes 100,000 Users in First Month

The Hong Kong dollar token has about HK$592.8 million in circulation, most of it added in a single week in mid-November, HSBC's own reports show.

HSBC's Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin, had more than 100,000 users in its first month, the bank said Thursday, as tokens in circulation stood at about HK$592.8 million (roughly $76 million).

HSBC Hong Kong chief executive Maggie Ng said the figure "shows that interest in HSBC RedCoin is translating into everyday use in the real economy," according to the bank's statement as reported by the South China Morning Post and The Standard. The statement, as reported, did not say how many of those users are active.

How the coin is backed

A stablecoin is a token meant to hold a fixed value, here one Hong Kong dollar, by keeping matching reserves. HSBC issues RedCoin under a stablecoin licence from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, and each token can be redeemed one-for-one for Hong Kong dollars, its RedCoin page says. The reserves are held in Hong Kong dollar cash.

RedCoin launched in October inside PayMe, HSBC's payment app, which has more than 3.3 million users, SCMP reported. Users can send it to friends or pay at more than 100,000 merchants. Eligible customers can also use it in HSBC's banking app to buy a tokenized class of a Hong Kong dollar money market fund.

One week drove most of the supply

HSBC's unaudited weekly statements show circulation of HK$83.2 million on Oct. 28 and HK$91.3 million on Nov. 11. It rose to HK$591.5 million by Nov. 18, an increase of 547.68%, according to the Nov. 18 statement. The statement does not give a reason for the jump. The daily figure for Nov. 25 shows a seven-day gain of about HK$1.3 million.

Reserves have exceeded circulation by HK$1 million in each statement. HSBC says its first independent attestation, an outside auditor's check of the reserves, is expected after the fourth quarter of 2026.

Ng said expansion into "broader wealth management and wholesale use cases will proceed in stages."

In our opinion: The user count measures reach. The balance was built by the roughly HK$500 million that arrived in the week to Nov. 18, and HSBC's statements do not say where it came from. That leaves the first independent attestation, due after the fourth quarter, as the document to read when it lands.
