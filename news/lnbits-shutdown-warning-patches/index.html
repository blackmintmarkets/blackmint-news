LNbits Tells Every Server Operator to Shut Down Pending Patches

The Lightning account system's developers say fixes should land in 24 to 48 hours. LNbits has not named the flaw or said how many servers were hit.

LNbits told everyone running its software to stop their servers immediately on Sunday, saying a wave of attacks had left it with security patches still to push. Contributor Ben Arc said the fixes should be merged and available within 24 to 48 hours.

The project's 8:56 a.m. ET post on X asked users to stop servers "IMMEDIATELY" and said, "it's better to err on the side of caution." Arc added three minutes later that operators should pass the warning on to anyone who might be running an instance. At 12:18 p.m. ET, LNbits said it had switched off every instance on its own hosted service as a precaution and expected services to resume within 24 hours.

From one extension to every server

The warning widened through the morning. At 7:33 a.m. ET, LNbits had asked operators with its Paywall extension enabled to turn it off while it investigated "a potential bug." The 8:56 a.m. post covered every server, with or without that extension.

How LNbits works

LNbits is open-source software that sits on top of a Lightning node and lets one operator give separate wallets to many users, with add-on extensions for things like paywalls and tipping. Because each server is connected to a node holding real bitcoin, a flaw in LNbits can reach that money. That's why the project is asking operators to stop servers rather than wait for a patch.

A loss one operator reported

Kevin Ravensberg, who runs the LNbits server behind the BitTipBot Telegram tipping bot, said newly created accounts withdrew 2,285,545 sats, about 0.023 bitcoin, from his server on Sunday before he stopped it, Bitcoin.com News reported. It said he plans to cover the bot's users himself. We could not independently confirm the figure.

What isn't known

LNbits has not said what the vulnerabilities are, whether the Paywall bug is among them, how many servers were attacked, or how much was taken in total. Its latest tagged release on GitHub is v1.6.2 from Sept. 18, and no new security advisory was listed when we checked at 6 p.m. ET.

Operators who stopped their servers should keep them off until a fixed release is published and LNbits says it is safe to restart.
