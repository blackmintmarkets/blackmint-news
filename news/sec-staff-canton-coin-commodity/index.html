SEC Staff Call Canton Coin a 'Digital Commodity' in No-Action Letter

SEC staff say Canton Coin is a digital commodity and won't push enforcement over how it's minted, as long as the facts Digital Asset gave them hold.

Canton Coin, a token with a market value near $3.9 billion, now has a signed letter from SEC staff calling it a "digital commodity."

The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance said Tuesday in a no-action letter that, relying on what it was told, Canton Coin fits the "digital commodity" label in the Commission's March 2026 crypto interpretation. The staff also said it won't recommend enforcement if the coin keeps being minted as described without registering under the Securities Act or Exchange Act.

The letter, signed by Deputy Chief Counsel Jonathan Ingram, answers an Oct. 30 request filed on behalf of Digital Asset Holdings, the company that created the Canton protocol.

What the staff leaned on

Two points. The staff said the coin derives its value "from the programmatic operation of the Canton Network, as well as supply and demand dynamics, rather than from the expectation of profits from the essential managerial efforts of others." And it said the network has no "central party."

How Canton Coin works

Per the request letter, the coin pays fees on Canton's shared transaction layer, the Global Synchronizer. Fees are set in U.S. dollars and the coin used to pay them is burned, so heavy use shrinks supply. New coins appear only as rewards to network operators and apps based on activity. The letter says there was no pre-launch sale or allocation.

Control is the other test. As of Sept. 30, the letter counts about 60 Super Validators, but only 13 node operators vote, one vote each, with two-thirds needed for changes. Digital Asset runs two of those 13 nodes.

The fine print

The staff wrote that "any different facts or conditions might require the Division to reach different conclusions," and that the response "does not express any legal conclusion on the issues presented." It is a staff view. Neither the Commission nor a court has ruled.

The coin was up about 3% over 24 hours at 5:55 p.m. ET, per CoinGecko. It came a day after the division updated its FAQs on that interpretation.

In our opinion: A no-action letter is a permission slip where the fine print is basically the whole slip, and Canton's holds only while 13 node operators keep splitting the votes. It matters beyond one token, because it shows which facts the staff weighs: no presale, burned fees and no single hand on the wheel. Watch for copycat requests, and for whether Digital Asset's two-node share ever grows.
