OCC Urges Community Banks to Consider Issuing Stablecoins

A new OCC fact sheet answers small banks' fears about deposit flight and yield, and invites them to apply as issuers before the GENIUS Act rule starts Jan. 18.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency wants community banks to consider issuing stablecoins before its GENIUS Act rule starts Jan. 18, 2027. A fact sheet reported Monday says interested banks, or their subsidiaries, are "encouraged to consider applying" to become permitted payment stablecoin issuers.

The start date comes from Bulletin 2026-50, issued Oct. 21. The stablecoin market it governs held about $303.5 billion on Monday, per DefiLlama.

The banks' worries, in the OCC's own list

The document is built as concerns and answers. Top of the list is deposit flight, the fear that customers move savings into stablecoins. The OCC says bank deposits can count as reserves backing a stablecoin, and that the effect on deposits is "uncertain, and estimates vary widely." It says it is watching for outflows.

On yield, the final rule dropped a proposed test (a "rebuttable presumption") for when a reward counts as banned interest. It now restates the law's ban on paying interest or yield to holders.

Smaller issuers get longer exam cycles

Large issuers face a full exam every 12 months. Some small issuers qualify for an 18- to 24-month cycle.

The fact sheet doesn't say how many community banks have shown interest or applied. Issuers must also tell customers that stablecoins are not FDIC-insured or backed by the government.

In our opinion: the question worth chasing is whether any small bank files an application before Jan. 18.
