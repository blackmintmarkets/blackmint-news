Anthropic Closes Day One at $97.17, 17% Above Its $83 IPO Price

The Claude maker opened at $111.50 before giving back part of the gain. Its $81.3 billion offering is the largest IPO on record.

Anthropic shares closed at $97.17 in their Nasdaq debut Thursday, 17% above the $83 offer price, after opening at $111.50, CNBC and AP reported.

The AI company, which trades under the ticker ANT, raised $81.3 billion by selling 980 million shares. That tops SpaceX's record of about $75 billion from June, AP said.

A $14 Slide From the Open

At the opening price, the company was valued at about $2.01 trillion, according to AP. By the close, its fully diluted market value was roughly $1.8 trillion, CNBC reported. That ranks it behind only Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, Amazon and SpaceX among U.S. companies, per CNBC.

More than 846 million shares changed hands, CNBC said, compared with 522 million on SpaceX's first day.

How an IPO price and an opening price differ

The $83 price was set Wednesday night by the company and its banks. Only investors who got an allocation, mostly institutions, paid it. The opening price comes later, from an auction on the exchange that matches all the buy and sell orders waiting for the first trade.

So the gap matters. Shares bought at $83 closed up about 17%. Shares bought at the $111.50 open closed down about 13%, by our math.

Why crypto traders noticed

Bitcoin was little changed on the day. It traded near $62,196 at 5:47 p.m. ET, down about 0.3% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

The deal also reached the Federal Reserve's debate. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on a call with reporters that what early holders do with their new access to cash "is going to become a macro-relevant statistic," Reuters reported. More money spent can mean more pressure on prices, and that feeds into rate decisions.

In our opinion: the first day handed out two very different report cards, depending on what time investors showed up. The bigger number is the paper wealth now sitting with early holders, which the Fed says it is watching. The next dates that matter are the lockup expiries, when insiders can first sell, and the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting.
