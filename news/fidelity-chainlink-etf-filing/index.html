Fidelity Files for Spot Chainlink ETF That Would Skip Staking

The proposed Fidelity Chainlink Fund would hold LINK through three custodians and list on Nasdaq as FLNK, but its holders would get no staking rewards.

Fidelity filed on Wednesday to launch a spot Chainlink exchange-traded fund, according to a Form S-1 registration statement posted on the SEC's EDGAR system. The Fidelity Chainlink Fund would hold LINK directly with three custodians and is expected to trade on Nasdaq.

LINK traded at $9.02 at 5:56 p.m. ET, up 1.7% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

How the fund would work

An S-1 is the form a company files to register shares it plans to sell to the public. A spot ETF buys the asset itself, not futures contracts. LINK is the token of Chainlink, an oracle network, meaning a system that feeds outside data such as asset prices into smart contracts, the self-running programs on blockchains. The filing says independent node operators deliver that data, and LINK is the standard unit of payment for their services.

The fund would not stake, which means locking tokens to help secure the network in return for rewards. "The Trust will not stake its LINK, and Shareholders will not receive staking rewards through the Trust," the filing says. It adds that Chainlink staking is capped at about 4% of total supply.

Who holds the tokens

Anchorage Digital Bank, BitGo Bank & Trust and Coinbase Custody would split custody of the fund's LINK. The sponsor is FD Funds Management, a wholly owned unit of FMR LLC, Fidelity's parent. The Block also reported the filing and the three custodians.

Here is the money trail: the fund would pay the sponsor one annual fee, taken as a percentage of its LINK holdings, and that fee covers other service costs. The percentage is still blank in the draft. Shareholders would get LINK's price moves, minus that fee.

Still a draft

The filing lists "FLNK" as the expected ticker in brackets, as it does with other details still to be filled in, such as the fee rate and the transfer agent's name. Trading also depends on approval of a Nasdaq application to amend its listing rules, the filing says.

In our opinion: Fidelity wants LINK inside a brokerage account but is leaving the staking yield on the table, a trade that keeps the product simple and slightly less generous to holders. Buyers would carry all of LINK's price risk, while the sponsor earns its fee on whatever assets arrive. Watch Nasdaq's listing-rule application, because until it clears, FLNK is a ticker in brackets.
