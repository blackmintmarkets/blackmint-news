South Korea Leans Toward a Fourth Delay of Its 22% Crypto Tax

Seoul is weighing another delay to its tax on crypto gains, citing gaps in overseas exchange data. A ruling-party bill would push the start to 2029.

South Korea's presidential office is leaning toward pushing back its crypto income tax a fourth time, Korean outlets reported Sunday, citing ruling-party sources. The 22% tax is set to start Jan. 1, 2027.

Officially, nothing is settled. The presidential office said a delay is "under review" and that no decision has been made, Yonhap reported.

For Korean traders the stakes are simple. The tax would take 22%, including local income tax, of yearly crypto gains above a 2.5 million won deduction, according to The Asia Business Daily and Financial News.

Korea's crypto tax has been "next year" since 2022

It was first due to start in 2022 and has been delayed three times since. The third date didn't stick either.

Foreign exchanges are the sticking point

The main worry is data from overseas platforms. The U.S. isn't expected to join the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, a deal for tax authorities to swap crypto account data, until 2029, Seoul Economic Daily and The Asia Business Daily reported. Officials fear the tax would land mostly on people who trade on domestic exchanges.

Both big parties want more time. Democratic Party lawmaker Park Min-gyu has filed a bill that would move the start to 2029, and the opposition People Power Party also backs a delay, both outlets reported. A final call is expected after talks between the government and the ruling party.

In our opinion: a tax first scheduled for 2022 may now show up in 2029. Korean investors have heard "next year" before.
