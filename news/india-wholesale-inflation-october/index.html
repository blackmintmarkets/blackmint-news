India's Wholesale Inflation Hits 12.29% as Fuel Prices Jump 32.8%

Crude oil and natural gas prices were up 70.8% from a year earlier. Economists in a Reuters poll had expected 12.1% for the headline rate.

India's wholesale prices rose 12.29% in October from a year earlier, up from 11.39% in September, the Ministry of Commerce and Industry said Monday. Economists in a Reuters poll had expected 12.1%.

Energy did most of the work. Fuel and power inflation climbed to 32.84% from 25.85%, and crude petroleum and natural gas prices were up 70.80% on the year, the ministry's figures show. Fuel and power made up roughly nine-tenths of the index's 0.71% monthly rise, Moneycontrol calculated.

What the wholesale index measures

The Wholesale Price Index tracks what businesses pay for goods in bulk, before retail markups, so fuel and metal costs show up there first. It is a year-on-year measure. Last October the index was falling, at minus 1.18%, Mint reported, which makes this year's jump look larger. Mint also said 12.29% is the highest reading in the series that uses 2022-23 as its base year.

Not every line rose

  • Mineral oils: 45.57%, from 40.02%
  • Electricity: 8.06%, after a 0.43% decline in September
  • Manufactured products: 9.54%, down from 9.76%
  • WPI food index: 8.58%, down from 8.66%

Basic metals rose 17.21% and chemicals 17.53%, Hindustan Times and the Times of India reported. The October numbers are provisional, built on an 81.1% response rate, so they can be revised.

Why rates are in play

The Reserve Bank of India sets rates to keep retail inflation near a 4% target, with a 2-point band on either side. The Economic Times reported that the data has added to expectations of a quarter-point rate rise at the RBI's December meeting.

Bitcoin was near $64,450 at 5:53 a.m. ET, down 1.6% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: a 70.8% jump in crude and gas costs is the load Indian businesses are carrying, and the miss against the Reuters poll was small. The real test comes at the RBI's December meeting, where the Economic Times says a quarter-point rise is now expected. Before then, watch whether retail prices follow wholesale ones, because retail inflation is the gauge the RBI targets.
