Bitcoin and Ether ETFs Shed at Least $1.47 Billion Last Week

Bitcoin funds lost $1.25 billion, up from $1.07 billion a week earlier, and the total could change: IBIT's Friday flow is still blank in Farside's table.

U.S. spot bitcoin and ether exchange-traded funds posted at least $1.47 billion in net outflows in the five trading days through Friday, Nov. 20, Farside Investors data show.

Bitcoin funds accounted for $1.25 billion of that and ether funds $221.7 million. The total is a floor: Farside's Friday entries for BlackRock's IBIT and ETHA, plus ether funds ETHB and FETH, were still blank late Saturday.

How a fund outflow works

A spot ETF holds the actual coins. When investors sell more shares than they buy, large brokers called authorized participants hand shares back to the fund and take cash or coins out, which shrinks its holdings. Farside's daily figure is the net of those creations and redemptions, in dollars.

IBIT led the bitcoin exits

Thursday was the largest day for both groups. Bitcoin funds lost $582.3 million, including $541.1 million from IBIT, while ether funds lost $131.2 million, $124.2 million of it from ETHA.

Monday was the only positive bitcoin day, a $7.9 million net inflow as IBIT took in $76.5 million. Fidelity's FBTC lost $198.9 million over the week, and Grayscale's GBTC and its Bitcoin Mini Trust lost $148.2 million combined. Morgan Stanley's MSBT took in a net $53.3 million.

Bigger than the week before

Bitcoin fund outflows were up from $1.07 billion in the Nov. 9-13 week, when all five sessions were negative. Ether fund outflows fell from $398.9 million. Block Stream Media tallied the same $1.47 billion from Farside's tables.

Bitcoin traded at $59,225 at 11:52 p.m. ET Saturday, up 5.0% in 24 hours, and ether at $1,660.50, up 6.2%, CoinGecko data show. Farside's table still shows bitcoin funds with $53.46 billion in net inflows since they launched in January 2024.

In our opinion: Monday was the good day, and it covered less than 1% of what left by Friday. The cell to watch is IBIT's blank Friday entry, since BlackRock's fund drove most of the week's bitcoin exits. Two weeks of bitcoin outflows now add up to about $2.3 billion, still a small slice of the $53.46 billion that has come in since launch. Thanksgiving closes U.S. markets Thursday, so next week brings fewer sessions to change the trend.
