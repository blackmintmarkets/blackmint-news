Japan's Economy Shrinks 0.1% as High Prices Squeeze Shoppers

Economists polled by Reuters expected 0.3% growth. Consumer spending fell 0.3% for a second straight quarter, a month before the Bank of Japan meets Dec. 17-18.

Japan's economy shrank at an annualized 0.1% in July through September, the Cabinet Office said Monday, its first contraction in four quarters. Economists in a Reuters poll had expected 0.3% growth.

The miss lands a month before the Bank of Japan's Dec. 17-18 policy meeting, and CNBC reported the data could complicate the central bank's plans to keep raising rates.

Households cut back for a second quarter

Private consumption, more than half of Japan's output, fell 0.3% from April-June. That is its second straight quarterly drop. Kyodo News pointed to high prices, a magnitude-7.1 earthquake in Kumamoto in late July and heavy August rain, while CNBC reported that household energy subsidies expired in September.

Trade pulled the number down too. Imports rose 2.5% and exports fell 0.2%, so net exports cut 0.6 percentage point from growth, the Cabinet Office tables show. Business investment rose 1.6%.

Why a tiny drop reads as 0.1%

The quarter-on-quarter change was small: real GDP slipped 0.02%. "Real" means adjusted for inflation, and in plain yen terms the economy grew 0.4% on the quarter. "Annualized" stretches one quarter's change into the pace it would hit over a full year. The Cabinet Office will revise these figures in a second estimate that folds in fiscal 2025 annual data.

Economists split on a December hike

"Today's GDP data is hardly a green light for the BOJ to hike in December," State Street Investment Management economist Krishna Bhimavarapu said in comments reported by CNBC. He pointed to a 2.1% annualized fall in real final sales, a measure of demand that strips out inventories. Capital Economics' Marcel Thieliant still expects a hike, CNBC reported.

Daiwa Institute of Research economist Hirohito Hatanaka told Kyodo that any December hike is unlikely to win a unanimous vote.

Bitcoin was near $64,840 at 11:46 p.m. ET Sunday, up 0.1% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: the 0.1% headline is the small number. The one worth tracking is the 2.1% slide in final sales, because that is the evidence the BOJ board will weigh against inflation on Dec. 18. If the vote splits, the dissents will say more about Japan's next hike than the decision itself.
