Supreme Court Rejects Custodia's Bid for a Fed Master Account

The justices gave no reason and noted no dissent, leaving in place a 2-1 appeals ruling that lets regional Fed banks turn down eligible banks.

The U.S. Supreme Court on Monday declined to hear Custodia Bank's appeal, ending the Wyoming crypto bank's fight for a Federal Reserve master account that began with its October 2020 application.

The justices gave no explanation, and no dissent was recorded, Quartz reported, citing Bloomberg. The order leaves standing an Oct. 31, 2025, ruling by the 10th Circuit Court of Appeals, which held 2-1 that the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City had discretion to reject Custodia's application. The Supreme Court did not rule on the merits, and the denial ends the case.

What a master account does

A master account is a bank's own account at a regional Fed bank. It lets the bank settle payments directly with the Fed and use systems such as Fedwire, which moves large dollar transfers between banks. A bank without one has to route money through a correspondent bank, a larger bank that holds funds and moves them on its behalf.

Custodia applied under a Wyoming special purpose depository institution charter, which lets it offer digital-asset custody. The Kansas City Fed denied the application in January 2023, citing the bank's concentration in crypto-related activities. A federal trial court in Wyoming sided with the Fed in March 2024.

Both sides' arguments

Custodia argued that federal law entitles eligible banks to have their applications approved. "Congress did not intend for access to the nation's banking system to depend on unaccountable regional Fed banks' whim," the bank told the court, Quartz reported, citing Bloomberg. The Trump administration urged the justices not to take the case, arguing the Federal Reserve Act gives reserve banks discretion. Black Mint found no public statement from Custodia on Monday's order as of noon ET.

The Fed has proposed a narrower "payment account" that would pay no interest and give no access to Fed credit. Kraken's banking unit received a limited purpose account from the Kansas City Fed in March, Crypto Briefing reported. Custodia told the court that kind of account would not be enough.

In our opinion: the decision that counts now is the Fed's own. Comments on its payment-account proposal closed July 27, and the final version will set how much of the payment system crypto-focused banks can reach without a master account. Custodia's own timeline is the measure of the court route: six years from application to Monday's order.
