Dollar and Staples Stocks Rise in November as Bitcoin Falls 25%

Almost everything fell this month. The dollar, consumer staples and cash funds held up, and Anthropic's IPO took in $119.5 billion while bitcoin ETFs lost $3.47 billion.

Almost every major market fell in November. Bitcoin dropped about 25% from its Oct. 31 level to $58,949 at 4:49 p.m. ET Friday, per CoinGecko and Yahoo Finance data. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 6,910.39, down 3.7% on the day and 10% below its record, CNBC and the AP reported.

A short list held up: the U.S. dollar, consumer staples stocks and, this week, Treasury bonds. The money trail is less tidy than "out of crypto, into safety." In the same weeks, investors handed Anthropic $119.5 billion and kept buying stock funds.

The November scoreboard

Change from the Oct. 30 close to Friday's close, from Yahoo Finance data, with the Oct. 30 closes checked against Nasdaq.com:

  • U.S. dollar index: up 2.0%
  • Consumer staples fund (XLP): up 1.2%
  • 7-10 year Treasury fund (IEF): down 0.1%
  • Dow: down 7.0%
  • S&P 500: down 8.8%
  • Gold futures: down 9.6%
  • Nasdaq Composite: down 11.1%
  • Ether: down about 29%, Oct. 31 to Friday afternoon

How it works: where money goes when prices fall

A falling price does not send dollars anywhere. Every sale has a buyer, so the cash only changes hands. If bitcoin drops 25%, holders lose value on paper, but no pile of cash leaves the market. Flows are different. They count dollars that actually moved into or out of a fund, a stablecoin or a share sale. That is why this story tracks both.

The dollar

The dollar index reached 103.9 on Thursday, its highest since April 2025, and was up 1.7% for the month by Friday midday, CNBC and Reuters reported. Its rally picked up on Nov. 10 after hot inflation data and Fed Governor Christopher Waller's call to weigh a half-point December hike. Higher expected rates make cash in dollars pay more.

Treasuries and grocery stocks

Bond buyers showed up Friday. The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 12 basis points to near 4.93%, its biggest drop in a month, CNBC and Reuters reported. Bond prices rise when yields fall. The long-dated Treasury fund TLT gained 2.6% for the week, though it is still slightly down for November.

Consumer staples led the S&P 500 on Friday, with General Mills, Walmart and Costco higher while chip stocks fell, CNBC and Benzinga reported. People buy cereal and toothpaste in a slump, so these companies' sales often hold steadier.

Gold did not catch the bid

Gold fell too. Spot gold was $3,521.38 an ounce at 4 p.m. ET Friday, down 9.3% in a week, USA Today reported. It sits about a third below its Jan. 29 record close of $5,405, GoldPrice.com data show. Gold pays no interest, so rising rate-hike bets and a stronger dollar weigh on it.

Cash: a record, then a pause

U.S. money market funds hit a record $8.016 trillion in the week to Nov. 4, the Investment Company Institute and Crane Data reported. LSEG Lipper counted $117.97 billion of inflows that week, the most since May, citing midterm caution and AI spending worries, Reuters reported. Crane's daily series shows money funds up $67.7 billion for the month through Nov. 18. The rush has paused: ICI showed a $3.58 billion dip in the week to Nov. 18.

The biggest buyer: Anthropic's IPO

Anthropic closed its IPO on Friday. It sold 1.127 billion shares at $83 for $93.5 billion and took in $26 billion more from a private sale to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, its release and CNBC said. That is $119.5 billion in all. The stock closed Friday at $88.91, down 8.5% on the day but above its $83 offer price.

Dip buyers in stock funds

Investors put a net $59.23 billion into global equity funds in the week to Nov. 18, the most since mid-June, including $12.3 billion into technology funds, LSEG Lipper data show, per Reuters and U.S. News. That money went in before Thursday's and Friday's selloff.

What left crypto

  • Spot bitcoin ETFs: $3.47 billion out from Nov. 2 to Nov. 19, Farside Investors data show, matching Yahoo Finance's count of about $3.5 billion.
  • Spot ether ETFs: about $973 million out over the same days, per Farside.
  • Stablecoins: supply fell from $296.99 billion on Oct. 31 to $283.56 billion on Friday, down $13.4 billion, DefiLlama data show. SatoshiMacro puts Friday's total at $283.6 billion, below the May 17 peak of $312.9 billion.

When a stablecoin is redeemed, the issuer pays out dollars and sells the matching reserves, mostly short-term Treasury bills. So that $13.4 billion went back into dollars.

What to watch

The Personal Consumption Expenditures inflation report comes next week, and the Fed meets Dec. 8-9. Traders priced about a 92% chance of a quarter-point hike in December on Friday, per CME FedWatch data cited by CNBC. ICI's next money fund report moves to Wednesday, Nov. 25, for Thanksgiving.

In our opinion: most of the value lost this month vanished in lower prices rather than moving somewhere else. The clearest measured flows went to cash funds early in the month and to Anthropic's record share sale, which by itself took in about 34 times what left bitcoin ETFs. Crypto's own exits were small next to that, but the $13.4 billion drop in stablecoins shows some money leaving the sector altogether. Next week's PCE report is the next test: a hot reading keeps a December hike in play, and this month that has favored the dollar and cash over bitcoin and gold.
