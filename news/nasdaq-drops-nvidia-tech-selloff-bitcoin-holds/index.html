Nasdaq Drops 2.5% as Nvidia Slide Drags Tech, Bitcoin Holds Near $65,400

Nvidia fell 4.1% to $156.14 ahead of Tuesday's earnings, and the S&P 500 lost 1.9% for a third straight drop. Bitcoin was up 0.4% over 24 hours.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.49% on Monday to 24,509.76, its third straight loss, as Nvidia led another sell-off in chip and AI stocks.

The S&P 500 lost 1.92% to 7,416.59 and the Dow fell 739.38 points, or 1.41%, to 51,608.17, per CNBC and AP. Bitcoin went the other way, barely. It sat at $65,429 at 5:47 p.m. ET, up 0.4% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko. Ether was at $1,832, up 0.3%.

Nvidia's slide since October

Nvidia closed at $156.14, down 4.12%, Investing.com data show. That is about 35% below its record close of $239.24 on Oct. 6, The Washington Post reported. Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week. The company reports third-quarter results after Tuesday's close.

The selling spread. Micron fell 7.62% and Intel 7.07%, and Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan each lost more than 2%, per CNBC. Falling stocks beat rising ones on the NYSE by 2,197 to 655.

Why chip stocks matter to crypto

Nvidia sits at the center of what traders call the AI trade, and CNBC said Tuesday's results will be judged as much on how the company moves forward after Huang's exit as on the numbers. Crypto feels it through the same pool of investors: a sell-off that cuts tech portfolios can leave less appetite for other volatile assets. On Monday, that spillover didn't show up in bitcoin's price.

Bitcoin had already taken its hit. It fell to about $62,000 late Saturday, its lowest since August, after forced selling of leveraged bets, CNBC reported. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index read 10, or "Extreme Fear," on Monday.

In our opinion: on a 2.5% down day for the Nasdaq, bitcoin was the calm one in the room while chip stocks took the losses. That split lasted one session, with a Fear & Greed reading of 10 behind it. The bigger test is Tuesday night, when Nvidia's numbers land with a new leadership story attached. The number to watch is bitcoin's Saturday low near $62,000, and whether Monday's split survives Wednesday's open.
