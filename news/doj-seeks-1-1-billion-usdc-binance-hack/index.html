DOJ Moves to Forfeit $1.1 Billion in USDC Tied to Binance Hack

Circle has frozen the tokens and agreed to mint replacement USDC for the U.S. if a judge orders forfeiture, the complaint says. Binance put its losses at $6.34 billion.

The U.S. Justice Department on Monday filed a civil forfeiture complaint seeking more than $1.1 billion in USDC that prosecutors say came from the Oct. 29 theft at Binance, the department said.

The complaint, filed in federal court in Washington, D.C., lists USDC held at 36 addresses on 18 blockchains. Balances in the filing add up to about $1.17 billion, The Block reported. U.S. investigators attribute the theft to North Korean hackers known as Lazarus Group, or APT38.

How a frozen stablecoin gets returned

USDC is a dollar-pegged token issued by Circle, which can block specific addresses so the coins there can't move. That is what happened here. But Circle told prosecutors it can't transfer the original tokens out of the blocked addresses, according to the complaint. Instead, Circle agreed to issue an equal amount of new USDC and send it to the United States once a court enters a forfeiture order.

Civil forfeiture is a case against the property itself, not a person, so no one is charged in this filing. The allegations have not been decided by a court. Prosecutors also asked a judge to keep the funds restrained while the case runs, after a temporary order granted on Nov. 12.

Part of a $6 billion recovery effort

The department said U.S. authorities have helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion in crypto from multiple sources since the theft. Binance has put its losses at about $6.34 billion and said it covered affected users' assets with its own funds.

"Following the Binance theft, the FBI and our partners moved quickly to trace and freeze stolen assets," Brett Leatherman, operations director of the FBI's Cyber Branch, said in the department's release.

In our opinion: This case shows the trade-off built into regulated stablecoins. An issuer that can freeze coins can also help return them, which is why this $1.1 billion could be held in place for the court. The next step is the court's ruling on keeping the funds restrained, and then any forfeiture order that would trigger Circle's replacement tokens.
