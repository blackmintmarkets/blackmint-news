Nvidia Posts $115.8 Billion Quarter and Guides to $130 Billion, Above Estimates

Nvidia's revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to $115.8 billion, above the $109.1 billion analysts expected, in its first report since Jensen Huang left as CEO.

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended Oct. 25, up 103% from a year earlier, and forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter, according to its earnings release filed Tuesday. Both numbers topped Wall Street estimates.

Analysts had expected $109.10 billion in revenue and adjusted earnings of $2.48 a share, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Nvidia earned $2.68 a share on an adjusted basis. The $130 billion forecast, plus or minus 3%, compares with an average estimate of $125.1 billion.

It was the company's first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week, CNBC and Investopedia reported.

Where the money came from

  • Data center: $107.6 billion, up 110% from a year earlier
  • Edge computing: $8.2 billion, up 42%
  • Net income: $65.3 billion, up 105%
  • Adjusted gross margin: 74.9%

Data center sales are the chips and networking gear that cloud companies and AI labs buy to train and run models. Gross margin is the share of each sales dollar left after the cost of making the product. Nvidia guided that margin down to 71% to 72% next quarter. Its outlook assumes no data center compute sales to China, and the quarter includes an extra week.

Nvidia also returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends during the quarter.

The stock closed at $160.50, up 3.2%, and rose about 2% more in after-hours trading, CNBC reported. Bitcoin traded at $64,762 at 5:47 p.m. ET, down 1% in 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: A $6.7 billion revenue beat in the first report without Huang as CEO gives the board a strong opening answer on succession. For crypto readers, the open question is whether a firmer tech tape lifts appetite for risk more broadly. The next tell is whether Wednesday's session holds the after-hours gain, and whether the 71% margin guide becomes the new worry.
