Nasdaq Futures Rise 3.3% as Asian Authorities Act to Steady Stocks

South Korea readied its stock stabilization fund and Taiwan curbed short sales after last week's 10% Nasdaq drop. Bitcoin rose 1% to near $61,340.

Nasdaq 100 futures rose 3.3% by 3:51 a.m. ET Monday, Reuters reported, after the Nasdaq fell 10% last week, its biggest weekly drop since March 2020. S&P 500 futures added 2.5%.

Bitcoin moved less. It traded near $61,340 at 5:52 a.m. ET, up 1% in 24 hours, per CoinGecko, and the Crypto Fear & Greed Index read 11, its "extreme fear" band.

Seoul, Taipei and Beijing step in

  • South Korea: The finance ministry said after a Sunday emergency meeting that it will prepare its Stock Market Stabilization Fund for prompt use, ease daily share-buyback limits for three months from Nov. 24 and step up checks for illegal short selling.
  • Taiwan: The Financial Supervisory Commission barred short sales below the prior day's close, except hedging by securities and futures firms, CNBC reported.
  • China: Exchanges in Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing will cut the minimum margin ratio for margin-financed stock purchases to 80% from 100% starting Nov. 30, per the same CNBC report.

Short selling is selling borrowed shares to profit from a fall. Margin financing is buying with borrowed money; a lower margin ratio means less of the investor's own cash per trade.

South Korea's KOSPI still fell about 3.3%, while Taiwan's index rose 2.1%, Reuters said. "This was no longer simply a tech selloff," Deutsche Bank strategist Jim Reid wrote, per Reuters.

Yields rise with rate-hike odds

The 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 to 5 basis points to about 4.96%, according to CNBC and Reuters. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Traders priced about a 96% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate increase in December, up from about 92% Friday, per CME FedWatch data cited by CNBC.

Higher expected rates lift returns on cash and bonds, which compete with assets that pay no yield, such as bitcoin.

New York Fed President John Williams speaks Monday. October core PCE inflation and revised third-quarter GDP are due Wednesday, CNBC said, and Dell's results Tuesday are another test, according to Reuters.

In our opinion: the number to watch is Wednesday's core PCE. Analysts cited by CNBC expect a 0.3% monthly rise after September's 3.1% annual pace, the highest since May. Seoul has readied its fund but has not said it is using it. Investors carrying borrowed money in both stocks and crypto face the most risk if hike odds keep climbing.
