a16z Renames Its Crypto Arm 'a16z Frontier' and Widens Its Focus to AI and Robotics

Andreessen Horowitz is renaming a16z Crypto to a16z Frontier, adding AI and robotics while saying it will keep investing in crypto.

One of crypto's best-known venture investors is changing its name and broadening its scope.

What happened

Andreessen Horowitz said Thursday it is renaming a16z Crypto to a16z Frontier. In a blog post, managing partner Chris Dixon said the new fund will back "frontier" startups building technologies and markets that may take years to develop, including artificial intelligence, robotics and new kinds of networks.

Dixon stressed that the firm is not walking away from digital assets. "This is an expansion: we will continue to invest in crypto and related ventures and advocate for sensible crypto regulation," he wrote.

He argued that as blockchains are used more widely, the label matters less. Wall Street and fintech firms now regularly launch products built on blockchains, he said, so it makes more sense to define companies by what they do than by the technology underneath.

Who is involved

Dixon will lead the fund with a16z founders Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz and general partners Eddy Lazzarin and Guy Wuollet. Fortune reported that the change widens the scope of a $2.2 billion fund announced in May. TechCrunch reported the unit has raised nearly $10 billion across five funds since 2018, with past bets including Coinbase.

Why it matters for crypto

The move can be read two ways. TechCrunch framed it as a sign that crypto-only investing has lost some of its pull with venture firms. Dixon's own framing is that blockchain technology is becoming ordinary infrastructure inside wider tech. Either way, the shift suggests crypto startups may increasingly compete with AI and other emerging fields for the same pool of venture money.
