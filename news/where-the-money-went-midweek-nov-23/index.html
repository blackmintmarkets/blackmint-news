Nasdaq Jumps 6.5% After Nvidia Reinstates Huang as CEO

The Nasdaq had its best day since April 2025 and recovered just over half of last week's S&P 500 drop. Spot bitcoin ETFs lost $569.7 million on Monday, after $1.79 billion in the week through Friday.

The Nasdaq Composite jumped 6.51% on Monday, its best day since April 2025, after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. The index closed at 24,089.56, per CNBC and Yahoo Finance. The S&P 500 gained 4.73% to 7,237.17, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1,447.98 points, or 3.01%, to 49,595.31, CNBC and AP reported. Xinhua published the same official closes.

This midweek note covers the stretch from Friday's close on Nov. 20 to Monday's close on Nov. 23, after our November "Where the Money Went" deep dive. Last week the Nasdaq fell 10% and the S&P 500 fell 8.6%, per Yahoo Finance data. Monday's bounce recovered just over half of that S&P 500 drop, the AP reported. Overnight stock futures were already firmer after authorities in Asia moved to steady their markets, as we reported Monday morning. The Nvidia news then sent U.S. cash indexes sharply higher. For how the board reversed course, see our deep dive on Huang's reinstatement.

How it works: prices can rebound without new measured flows

A rising price does not, by itself, tell you that a pile of new cash arrived. Every buy still has a seller, so the cash only changes hands. If the Nasdaq jumps 6.5%, holders gain value on paper. Flows are different. They count dollars that actually moved into or out of a fund, a stablecoin or a share sale. That is why this story tracks both, and why some figures below still cover Friday or the week through Friday, when the complete reports are in.

Nvidia and the sector turn

Nvidia shares closed at $165.05, up $39.52, or 31.48%, from Friday's close of $125.53, per Yahoo Finance and The Guardian. Reuters, via StreetInsider, said the move added roughly $950 billion to Nvidia's market value. The Guardian put the increase at $952 billion, from $3.03 trillion on Friday to $3.977 trillion.

The technology sector led the rebound. The Technology Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLK) rose 8.97% to $162.38, per Yahoo Finance. CNBC said the S&P 500 technology sector soared 9%, and Xinhua put it at up 9.03%. Nine of the 11 S&P 500 sectors finished higher. Energy and consumer staples were the laggards; the matching funds, XLE and XLP, closed down 1.06% and 0.09% on Yahoo Finance.

Bitcoin bounced over the weekend, then sat out Monday's stock rally

Bitcoin was about $58,300 at 4 p.m. ET Friday and about $61,192 at 4 p.m. ET Monday, up 5.0%, per CoinGecko, with Yahoo Finance's 4 p.m. hour closes showing a similar 4.9% rise. Ether rose 7.8% over the same window, from about $1,599 to about $1,724.

The path was not a straight line. CoinGecko hourly data show bitcoin near $55,700 early Saturday. It then recovered, and it traded around $63,700 on Monday morning as the Nvidia news hit, matching the move we recorded in Monday's deep dive. By the 4 p.m. equity close it was back near $61,200. The Motley Fool similarly described bitcoin as little changed on Monday itself while the Nasdaq surged 6.51%.

The dollar, Treasuries, gold and oil

The U.S. dollar index closed at 104.32, up 0.49% from Friday's 103.81, per Yahoo Finance. Reuters said the dollar firmed at 104.32 and had peaked at 104.53 on Monday. The Invesco DB US Dollar Index Bullish Fund (UUP) rose 0.54%.

The 10-year Treasury yield was little changed, closing at 4.918% on Yahoo Finance's ^TNX series after Friday's 4.912%. CNBC had the 10-year near 4.93% during the session. The 7-10 year Treasury fund IEF was flat, down less than 0.02%.

Gold futures rose 3.9% to $3,651.40, per Yahoo Finance. CNBC had U.S. gold futures at $3,649, up 3.8%, and spot gold at $3,650.62, up 3.4%, as of about 1:35 p.m. ET.

West Texas Intermediate crude for January delivery settled at $80.17 a barrel, down $3.24, or 3.88%. Brent for January settled at $89.37, down $3.20, or 3.46%, Xinhua reported. Yahoo Finance showed the same settlement prices.

What the flow reports show

The complete flow prints show money leaving spot crypto ETFs last week, and again on Monday, even as prices bounced after Friday's close.

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.790 billion of net outflows in the week of Nov. 16 to Nov. 20, Farside Investors data show. SoSoValue data reported by Bitcoin.com also put the weekly total at $1.79 billion. Bloomingbit, also citing SoSoValue, published the same weekly figure.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $1,367.5 million of that week, or about 76% of the total, per Farside. SoSoValue via Bitcoin.com put IBIT at $1.37 billion, and Bloomingbit put it at $1.368 billion. The rest of the week's fund tally, published by Farside and, with only small rounding differences, by Bitcoin.com from SoSoValue and by Trading Notified from Farside's daily prints, was:

  • Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC): $198.9 million out
  • Grayscale Bitcoin Trust (GBTC): $119.3 million out
  • ARK 21Shares Bitcoin ETF (ARKB): $68.0 million out
  • Bitwise Bitcoin ETF (BITB): $43.6 million out
  • Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust (BTC): $28.9 million out
  • Morgan Stanley's Bitcoin Trust (MSBT): $53.3 million in, the week's largest inflow

Smaller funds added a further net outflow. WisdomTree's BTCW took in $0.3 million, per Farside and Trading Notified.

Friday, Nov. 20, was the heaviest day of the week and of November: $669.2 million left the bitcoin funds, per Farside. CryptoDataAPI, which compiles Farside's settled daily totals, shows the same Friday figure, as does Trading Notified. IBIT accounted for $544.7 million of that session, per Farside, matching SoSoValue's Friday IBIT figure of $544.73 million in the Bitcoin.com report. Farside's Friday table also showed $72.4 million out of FBTC, $32.8 million out of GBTC, $12.9 million out of the Grayscale Mini Trust, $7.0 million out of BITB and $1.7 million out of Franklin's EZBC, partly offset by a $2.3 million inflow into MSBT. Those Friday fund figures also appear in CryptoDataAPI's daily table. Farside's historical IBIT column lists Friday's $544.7 million as the fund's largest one-day outflow, one session after Thursday's $541.1 million IBIT outflow and $582.3 million total.

Only Monday, Nov. 16, was a net inflow, at $7.9 million, per Farside and CryptoDataAPI. The next four sessions were outflows, and each was larger than the last: $239.9 million on Tuesday, $306.5 million on Wednesday, $582.3 million on Thursday and $669.2 million on Friday. The November daily totals through Monday, Nov. 23, are shown in the Black Mint chart with this story.

The week was the fourth in a row of net bitcoin ETF outflows. Bitcoin.com, citing SoSoValue, and Trading Notified, summing Farside, both put the four-week total, covering the weeks of Oct. 26-30 through Nov. 16-20, at about $4.34 billion.

November through Friday is $4.137 billion of net outflows over 15 sessions, with only two inflow days, per CryptoDataAPI's settled table. The three complete November weeks on Farside sum to the same $4,137.0 million, and Trading Notified also put November through Friday at about $4.14 billion. June 2026 remains the largest month of outflows in that record, at $4.51 billion, per CryptoDataAPI's monthly history and Trading Notified's Farside sum. October's total was a $515.9 million outflow, per CryptoDataAPI and Coinburn. IBIT accounted for $3.49 billion of November's exits through Friday, CryptoDataAPI's sum of Farside's daily IBIT figures shows, with FBTC at $217.1 million out, GBTC at $219.4 million out and MSBT at $111.0 million in. Monday, Nov. 23, added another $569.7 million of outflows, per Farside and CryptoDataAPI, so November through Monday is $4.707 billion of net outflows over 16 sessions, with only two inflow days. IBIT's November outflow through Monday is $3.995 billion, FBTC's is $203.8 million, and GBTC's and MSBT's are unchanged from Friday.

Since the funds launched on Jan. 11, 2024, cumulative net inflows through Friday were $52.91 billion, per CryptoDataAPI, covering 719 settled trading days. After Monday's $569.7 million outflow, Farside's running total fell to $52,341 million through Monday, Nov. 23, and CryptoDataAPI also shows $52.34 billion across 720 settled trading days. SoSoValue-based tallies are a little lower: Bloomingbit put cumulative net inflows at $52.84 billion as of Friday, and total net assets at $71.35 billion. Bitcoin.com, also citing SoSoValue, published the same $71.35 billion asset figure and said it was down from $86.2 billion a week earlier, with falling bitcoin prices accounting for much of that decline alongside the withdrawals. Small gaps between providers are common. The daily creation and redemption figures are closer.

Spot ether ETFs had $305.8 million of net outflows in the same week, per Farside and SatoshiMacro, which compiles Farside. SoSoValue, in the Bitcoin.com report, put the ether total at $314.17 million. BlackRock's iShares Ethereum Trust (ETHA) accounted for $230.0 million of the Farside week, a figure SatoshiMacro also lists and that Bitcoin.com rounded to about $230 million. Friday's ether ETF outflow was $97.3 million, per Farside and SatoshiMacro. Bitcoin.com said the week was the eighth in a row of ether ETF outflows. SatoshiMacro's eight-week table is negative in every week from the week beginning Sept. 28 through the week beginning Nov. 16. Combined bitcoin and ether ETF outflows last week were about $2.10 billion in SoSoValue's figures, or $2.096 billion in Farside's. On Monday, Nov. 23, spot ether ETFs had a further $84.9 million of net outflows, per Farside and CryptoDataAPI: $90.5 million out of ETHA, partly offset by a $5.6 million inflow into Fidelity's FETH.

A daily ETF flow total is a net of share creations and redemptions. It does not count how many people bought or sold bitcoin or ether in the open market. Authorized participants create new shares against the bitcoin or ether the fund holds, or redeem shares and take the underlying asset back. A net outflow means more shares were redeemed than created that day. The tables do not name the end investor, and they do not, by themselves, prove a change of long-term view.

The outflows arrived as bitcoin slipped below $60,000 on Friday, alongside last week's drop in technology stocks, Bitcoin.com said. The Motley Fool also tied this week's price pressure to the size of last week's ETF exits. Bitcoin.com and The Motley Fool describe the price backdrop. They do not demonstrate a cause. Researchers at Oak Research, writing on Nov. 7 about the CFTC Commitments of Traders report as of Nov. 3, said a drop in hedge-fund futures shorts around the Oct. 30 CME expiry, together with about $1 billion of ETF outflows in that earlier window, was consistent with cash-and-carry trades reaching maturity without being renewed. They also said the data cannot identify the same funds behind the ETF flows, and that outflows in that window can mix unrenewed arbitrage with directional selling after the late-October Binance incident. Those comments predate last week's $1.79 billion and do not identify who redeemed shares in the week through Friday.

Monday's Farside tables are now complete. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had $569.7 million of net outflows on Monday, Nov. 23, per Farside, the second-largest session of November after Friday's $669.2 million. IBIT accounted for $509.3 million, the Grayscale Mini Trust for $61.1 million and Bitwise's BITB for $12.6 million, partly offset by a $13.3 million inflow into FBTC. All other bitcoin funds were flat. Spot ether ETFs had $84.9 million of net outflows: $90.5 million out of ETHA and a $5.6 million inflow into FETH. CryptoDataAPI, which compiles Farside's settled daily totals, shows the same Monday figures. We did not find a second, independent daily source for Monday, so the Monday fund figures are attributed to Farside.

USD-pegged stablecoin supply, measured at 7 p.m. ET on DefiLlama, rose from $282.50 billion on Friday to $283.22 billion on Monday, a $0.72 billion increase. That Friday level is the $282.5 billion low we reported Nov. 22. The gain is small next to last week's ETF outflows, and it does not erase the drop earlier in November.

The Justice Department's move to forfeit about $1.1 billion in USDC tied to the October Binance incident is a legal claim on tokens already issued, not a redemption figure in the DefiLlama supply change.

What to watch

The Personal Consumption Expenditures inflation report is due Wednesday, and the Fed meets Dec. 8-9. CNBC said CME FedWatch on Monday showed a roughly 98% chance of a quarter-point December hike, up from around 88% on Friday. Reuters later said traders had fully priced in a 25-basis-point move. U.S. markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday's session ends at 1 p.m. ET, CNBC noted.

In our opinion: Monday's stock rebound showed up in prices, while bitcoin ETF flows kept falling. Spot bitcoin ETFs lost another $569.7 million on Monday, on top of $1.79 billion in the week through Friday, which was the fourth straight week of outflows, with $669 million on Friday itself. ICI and Lipper have not posted midweek numbers. Bitcoin's jump toward $63,700 on the Nvidia news faded by the closing bell, while Nvidia held a 31% gain. Wednesday's PCE report is the next scheduled test for rate-hike bets that have been supporting the dollar.
