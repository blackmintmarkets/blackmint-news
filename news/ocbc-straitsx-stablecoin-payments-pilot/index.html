OCBC and StraitsX Test Stablecoin Payments Into Business Accounts

NTT DOCOMO ASIA is the first customer. Payments travel as stablecoins and land as ordinary money, the second StraitsX-OCBC project in five days.

Singapore lender OCBC and stablecoin issuer StraitsX said on Monday they are piloting cross-border business payments that travel as stablecoins and arrive as ordinary money in clients' OCBC accounts. NTT DOCOMO ASIA is the first customer, the companies said in a joint announcement dated Nov. 23 in Singapore.

It is the second project the two firms have announced in five days. On Nov. 19, StraitsX, OCBC and Standard Chartered said they had finished a proof of concept moving tokenized bank deposits between the two banks.

How the payment travels

A stablecoin is a token built to hold a fixed value, such as one U.S. dollar, backed by reserves. In the pilot, a payer abroad sends stablecoins to a "dedicated wallet address" that StraitsX assigns to one business. That address tells StraitsX who the money is for. StraitsX then runs compliance checks, matches the payment to the right invoice and settles it. OCBC handles the "off-ramp," the step that turns the tokens into regular money in the client's bank account. The receiving company never has to hold or manage the tokens itself.

StraitsX, which issues the XSGD and XUSD stablecoins, holds a Major Payment Institution license from the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS). Both firms take part in BLOOM, a MAS program for testing new forms of digital money, the announcement said.

"Stablecoins can move value efficiently across borders, but businesses still need a straightforward way to receive and manage those payments," StraitsX Chief Executive Tianwei Liu said in the statement. OCBC's Carmen Chan, deputy head of global transaction banking, said insights from the pilot would help the bank deliver value as cross-border payments change.

Fintech News Singapore also reported the launch. The companies did not give payment volumes or say how many OCBC clients will join. The pilot starts with "selected" business payment flows, and any expansion depends on regulatory, compliance and operational requirements, they said.

In our opinion: The customer here never touches a token, and that is the design. The bank keeps the client relationship and StraitsX earns its place in the plumbing, so both sides gain if this grows. The number to look for is volume: until OCBC or StraitsX says how much money moved this way, a pilot with one named customer is still a test drive.
