CleanSpark Posts $987 Million Annual Loss as Bitcoin Output Falls

CleanSpark's revenue fell 25% to $576.5 million as it mined 8% fewer coins, while its Georgia site signed a 20-year, $6.6 billion data center lease.

CleanSpark lost $987.1 million in the fiscal year ended Sept. 30, after a $364.5 million profit a year earlier, the bitcoin miner said Tuesday in its results release and annual report.

Revenue fell 25% to $576.5 million. In the fourth quarter alone, revenue dropped 46% to $120.9 million, though the company posted net income of $9.8 million for the quarter.

A $281.6 million bitcoin markdown

CleanSpark recorded a $281.6 million loss on the fair value of its bitcoin, compared with a $425.6 million gain the year before. The annual report ties that to bitcoin falling from about $114,100 to about $83,600 between the two fiscal year-ends. It also booked a $118.7 million loss on bitcoin pledged as collateral and $177.1 million in impairment charges, mostly tied to converting its Sandersville, Georgia, site from mining to data center use.

Under fair-value accounting, a company marks the bitcoin it holds to market each quarter, so a lower price shows up as a loss even if no coins are sold. An impairment is a write-down of equipment or property the company no longer expects to earn as much from.

Fewer coins mined, a long lease signed

CleanSpark mined 7,253 bitcoin in the year, down 8%, as its average hashrate, the computing power it points at the network, fell 21% to 36.0 exahashes per second. It held 13,530 bitcoin, including collateral, worth $1.1 billion on Sept. 30.

The Sandersville site now has a 20-year, $6.6 billion lease with a tenant the company called "high investment grade" without naming it. CleanSpark funded construction with $2.276 billion of 7.875% senior secured notes due 2031 and plans its first data hall for November 2027.

CleanSpark shares closed at $5.10 on Tuesday, down 7.8%, before the results came out, according to Nasdaq. Bitcoin traded near $59,995 at 5:48 p.m. ET, down 1.7% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko, with Coinbase showing $59,970.

In our opinion: The bigger story sits in Georgia. Spread evenly, the $6.6 billion over 20 years averages about $330 million a year, more than half of what mining brought in during fiscal 2026. Much of the loss was a paper markdown on coins the company still holds, and at Tuesday's price those 13,530 bitcoin would be worth about $812 million. The date to watch is November 2027, when the first data hall is due.
