Stablecoin Supply Falls to $282.5 Billion, Lowest Since September 2025

Supply is down $23.2 billion from its Oct. 7 high after seven straight weekly declines. Tether's USDT lost $7.9 billion in 30 days, DefiLlama data show.

The total supply of stablecoins fell to $282.51 billion on Saturday, its lowest daily level since September 2025, according to DefiLlama data. It stood at $282.54 billion on Sunday, down $4.55 billion from a week earlier.

How supply shrinks

Stablecoins are tokens built to hold a steady value, such as $1, backed by reserves like cash and short-term government debt. When a large holder sends dollars to an issuer such as Tether or Circle, new coins are created. When holders hand coins back for dollars, the issuer burns them and supply falls. The total is a rough measure of how many dollars sit inside crypto in token form.

Seven Sundays, seven declines

Measured Sunday to Sunday, supply has dropped seven weeks in a row, DefiLlama data show. It is down $23.16 billion, or 7.6%, from an autumn high of $305.70 billion on Oct. 7, and $30.32 billion below the record $312.87 billion set on May 17.

Three coins account for most of the $18.57 billion drop over the past 30 days:

  • Tether's USDT: $174.54 billion, down $7.88 billion in 30 days
  • Circle's USDC: $68.66 billion, down $3.60 billion
  • Sky's USDS: $3.26 billion, down $3.48 billion, including $2.06 billion in the past week

CoinGecko put USDT's market value at $174.59 billion and USDC's at $68.38 billion on Sunday evening, close to DefiLlama's counts. The two providers track supply in slightly different ways.

The wider market

Bitcoin traded at $60,921 at 5:53 p.m. ET, up 1.4% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index, a sentiment gauge from 0 to 100, read 11, or "Extreme Fear," on Sunday and has stayed between 10 and 12 for eight days.

The data do not show who redeemed or why. Supply also falls when issuers burn coins they hold in reserve, and DefiLlama does not separate those cases.

In our opinion: Stablecoins are crypto's cash drawer, and the drawer is $23 billion lighter than it was in early October. Every redeemed coin is a dollar that went back to a bank account instead of waiting to buy tokens, and Tether alone accounts for more than $7 billion of the past month's drop. The first sign of a turn would be a week of net creation at Tether or Circle, and we will check that number again next Sunday.
