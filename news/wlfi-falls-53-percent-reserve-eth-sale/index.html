WLFI Falls 53% After World Liberty Wallet Sells $20 Million in Ether

World Liberty's token hit a record low near $0.0167 after a project wallet sold ether and repaid part of a Dolomite loan. Justin Sun called for delistings.

World Liberty Financial's WLFI token fell 53.4% in 24 hours to about $0.0191 at 5:53 a.m. ET Saturday, according to CoinGecko, after touching a record low of $0.0167 shortly before 1 a.m. ET. That leaves it about 94% below its September 2025 high of $0.331.

The drop followed an ether sale by a project treasury wallet. OnchainBeat, which checked the transactions on Etherscan, said the wallet sold 10,600 ETH and 1,378 mETH, a staked-ether token, for about $20.1 million in USDC and USD1 on Friday evening, UTC. It said about $16.59 million in USDC then went to the lending protocol Dolomite to pay down a loan, a transfer Bitcoin.com News also reported. On-chain analyst EmberCN estimated the ether was bought last year near $3,244 a coin, implying a loss of about $19.26 million. Mempool Brief said that figure is an on-chain estimate, not an audited result.

How a token loan works

On Dolomite, a borrower posts tokens as collateral and borrows stablecoins against them. If that collateral loses too much value, the position can be sold off automatically; repaying part of the loan lowers that risk. In April, World Liberty pledged about 5 billion WLFI to borrow $75 million, crypto.news and CoinDesk reported.

USD1, the project's dollar stablecoin, dipped to about $0.94 early Saturday, both outlets reported, and was back at $0.998 at 5:53 a.m. ET on CoinGecko.

Sun's allegations and the other side

Tron founder Justin Sun, an early investor who sued World Liberty in April over frozen tokens, alleged in an X post that insiders had sold tokens improperly and defrauded investors, and urged exchanges to delist WLFI and USD1. His post gave no wallet addresses or transaction details, crypto.news said. World Liberty, which is backed by the Trump family, gave a different account in a May lawsuit against Sun, saying its token restrictions were allowed under its sale terms and accusing him of a campaign to push the price down. The company had not commented on Saturday's sale, according to CoinDesk and Bitcoin.com News.

In our opinion: The company has not explained the sale, so the on-chain record is the only account readers have. The pressure point is the collateral: the April loan was backed by about 5 billion WLFI, and that token lost half its value overnight. Watch for a World Liberty statement, any exchange response to Sun's call, and whether USD1 keeps trading at $1.
