PCE Inflation Rises to 3.8% in October as Core Tops Forecast

Core PCE rose 3.3%, above the 3.2% forecast, as incomes jumped 0.7% and Q3 growth was revised to 4.3%, ahead of the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting.

U.S. prices rose 3.8% in the 12 months to October on the Fed's preferred gauge, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said at 8:30 a.m. ET Wednesday. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 3.3%.

The core figure came in a tenth of a point above the 3.2% economists expected, according to CNBC and The Defiant. The Federal Reserve targets 2% inflation, and markets had already priced a quarter-point rate hike for its Dec. 8-9 meeting before the release, CNBC reported.

How the PCE gauge works

The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index tracks what U.S. households pay for goods and services. The "core" version drops food and energy because those prices swing the most. The Fed officially follows the headline number, but policymakers treat core as the better guide to longer-term trends, CNBC noted.

Rates matter for crypto through yield. A higher Fed rate lifts what cash and Treasury bills pay, which raises the bar for assets that pay no interest, such as bitcoin.

Incomes rose faster than spending

Personal income rose $201.8 billion, or 0.7%, in October, while spending rose 0.3%, BEA said. CNBC said economists had expected income growth of 0.4%. The saving rate rose to 4.1%, and prices rose 0.3% for the month on both the headline and core measures.

A separate BEA release revised third-quarter growth up 0.9 percentage point, to a 4.3% annual rate, mainly on investment and consumer spending.

Rate odds for 2027 eased

Treasury yields fell after the data. Traders still priced two more hikes but cut the odds of a third in 2027 to about 1 in 3, from 50-50 on Tuesday, based on CME Group's FedWatch, CNBC reported. "America's inflation problem is bigger than an energy shock," Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union, said in comments carried by CNBC.

Bitcoin was near $58,230 at 11:49 a.m. ET, down 2.1% over 24 hours, and ether was near $1,635, down 2.2%, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The number that matters for December is 3.3%, the core reading that came in above forecasts. With a hike already priced, the Dec. 9 statement and the Fed's new projections carry the real information. The next PCE report lands Dec. 23, two weeks after that meeting.
