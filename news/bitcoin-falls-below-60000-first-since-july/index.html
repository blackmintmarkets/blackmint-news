Bitcoin Falls Below $60,000 for the First Time Since July

Bitcoin traded near $59,500 at 11:51 a.m. ET Friday, down 5.5% in 24 hours, as AI stocks sold off and a crypto sentiment index sat at 12.

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, Reuters reported. It traded near $59,500 at 11:51 a.m. ET, down 5.5% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

The drop puts bitcoin about 30% below where it stood 30 days ago, near $84,500 on Oct. 21, per CoinGecko data. Yahoo Finance data show the coin down 23% so far in November, which would be its worst month since June 2022, when it fell 38%, Yahoo Finance reported.

Tech stocks and fund exits

Reuters tied the move to concerns about high valuations in the AI trade and other risk assets. Chip stocks fell Friday after Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, with Nvidia down about 3.8% and AMD about 5.1% at the time of Decrypt's report.

Ether traded near $1,643 at 11:51 a.m. ET, down 7.4% over 24 hours, per CoinGecko.

U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds lost $582.3 million on Thursday, taking November withdrawals to about $3.5 billion, according to Farside Investors data cited by Yahoo Finance.

How the fear gauge works

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index scores market mood from 0, extreme fear, to 100, extreme greed. It blends price swings, trading volume, social media activity and other market data. The Alternative.me version stood at 12 on Friday, in its "Extreme Fear" band. A low reading describes mood; it does not predict the next move.

The other view

Fundstrat strategist Sean Farrell said Thursday that signs of a bottom were starting to emerge, but that selling had not yet reached past levels he would call compelling, Yahoo Finance reported.

In our opinion: the number that matters is $3.5 billion, the November exit from U.S. bitcoin funds. It comes on top of a 30% price drop in 30 days, and the losses sit with fund holders as well as the miners and treasury companies that keep bitcoin on their books. Friday's flow figures from Farside Investors will show whether the exits slowed or sped up.
