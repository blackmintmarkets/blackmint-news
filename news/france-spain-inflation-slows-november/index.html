France and Spain Inflation Slows More Than Forecast in November

France's harmonised rate fell to 3.2% and Spain's to 4.1%, both below Reuters poll forecasts, as fuel prices eased. The ECB meets Dec. 16-17.

Inflation in France and Spain slowed more than economists expected in November, flash estimates from the two countries' statistics agencies showed Friday. France's EU-harmonised rate fell to 3.2% from 3.7% in October, INSEE said. Spain's fell to 4.1% from 4.9%, INE said.

Analysts polled by Reuters had expected 3.6% for France, Reuters reported. For Spain's national index, which also came in at 4.1%, the Reuters poll had expected 4.6%, according to a separate Reuters report.

Fuel Prices Drove Both Slowdowns

In France, energy inflation eased to 19.2% from 24.1% as oil product prices slowed sharply, though gas prices rose faster, INSEE said. Services inflation dipped to 2.3% from 2.6%. Prices fell 0.7% from October.

In Spain, INE said the drop came mainly from lower prices for vehicle fuel and lubricants. Core inflation, which leaves out energy and unprocessed food, fell to 2.6% from 3.1%.

What a Flash Estimate Is

A flash estimate is an early reading built from a partial sample of prices, released before the full data. INSEE publishes final November figures on Dec. 11. The harmonised index (HICP) uses the same method across EU countries so rates can be compared, and the European Central Bank measures inflation for its 2% target with the euro-area HICP.

The ECB's Governing Council holds its next policy meeting on Dec. 16-17. Interest-rate decisions affect the cost of borrowing across markets, including for traders who use leverage on crypto and other risk assets.
