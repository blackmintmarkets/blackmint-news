New Zealand Forecasters Lift One-Year Inflation Outlook to 2.92%

One-year expectations jumped 32 basis points in the RBNZ survey, while five- and 10-year views eased. Forecasters see the cash rate at 3.40% in a year.

New Zealand's business leaders and professional forecasters now expect inflation of 2.92% a year from now, up from 2.60% three months ago, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Survey of Expectations showed on Tuesday, Nov. 17, local time. That is a 32-basis-point jump in one quarter.

The longer view moved the other way. Two-year expectations edged up to 2.36% from 2.34%, five-year expectations fell to 2.17% from 2.31%, and 10-year expectations slipped to 2.13% from 2.20%, according to the survey and Trading Economics.

What the survey measures

Every quarter the central bank asks a panel of executives and economists where they think prices, wages and interest rates are heading. This round drew 44 responses, collected from Oct. 23 to 30. The two-year figure gets the most attention because that is roughly when a rate change made today works its way into prices, FXStreet noted. Expectations matter on their own, too: a firm that expects higher costs tends to raise prices and wages in advance.

Respondents also lifted their rate forecast. They see the Official Cash Rate at 3.40% a year from now, up from 3.21% in the last survey, and at 3.05% by the end of December. Actual inflation was 3.7% in the September quarter, down from 4.1% in June, and unemployment rose to 5.7%, the RBNZ said.

For markets, the survey adds New Zealand to a list of places where rate expectations are climbing. Kansas City Fed President Jeffrey Schmid said on Sunday the Fed "has some work to do on inflation," CNBC reported. Higher expected rates raise the return on cash and bonds, which compete for the same money as assets with no yield, such as bitcoin.

In our opinion: The split is the real story. Forecasters see a bumpier year ahead but still trust the long run, with 10-year expectations at 2.13%, close to the RBNZ's 2% target midpoint. Borrowers carry the stake, since the one-year cash-rate forecast rose 19 basis points in a single survey. The next check is the central bank's own rate decision and whether it sounds as worried as its panel.
