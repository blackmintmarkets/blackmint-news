Hong Kong SFC Adds Hyperliquid to Its Unlicensed Platform Alert List

The regulator says Hyperliquid's trading site isn't SFC-licensed and is suspected of serving Hong Kong users. The listing carries no fine or court finding.

Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission added "Hyperliquid / Hyperliquid Hong Kong" to its alert list of suspicious virtual asset trading platforms on Nov. 18, according to the regulator's entry. It names the website app.hyperliquid.xyz/trade.

The SFC said the entity is not licensed by it and is "suspected of operating, or holding itself out as operating, a virtual asset trading platform in Hong Kong and/or actively marketing its services to the Hong Kong public."

What the Alert List Is

The list is a public warning, not a fine, a court ruling or a block on the site. The SFC uses it to flag platforms that appear to serve Hong Kong residents without a license. Its standard notice says users of an unlicensed platform are not protected by SFC supervision and may lose everything if the platform fails or is hacked. Hyperliquid is the newest name on the list, which also includes BTCC, added on Oct. 22, per the SFC alert list.

Crypto Briefing and The Defiant also reported the listing.

How Hyperliquid Works

Hyperliquid runs its own blockchain, and its core includes on-chain order books for perpetual futures and spot trading, according to its documentation. Perpetual futures are derivatives that let traders bet on a price with borrowed money and no expiry date. Users keep control of their own funds instead of depositing them with a company, Crypto Briefing reported.

That design is why the case is worth following. Hong Kong licenses platforms based on who they serve, and the SFC's entry does not say how it treats an exchange that runs on a blockchain with no local firm behind it.

No Response Yet

Hyperliquid's official X account had not posted about the listing as of 5:50 a.m. ET on Thursday. The SFC entry lists no address or operating company.

In our opinion: The question worth chasing is who answers for a platform that has no Hong Kong office. The listing is a warning to Hong Kong users about the risk, and the SFC has not announced any block on the site. The next step to watch is whether Hyperliquid responds or limits access from Hong Kong.
