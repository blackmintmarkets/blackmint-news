Arkansas Sues Kalshi, Alleging Illegal Gambling Platform

Attorney General Tim Griffin filed in Independence County under the Deceptive Trade Practices Act, seeking restitution, penalties and an injunction.

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin sued KalshiEX LLC and Kalshi Inc. on Nov. 4, alleging the prediction-market operator offered illegal gambling to Arkansas residents, the attorney general's office said.

The civil case was filed in Independence County Circuit Court. Griffin's release says the state seeks restitution, civil penalties, unpaid taxes and injunctive relief under the Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act. The Northwest Arkansas Democrat-Gazette and the Arkansas Advocate reported the same filing date and court.

What the state alleges

Griffin's statement says Kalshi lets users wager on election and sports outcomes, among other events without Arkansas gambling licenses, taxes or age limits that apply to licensed operators. The release says the platform is open to people as young as 18, while Arkansas sets a 21-and-over floor for regulated casino and sports wagering. It points to Kalshi's self-exclusion tools and deposit limits, plus problem-gambling resources as evidence the company treats the product like gambling even while calling it a prediction market.

"Arkansas law is concerned with substance, not semantics," Griffin said in the release. That quote is the state's framing. The complaint, as summarized by the Democrat-Gazette and Arkansas Advocate, asks the court to declare Deceptive Trade Practices Act violations and order refunds for in-state users who lost money.

We found no public statement from Kalshi responding to the Arkansas complaint as of 6:10 a.m. ET Thursday. The company has previously described its contracts as event contracts overseen by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission. That federal framing is the core dispute in several state cases.

Prediction markets let users buy and sell contracts that pay out based on whether an event happens. Kalshi is registered with the CFTC as a designated contract market. States that sue argue sports and entertainment contracts are still illegal wagers under local law even if a federal futures regulator lists them.

Griffin filed a separate suit the same day against VGW, the operator behind Chumba Casino and related sweepstakes games, the Democrat-Gazette reported. Arkansas joins other states that have targeted Kalshi in court.

In our opinion: the forum fight is the stake. If state consumer-protection suits can reclassify CFTC-listed event contracts as illegal gambling, the product map for sports and election markets shrinks state by state. Watch for Kalshi's first filing in Independence County and whether other attorneys general copy Arkansas's Deceptive Trade Practices Act path.
