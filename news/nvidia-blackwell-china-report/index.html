Nvidia Ends Up 0.8% After Intraday Dip, Bloomberg China Chip Report

Bloomberg's report on Blackwell hardware at a Shanghai site tied to Alice Huang came before the open, yet Nvidia closed higher. What the filings show.

Nvidia stock fell as much as 2.6% on Friday, then recovered to close up 0.8% at $150.49, on a day Bloomberg reported that Blackwell-based hardware appears in Chinese loan filings for a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang. Yahoo Finance and Nvidia's investor page both show a low of $145.39 against Wednesday's $149.30 close. U.S. markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and trading ended at 1 p.m. ET Friday.

The chart below is Black Mint's, built from those two sources. It adds Friday's story to our midweek note on Nvidia's Monday rally.

What Bloomberg's filings show

Bloomberg published its report at 6:45 a.m. ET. It says two Chinese companies listed Blackwell hardware at the Yangtze River Delta Project in Shanghai, in documents filed with Chinese regulators and lenders.

  • Shanghai Shuoyao offered a GB300 NVL72 rack, which holds 72 Blackwell Ultra chips, as collateral for a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank. The rack's serial number appears in November filings.
  • Shanghai Kezhan described a GB200 server at the site when it took a 90 million yuan loan in late 2025 from a leasing firm owned by a Shanghai government agency.

Bloomberg typed the serial number into a public warranty database, which traced it to Taiwan's Gigabyte Technology. Gigabyte declined to say who bought the rack first and said it follows applicable laws. Engadget and Anadolu carried the same details. Blackwell chips need a U.S. license to ship to China, and President Donald Trump has said he does not want them sent there for now, Bloomberg reported.

What the report leaves open

Nobody has shown how or when the rack reached China. Bloomberg said the gear often passes through several hands before it is installed, and that Nvidia did not answer its questions. Bloomberg also said it found no evidence that Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud company Alice Huang once ran, sent the hardware to the site. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and Shuoyao called its tie to Megaspeed "ordinary commercial cooperation."

The filings are Chinese loan records that Bloomberg reviewed, and they are not public. Black Mint could not see them, so every fact above about their contents is Bloomberg's account.

Who Alice Huang is, and what is alleged

Alice Huang is the former Megaspeed chief executive whose affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang led to his resignation, Reuters reported. The resignation came Nov. 12, Quartz said, and the two Huangs are not related. U.S. authorities suspect she acted for Chinese intelligence, and China calls that groundless. Singapore said it investigated her over possible false declarations in server purchases, not espionage. Megaspeed says she left in August 2025. Reuters could not reach her for comment.

Among other ties, Bloomberg points to Kang Wei, the facility's president. Reuters identifies him as her husband. In a separate Nov. 25 report, Reuters found central-bank credit records listing up to 764 AI servers and modules worth $270 million at firms tied to her family and associates, much of it at a Shanghai site called the Yangtze River Delta AI supercomputing centre. Those records do not show whether the servers held chips barred from export.

The week around the report

Nvidia closed at $125.53 on Nov. 20 and $165.05 on Nov. 23, the day its board reinstated Jensen Huang. It is down 26.5% from its Oct. 30 close of $204.64, Yahoo Finance data show.

  • Nov. 23: the Commerce Department added seven entities to its Entity List, including Megaspeed units and Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary Apex, citing a significant risk of chip diversion, the Federal Register says. Alibaba called the Apex listing erroneous, Reuters reported.
  • Nov. 25: S&P Global Ratings put Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch Negative over governance, Reuters and Bloomberg reported.
  • Nov. 26: Nvidia told server partners to stop shipping to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported, per Reuters.

Friday's volume through the 1 p.m. close was 185.8 million shares. No single cause for the dip is confirmed. Investor's Business Daily pointed to the Southeast Asia halt and next week's testimony, along with the Bloomberg story.

What comes next

Huang is due for a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1, and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter on Dec. 2, the House Select Committee on the CCP and CNBC said. The Senate Banking Committee has scheduled Huang for a public hearing at 10 a.m. ET on Dec. 3, titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls," the committee's hearing notice and CBS News show.

In our opinion: Friday's price action says less than the headline did, since the stock gave back its dip within hours. The open question is who first bought that rack from Gigabyte, and neither the filings nor the companies have answered it. We will be following Dec. 1 closely, because the committee chairs said they want answers on how Nvidia's chips reached Chinese users.
