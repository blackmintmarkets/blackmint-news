Cross River, LendingUSA Launch Bitcoin-Backed Loans Up to $250,000

Borrowers can pledge bitcoin at 30%, 50% or 70% loan-to-value, held by a third-party custodian. It is Cross River Bank's first bitcoin-secured program as lender.

Bitcoin holders who need dollars but don't want to sell have one more bank-originated option. LendingUSA and Cross River Bank on Monday launched loans of $10,000 to $250,000 secured by bitcoin, the companies said.

It is Cross River's first bitcoin-secured lending program as the loan originator, according to the release and The Block. The bank, based in Fort Lee, New Jersey, has worked with LendingUSA for more than 10 years on point-of-sale and personal loans.

"People who invest in Bitcoin for the long term still have financial needs today," LendingUSA CEO Camilo Concha said in the announcement.

How a bitcoin-backed loan works

The borrower pledges bitcoin as collateral and gets dollars. Loan-to-value, or LTV, is the loan's size as a share of the collateral's value: at 50% LTV, $100,000 of bitcoin backs a $50,000 loan. Borrowers here can pick 30%, 50% or 70%, and a qualified third-party custodian holds the coins for the life of the loan.

There are two repayment setups. With interest-only, the borrower pays interest monthly and owes the principal at the end. With the no-monthly-payment option, interest builds up and everything is due at maturity; that one is offered only at 30% or 50% LTV. Paying early carries no penalty.

Rates not yet published

The companies did not publish interest rates. Rates depend on the LTV, the repayment option and AutoPay enrollment, and are disclosed after an application is received, the release says. Approval also depends on credit history and ability to repay.

What happens if bitcoin falls

A drop in bitcoin's price may require the borrower to add collateral or repay, and some or all of the pledged bitcoin can be liquidated, according to the companies' risk notice. A liquidation may be a taxable event, and if the sale doesn't cover the loan, the borrower still owes the rest. Bitcoin traded near $62,088 at 11:50 a.m. ET Monday, per CoinGecko.

In our opinion: Selling bitcoin to cover a big bill has always meant a tax form and a lost position; this product trades both for an interest rate nobody outside the companies has seen yet. That rate is the question worth chasing. The 70% LTV setting leaves the thinnest cushion, so borrowers there carry the most liquidation risk, and we'll be watching whether other banks start originating these loans themselves.
