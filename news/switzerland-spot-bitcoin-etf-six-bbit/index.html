Switzerland's First Spot Bitcoin ETF Debuts on SIX With 350 BTC

BIT Asset Management's BBIT fund holds bitcoin in Swiss cold storage, charges 0.19% a year and is open only to investors in Switzerland.

Switzerland's first spot bitcoin exchange-traded fund began trading on SIX Swiss Exchange on Thursday, holding 350 bitcoin at launch, its sponsor BIT Asset Management said.

The Bitcoin Fund ETF, ticker BBIT, trades in Swiss francs and U.S. dollars and charges 0.19% a year, according to SIX and the issuer, Pernet von Ballmoos. Swiss regulator FINMA approved the fund on Oct. 20 after an 18-month review, BIT said.

A fund instead of a note

Switzerland has listed bitcoin products for years, but they were exchange-traded products, or ETPs. Those are debt securities, so a buyer holds a claim on the issuer and carries its credit risk. BBIT is an investment fund under Swiss collective investment law. Its bitcoin sits in segregated cold storage, meaning offline wallets, at AMINA Bank, a Swiss bank, segregated from other assets.

SIX said it has listed bitcoin-linked structured products since 2016 and a physically backed single-bitcoin ETP since 2019. BBIT is the exchange's first spot bitcoin ETF, and BIT AM said it is currently Europe's only one.

Who can buy, and at what price

The public offering is limited to Switzerland and is not open to U.S. persons, BIT said. Units were issued at $10 when the fund launched on Nov. 19 and listed at $9.26, according to the PvB fund page. PvB classes the fund as an alternative investment with particular risk and says a total loss is possible.

Bitcoin traded near $60,450 at 5:50 a.m. ET, up 2.7% in 24 hours, per CoinGecko. BIT said in its release that bitcoin "trades at roughly half its October 2025 high." In the U.S., spot bitcoin ETFs had net outflows of $453 million on Wednesday, per Farside Investors.

Flow Traders is the market maker, and Deloitte audits the fund, BIT said.

In our opinion: BIT says it is comfortable launching with bitcoin near half its 2025 peak partly because much of the leverage has left the market. Eighteen months of review for one ticker is Swiss thoroughness at work. The real change for Swiss savers is structure: a fund holds the coins for its unitholders, while an ETP buyer is a creditor of the issuer. The number to watch is whether BBIT grows past its 350 starting coins once Swiss banks offer it to clients.
