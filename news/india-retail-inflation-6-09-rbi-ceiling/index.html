India's Retail Inflation Breaks the RBI's Ceiling, Rising to 6.09%

Food prices rose 8.94% from a year earlier, and economists polled by Reuters had expected 5.93%. The RBI's next rate decision is Dec. 4.

India's retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October, government data showed Thursday, above the 6% upper limit of the Reserve Bank of India's tolerance band. It was 5.54% in September.

The reading lands three weeks before the RBI's next policy decision on Dec. 4, and it is the last inflation print the central bank will see before then, Moneycontrol and News18 reported.

Food did the heavy lifting

Food prices rose 8.94% from a year earlier, by the government's consumer food price index, up from about 7.7% in September, the Ministry of Statistics figures show. Rural inflation was 6.63% and urban inflation 5.41%, the ministry said in its release, as quoted by News18. All figures are provisional.

Economists polled by Reuters had expected 5.93%, with forecasts from 5.50% to 6.60%, The Economic Times reported, carrying Reuters' story. Onion prices have nearly doubled since January, and food makes up nearly 40% of the index, according to that report.

How India's inflation target works

The consumer price index tracks what households pay for a fixed basket of goods and services. India now measures it against a 2024 base year, a change made on Feb. 12, 2026. The RBI aims for 4% inflation and treats anything from 2% to 6% as tolerable. When prices run above that band, the bank can raise its repo rate (what it charges commercial banks to borrow), which pushes up borrowing costs across the economy.

The RBI started raising rates in October. Economists in an earlier Reuters poll expected another 25 basis point increase in December, to 5.75%. Upasna Bhardwaj, chief economist at Kotak Mahindra Bank, expects 50 more basis points of hikes in the fiscal year that ends in March 2027, according to comments carried by News18.

In our opinion: the date that matters now is Dec. 4. October came in 0.16 percentage points above the Reuters median, and food, the biggest slice of the basket, is running near 9%, so the RBI has little room to wait. For crypto readers, India is one more large economy where borrowing costs are going up this winter.
