SEC Says Backward Hat Capital Raised $560 Million in Ponzi Scheme

The SEC won an asset freeze against the Indianapolis firm, which it says promised crypto-funded forex trading returning at least 6% a month. The CFTC also sued.

The Securities and Exchange Commission said Friday, Nov. 20, that it obtained a temporary restraining order and asset freeze against Indianapolis-based Backward Hat Capital, alleging the firm and its co-founders raised about $560 million from more than 2,000 investors in a Ponzi scheme, according to the SEC's release.

What the complaint alleges

The SEC says co-founders Phillip Williams and Devin Johnson told investors their money would be converted to crypto assets and used for foreign exchange (FX) arbitrage, run by an "AI-driven" algorithm they said returned at least 6% a month. Arbitrage means profiting from small price gaps between markets.

Instead, the complaint alleges, the defendants paid more than $250 million to earlier investors with newer investors' money and moved more than $220 million to themselves. That pattern is what makes a Ponzi scheme: returns paid from fresh deposits, not profits.

The SEC also charged Johnson's firm Traders Point Capital and Joseph Anderson, who it says produced fake "audit reports" for investors. The case was filed in federal court in the Southern District of Indiana.

Parallel cases

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission filed its own complaint alleging the scheme took in more than $550 million from at least 2,100 participants and did little, if any, forex trading. Federal prosecutors in Manhattan charged Williams and Johnson with securities fraud and conspiracy, and both were arrested on Nov. 18, the IRS Criminal Investigation unit said.

The charges are allegations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty. Black Mint did not find a public response from the defendants or their lawyers in the filings or reports reviewed.

The SEC urged investors to use resources on Investor.gov to check offers before investing.

In our opinion: the stake is the money of more than 2,000 investors, and the asset freeze is what stands between them and further losses. Promised returns of 6% a month would more than double money in a year, the kind of figure the SEC's own warning flags. The next thing to watch is whether the Indiana court extends the temporary freeze into a longer-lasting order.
