Bitcoin Miners' Pay Hits Record Low as 9% Difficulty Rise Nears

Hashprice, what a unit of mining power earns in a day, fell to $26.16 on Saturday. Bitcoin's next difficulty reset is on track for a 9% increase on Thursday.

Bitcoin miners are earning less per machine than at any point in Firsthand's hashprice record, which runs back to 2013, and the network is on track to make their work about 9% harder on Thursday.

Hashprice, the dollar amount that one petahash per second of computing power earns in a day, fell to $26.16 on Saturday, Nov. 21, according to Hashrate Index data cited by Bitcoin.com News and Investing.com. Firsthand, which keeps its own series, put Saturday morning's figure at $27.14, below the previous low of $27.74 on June 6.

How miner pay works

Miners collect newly issued bitcoin plus transaction fees for each block they add. Hashprice turns that into a daily wage per unit of computing power, so it falls when bitcoin's price drops or when more machines compete for the same reward. Fees made up 0.64% of block rewards over a recent 24-hour period, both Bitcoin.com News and Investing.com reported, which leaves miner income tied almost entirely to the price.

Firsthand said the drop came from price alone. Measured in coins, a petahash earned about 0.000470 BTC a day, 3% more than at the June low.

Thursday's reset

Every 2,016 blocks, or about two weeks, Bitcoin resets its mining difficulty so blocks keep arriving roughly every 10 minutes. When computing power grows, difficulty rises and each machine wins less bitcoin.

The last reset, on Nov. 14, cut difficulty 9.13%, mempool.space data show. Network hashrate fell to about 912 exahashes per second that day, according to Bitcoin.com News and Investing.com, and mempool.space now puts it at about 1,035 EH/s. Its estimate on Sunday called for an increase of about 9% at block 977,760, expected around 8:30 p.m. ET on Thanksgiving Day. The figure can shift as blocks arrive.

Bitcoin traded at $61,180 at 11:52 a.m. ET Sunday, up 1.9% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko.

In our opinion: The number that matters is Thursday's reset. The Nov. 14 cut gave miners room to breathe, and a 9% increase would take most of it back unless revenue recovers first. With fees under 1% of rewards, the bitcoin price is effectively the whole paycheck, so the hashrate trend into Thursday will show how many machines still pencil out.
